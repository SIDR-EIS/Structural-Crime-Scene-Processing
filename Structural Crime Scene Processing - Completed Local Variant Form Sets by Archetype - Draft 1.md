# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Completed Local Variant Form Sets by Archetype

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document adds completed local variant form sets for the main agency archetypes used throughout the project.

The role-specific observation-variant companion explains how local agencies may adapt role-facing observation and review prompts without deleting the method's fixed core.

The local rollout-policy, dashboard, and rollout-packet companions show how those choices are governed institutionally.

This companion shows what a completed local variant set looks like once an agency has actually made those decisions and documented them in deployable form.

Its purpose is to make the local-variant layer concrete enough for rollout, audit, supervision, and calibration.

---

## What This Document Does

This companion provides completed local variant form sets for:

- a large municipal department
- a county sheriff or rural office
- a campus or institutional safety agency
- a regional task-force environment

Each archetype package includes:

- a short archetype profile
- completed one-page local variant summary sheets for the archetype's starter roles
- a completed fixed-core preservation table
- a completed local additions table
- a completed forbidden deletion review table
- a completed local escalation routing strip
- a completed local variant narrative strip
- a short explanation of why the set is locally sound

These are not blank templates.

They are finished deployment examples meant to show how local adaptation should look when it is being done correctly.

## How to Read These Form Sets

Read each example at three levels.

First, read it as a completed local deployment packet.

Second, compare it against the fixed-core template language to see what stayed unchanged across all archetypes.

Third, inspect the local additions to see whether they expose real local pressure or merely accommodate local preference.

The goal is not to make every agency look the same.

The goal is to make every safe local adaptation visibly accountable.

---

## Fixed-Core Rule for Completed Local Variants

Every example in this document obeys the same rule:

- the fixed core stays visible
- local pressure is named rather than implied
- local additions expose real local blind spots
- deletion pressure is documented rather than hidden
- escalation routing points to actual owners

If a local variant cannot explain why it is safe, it is not ready for use.

If it cannot show who will review it, it is not governance.

If it only adds convenience language, it is not a structural variant.

## Example Set A. Large Municipal Department

### 1. Archetype profile

- Agency type: large municipal department with patrol depth, dedicated scene technicians, detective squads, specialist queues, and routine prosecutorial interface
- Typical local pressure points: volume, shift overlap, early event labeling, high-rise shared-space complexity, strong specialty presence masking weak ownership, and review pressure for smooth handoffs
- Starter roles chosen for local variant deployment: patrol, scene technician, detective, supervisory review

### 2. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: patrol

- Role: patrol and first response
- Archetype: large municipal department
- Fixed core:
  - boundary behavior must stay explicit
  - disturbance accounting outranks event shorthand
  - entrant logging remains mandatory in shared spaces
  - patrol may preserve but not settle category
  - transferability must survive shift turnover
- Local pressure points:
  - multi-unit arrival creates false confidence in early control
  - elevators, lobbies, and shared corridors are often under-owned
  - radio shorthand pushes event labels too early
  - resident and witness traffic creates hidden disturbance
- Added local fields:
  - outer shared-space owner named by unit number
  - elevator-bank status at first control
  - building access-system pull requested by time
  - resident-control point named
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - outer boundary ownerless for more than one correction cycle -> precinct watch commander and on-scene supervisor
  - early event-type language driving access control -> patrol sergeant and pilot lead
- Next recalibration date: 2026-05-15

### 3. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: scene technicians

- Role: scene technicians
- Archetype: large municipal department
- Fixed core:
  - technician inherits disturbance, not theory
  - anchor hierarchy must stay explicit
  - graph and collection logic must preserve transferability
  - technician documentation must expose residue rather than smooth it
- Local pressure points:
  - specialists are assumed to "fix" early patrol ambiguity
  - high call volume encourages broad collection without discriminating purpose
  - detective arrival often pressures early interpretive alignment
  - high-rise and multi-unit scenes complicate propagation-path boundaries
- Added local fields:
  - inherited-disturbance warning block on arrival
  - scene graph version number at first detective handoff
  - building-log pull status tied to anchor table
  - queue-sensitive lab priority justification field
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - technician asked to collapse to preferred event frame before graph completion -> scene supervisor
  - broad collection plan without ranked discriminating logic -> unit sergeant and quality lieutenant
- Next recalibration date: 2026-05-15

### 4. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: detective

- Role: detectives
- Archetype: large municipal department
- Fixed core:
  - detective must manage bounded hypotheses rather than inherit narrative
  - witness and resident statements remain category pressure until anchored
  - reconstruction must stay subordinate to sequence evidence
  - package ownership includes residue visibility
- Local pressure points:
  - serious-case response culture rewards fast interpretive fluency
  - prosecutor contact begins early in sensitive cases
  - multiple specialist voices can create false closure
  - supervisor briefings may reward polished early summaries
- Added local fields:
  - event-category pressure sources named separately from anchors
  - unresolved access-history owner and due time
  - first prosecutor consult point recorded
  - oral-rescue dependency check at first review
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - detective summary exceeds current anchors in first review -> case supervisor
  - unresolved access-history omitted from package cover sheet -> investigative lieutenant
- Next recalibration date: 2026-05-15

### 5. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: supervisory review

- Role: supervisors and review boards
- Archetype: large municipal department
- Fixed core:
  - supervision asks structural questions, not morale questions
  - closure and deferral remain observable conditions
  - review must detect hidden rescue and false stability
  - status language must match residue honestly
- Local pressure points:
  - high volume encourages "good enough" packaging
  - specialty presence can be mistaken for structural completeness
  - command briefings can reward persuasive summary over residue visibility
  - shift change hides who actually corrected drift
- Added local fields:
  - shift-boundary ownership check
  - hidden-expert dependency check
  - specialist-presence versus actual transferability prompt
  - command-brief overclaim risk note
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - package marked ready while ownership gaps remain unnamed -> bureau commander and pilot director
  - review board treating specialist presence as sufficiency -> quality review board chair
- Next recalibration date: 2026-05-15

- next local review date: 2026-05-15

### 10. Completed local variant narrative strip

- `What this agency variation protects:` high-volume specialist environments from confusing staffing density with structural clarity
- `What this agency is most at risk of hiding:` ownerless shared spaces, early narrative rescue, and specialist-driven false stability

### 11. Why this set is locally sound

This set adds fields only where the large-municipal environment creates repeat failure modes.

It does not relax the method because capacity exists.

It uses local adaptation to expose exactly where large departments tend to hide weakness under smooth staffing, smooth speech, and specialist presence.

## Example Set B. County Sheriff or Rural Office

### 12. Archetype profile

- Agency type: county sheriff or rural office with wide geography, frequent role compression, delayed technician access, and remote supervision
- Typical local pressure points: single-deputy early control, distance to scene, overnight scarcity, delayed lab and legal input, and dependence on a few generalists
- Starter roles chosen for local variant deployment: patrol or deputy first response, generalist investigator, supervisory review

### 13. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: patrol or deputy first response

- Role: patrol or deputy first response
- Archetype: county sheriff or rural office
- Fixed core:
  - early disturbance accounting remains mandatory
  - boundary behavior stays explicit even when staffing is thin
  - deferral must be named, not implied
  - family or neighbor routine talk cannot become category proof
  - transfer notes must preserve what changed during delay
- Local pressure points:
  - one deputy may hold scene and people simultaneously
  - large properties make outer-perimeter decisions easy to under-scope
  - local familiarity creates false confidence in "routine" scenes
  - daylight delay encourages silent deferral
- Added local fields:
  - outer property zone sweep completed or deferred
  - remote-supervisor contact time
  - deferral trigger table started by first responder
  - weather and travel-impact note tied to disturbance preservation
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - unowned deferred work after first backup arrival -> shift sergeant
  - neighbor or family routine narrative replacing access accounting -> investigator and sergeant
- Next recalibration date: 2026-05-22

### 14. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: generalist investigator

- Role: generalist investigator or detective
- Archetype: county sheriff or rural office
- Fixed core:
  - bounded hypotheses remain mandatory under scarcity
  - reconstruction must wait on owned sequence conditions
  - nulls and delays remain visible in the package
  - transferability matters even when the same person carries several functions
- Local pressure points:
  - role compression makes oral memory feel sufficient
  - delayed specialist arrival encourages overreach by the generalist
  - longstanding community knowledge distorts category control
  - limited overnight support increases temptation to convert deferral into closure
- Added local fields:
  - role-compression warning check
  - specialist consult request time and scope
  - daylight reentry conditions table
  - local-familiarity bias note where witnesses are known to responders
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - single investigator effectively certifying own unresolveds -> supervisory review
  - delayed specialist consult used to justify narrative closure -> sheriff or investigations lieutenant
- Next recalibration date: 2026-05-22

### 15. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: supervisory review

- Role: supervisory review
- Archetype: county sheriff or rural office
- Fixed core:
  - supervision must separate scarcity from excuse
  - deferral must be owned with dates or triggers
  - unresolveds remain visible into downstream transfer
  - review must detect hidden dependency on one trusted generalist
- Local pressure points:
  - small agencies often rely on a few respected veterans
  - remote review can flatten scene complexity
  - limited local legal access creates threshold ambiguity
  - practical pressure rewards "good enough until daylight"
- Added local fields:
  - distance-versus-excuse check
  - named daylight reentry gate
  - hidden-expert dependency prompt
  - regional-specialist interface owner field
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - supervisor approves package with unnamed daylight reentry -> command staff
  - same generalist repeatedly carries scenes without transfer-ready documentation -> sheriff and implementation lead
- Next recalibration date: 2026-05-22

## Example Set C. Campus or Institutional Safety Agency

### 22. Archetype profile

- Agency type: campus or institutional safety agency with strong property and access-control systems, uneven criminal-investigation depth, and significant administrative review pressure
- Typical local pressure points: administrative urgency, reputational sensitivity, hybrid policy and criminal-review pathways, and overreliance on access-control data as if it were self-explanatory
- Starter roles chosen for local variant deployment: patrol or safety response, supervisory review, administrative or legal review

### 23. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: patrol or safety response

- Role: patrol or institutional safety response
- Archetype: campus or institutional safety agency
- Fixed core:
  - boundary behavior still applies even in semi-public spaces
  - access-control data supports anchors but does not replace scene observation
  - disturbance accounting remains primary
  - witness comfort and community messaging do not control category
- Local pressure points:
  - residence halls, offices, and mixed-use buildings create blurred public/private assumptions
  - administrative leaders often want fast reassurance language
  - digital-access records are often mistaken for full sequence proof
  - non-sworn or mixed-authority staff may hesitate to preserve residue aggressively
- Added local fields:
  - access-system preservation request time
  - building-manager or facilities-control point named
  - administrative notification point separated from investigative transfer
  - semi-public-space boundary note
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - reassurance language outrunning disturbance accounting -> shift supervisor and pilot lead
  - access-system narrative treated as complete event sequence -> investigative liaison
- Next recalibration date: 2026-05-29

### 24. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: supervisory review

- Local pressure points:
  - campus leadership seeks stability language quickly
  - mixed administrative and safety meetings blur review purpose
  - nontraditional staffing lines complicate ownership
  - facilities and IT data can dominate physical-scene interpretation
- Added local fields:
  - criminal versus administrative track status
  - reassurance-language risk prompt
  - facilities and IT evidence ownership note
  - mixed-authority handoff owner
- Red-trigger escalation path:

## Example Set D. Regional Task-Force Environment

### 32. Archetype profile

- Agency type: regional task-force environment with mixed agencies, rotating personnel, cross-jurisdictional review, and specialist concentration unevenly distributed across partners
- Typical local pressure points: shared authority, uneven documentation habits, ambiguous ownership at handoff points, and package drift across agencies using different legacy language
- Starter roles chosen for local variant deployment: task-force intake or operations lead, scene-tech liaison, supervisory or review-board function, downstream prosecutor or legal coordinator

### 33. Completed one-page local variant summary sheet: task-force intake or operations lead

- Role: task-force intake or operations lead
- Archetype: regional task-force environment
- Fixed core:
  - one support standard must govern across agencies
  - cross-agency residue must remain visible rather than averaged out
  - intake must record ownership transitions explicitly
  - no agency's legacy shorthand may substitute for the shared grammar
- Local pressure points:
  - multiple agencies bring different report habits
  - early prestige dynamics can silence weaker partners
  - operating tempo encourages oral brief dominance
  - partners may assume someone else owns unresolveds
- Added local fields:
  - originating agency and inherited package condition
  - cross-agency terminology check
  - ownership-transition ledger start point
  - partner-capability mismatch note
- Red-trigger escalation path:
  - unresolved ownership drifting between agencies -> task-force commander
  - one agency's shorthand displacing shared grammar -> implementation lead
- Next recalibration date: 2026-06-05

## Cross-Archetype Reading Notes

Every archetype preserved the same fixed core.

What changed across the examples was not the truth standard.

What changed was where each environment is most likely to hide weakness.

Large municipal variants focus on ownerless complexity and specialist-driven false stability.

Rural variants focus on scarcity, delay, and role compression.

Campus variants focus on mixed authority, reputational pressure, and digital-system overconfidence.

Task-force variants focus on interagency translation, ownership seams, and political rescue.

Those are legitimate local differences.

They do not justify changing the method's core.

## Common Misreadings

### Misreading 1: A local variant is just a shorter local quick-reference sheet

It is not.

A local variant is a governed explanation of why certain local prompts are needed and why the fixed core remains intact.

### Misreading 2: The local additions table is where agencies put whatever they want

It is not a wish list.

Every added field must expose a real local blind spot and must have a named review owner.

### Misreading 3: The forbidden-deletion table is only for formal audits

It should be used during drafting.

If deletion pressure is not documented early, it will usually reappear later as drift.

### Misreading 4: One archetype can simply borrow another archetype's set unchanged

Agencies may borrow structure.

They should not borrow local pressure assumptions without review.

### Misreading 5: Once the form set exists, local review is finished

Completed variants are starting points, not permanent approvals.

They still require review cadence, observation, audit, and recalibration.

# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Agency-Specific Exemplar Rollout Packets

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document shows what a completed local rollout packet can look like when the Structural Crime Scene Processing project is adapted to a real agency environment.

It is written for:

- command sponsors
- implementation owners
- training leads
- audit leads
- policy drafters
- local rollout teams
- agencies deciding how to assemble the project's materials into one deployable package

Its job is to answer a specific question:

what does a finished local rollout packet actually look like when policy, observation variants, dashboards, audit logic, signoff logic, and corrective action are assembled for a specific agency type.

---

## What This Document Is

This document is:

- an exemplar companion
- a packaging guide by example
- a bridge from templates to near-ready deployment kits
- a demonstration of how the project stack can behave as one local system

It is not:

- a final signed policy for any real agency
- a universal master packet that every agency should copy directly
- a substitute for legal, labor, or command review

These are exemplars.

They are meant to show structure, sequence, and integration.

---

## Why Exemplar Packets Are Needed

By this stage, the project contains:

- doctrine
- role guides
- forms
- exercises
- scoring
- signoff
- calibration
- observation
- audit
- dashboard templates
- dashboard population guidance
- local policy templates

That is a strong stack.

It still leaves one practical problem.

Local teams may understand each piece and still not know how the pieces should sit together in one actual rollout packet.

Exemplar packets solve that problem.

They show:

- what gets included
- what gets emphasized for a given agency type
- what a first reporting cycle looks like
- what a first audit result might look like
- what corrective action looks like when it is written cleanly

Without this layer, many agencies will keep asking:

- what does a real one look like

This document answers that question.

---

## How to Read These Packets

Each packet in this document includes:

- agency profile
- rollout scope
- local policy package summary
- role-variant set
- dashboard summary
- audit snapshot
- training and signoff snapshot where relevant
- immediate corrective-action ledger
- why the packet is structurally sound

These packets are intentionally bounded.

They do not attempt to reproduce every supporting document in full.

They show how the system should be assembled and what early signals should look like.

---

## Packet Architecture

Every exemplar packet in this document follows the same structure:

1. local environment summary
2. fixed-core commitments preserved
3. adopted policy stack
4. local role-variant stack
5. dashboard snapshot
6. audit snapshot
7. training and signoff snapshot
8. corrective-action ledger
9. deployment reading notes

This repeated structure is deliberate.

If an agency cannot produce these nine things, its local package is not yet integrated.

## Exemplar Packet A. Large Municipal Department

### 1. Agency profile

**Agency type:** large municipal department  
**Archetype:** Large municipal department  
**Pilot environment:** one patrol district, one scene unit, one detective squad, one downstream legal review team  
**Call environment:** moderate to high volume  
**Specialist availability:** in-house scene unit, in-house review structure, local prosecutor coordination  

### 2. Local rollout objective

The objective of this packet is to show how a large department can launch with enough structure to preserve transfer integrity across many handoffs without allowing local subunits to drift into multiple competing versions of the method.

### 3. Fixed-core commitments preserved

This packet preserves:

- observable closure criteria
- named deferral ownership
- handoff transferability
- structural supervisory questions
- downstream boundedness
- visible drift reporting

### 4. Local policy package summary

**Adoption directive**

- pilot scope applies to District 2 patrol, central scene unit, violent-crimes squad, and charging-review prosecutors assigned to the pilot
- executive sponsor: deputy chief of investigations
- implementation owner: captain, reform and quality office

**Pilot operating order**

- pilot duration: 90 days
- scenes in scope: homicide, suspicious death, serious assault with unresolved scene geometry, high-friction officer-involved entry scenes
- weekly pilot review and monthly command review required

**Role and supervision policy**

- patrol sergeants use structural first-question set
- investigative lieutenants review closure contracts and deferral ownership before case-transfer approval
- review-board members must review packages, not only live briefings

**Observation and local variant policy**

- local patrol variant approved for multi-unit arrival scenes
- local scene-tech variant approved for full-time CSI teams
- local detective and supervisory variants approved

**Audit and reporting policy**

- first-wave live-use audit at day 21
- transfer-integrity audit at day 45
- drift audit at day 90
- command dashboard every two weeks

### 5. Local role-variant stack

Approved local variants:

- large municipal patrol variant
- full-time CSI unit technician variant
- dedicated detective-bureau variant
- patrol-sergeant and investigative-lieutenant supervisory variants
- in-house laboratory variant
- embedded early-review prosecutor variant

Key local pressure points:

- too many early meaning carriers
- strong appearance of order masking interpretive leakage
- multiple handoffs causing oral rescue
- subunit-level terminology drift

### 6. Dashboard snapshot

**Reporting window:** pilot weeks 1-2  
**Coverage confidence:** High  

**Command dashboard summary**

- Adoption Integrity: `Amber`
- Transfer Integrity: `Amber`
- Training and Readiness: `Green`
- Specialist Alignment: `Amber`
- Drift and Risk: `Amber`
- Corrective Action: `Green`

**Top reported indicators**

- explicit closure rate: 7 / 10 sampled scenes
- named deferral ownership rate: 15 / 19 unresolved items
- transfer-ready package rate: 4 / 8 sampled packages
- oral rescue dependency rate: 3 / 8 sampled packages
- structural question discipline rate: 6 / 8 observed supervisory interactions
- downstream boundedness rate: 5 / 6 reviewed legal outputs

**Narrative strip**

- `Strengthened:` scene technicians are visibly ranking anchors and documenting transfer-relevant distinctions.
- `Drifted:` district patrol scenes with many early arrivals still show interpretive leakage before the boundary stabilizes.
- `Next action:` retrain District 2 patrol supervisors on informational-boundary control and multi-unit first-language discipline.

### 7. Audit snapshot

**Audit packet used:** Packet B. First-Wave Live-Use Audit  
**Disposition:** Proceed with Conditions  

**Top fault lines**

1. Early multi-unit arrival scenes still generate uncontrolled explanatory language before structural boundary control is established.
2. Detective-to-prosecutor transfer remains partially dependent on oral summary in complex scenes.
3. One investigative lieutenant still rewards clean briefing language more than explicit residue.

**Why the disposition is not full proceed**

- the pilot is real
- structural use is visible
- transfer remains too person-dependent to call secure

### 8. Training and signoff snapshot

**Training dashboard summary**

- clear passes: 18
- passes with conditions: 6
- remediation assignments: 4
- not-yet-ready: 1

**Downstream legal signoff summary**

- charging review scope: 3 clear pass, 1 pass with conditions
- warrant review scope: 2 clear pass, 2 pass with conditions
- demonstrative audit scope: 1 remediation and re-run

### 9. Immediate corrective-action ledger

| Fault line | Layer | Corrective action | Owner | Due date | Evidence for closure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interpretive leakage during multi-unit arrival | Supervision / patrol | Run multi-unit first-language drill with patrol sergeants and shift teams | District 2 patrol captain | Day 30 | 3 observed scenes with improved informational-boundary control |
| Detective-to-prosecutor oral rescue dependency | Role interface | Require transfer-readiness review note on all sampled pilot packages | Violent-crimes lieutenant | Day 35 | 5 package reviews showing independent readability |
| Residue minimized in one supervisory lane | Supervision | Review and coach lieutenant on closure-contract and residue-governance questions | Investigations major | Day 28 | 2 reviewed meetings showing improved structural question use |

### 10. Why this packet is structurally sound

This packet works because:

- the policy layer is explicit
- the role variants are narrow and justified
- the dashboard surface does not hide drift
- the audit disposition is real rather than flattering
- corrective actions are owned and time-bounded

This is what a large agency packet should do.

It should not merely sound sophisticated.

It should make handoff fragility and supervisory distortion visible quickly enough to correct them.

## Exemplar Packet B. County Sheriff or Rural Office

### 11. Agency profile

**Agency type:** county sheriff / rural office  
**Archetype:** County sheriff or rural pattern  
**Pilot environment:** patrol-first responders, generalist investigators, regional scene support, external state lab, county attorney liaison  
**Geographic constraint:** long travel times, delayed specialist arrival  
**Staffing model:** role compression common  

### 12. Local rollout objective

The objective of this packet is to show how a lower-resource, longer-distance environment can adopt the method honestly without pretending that every scene will have urban staffing depth.

### 13. Fixed-core commitments preserved

This packet preserves:

- explicit marking of low-visibility conditions
- disturbance accounting over long windows
- owned unresolveds
- role-compression marking
- transferability despite low staffing
- gray conditions when downstream visibility is low

### 14. Local policy package summary

**Adoption directive**

- pilot scope applies to Patrol Shift B, generalist investigations, sheriff supervisory review, and county attorney pre-charge consultation on designated major scenes
- sheriff is command sponsor
- implementation owner is the lieutenant over patrol and investigations

**Pilot operating order**

- pilot duration: 120 days
- scenes in scope: suspicious death, major assault, fire scenes with unclear initiation path, isolated rural scenes with delayed specialist arrival
- observation may be partial when staffing or safety prevents full coverage, but missing visibility must be marked explicitly

**Role and supervision policy**

- same responder may carry multiple functions, but role shift must be marked
- supervisory review must distinguish forced deviation from drift

**Observation and local variant policy**

- rural patrol variant
- generalist detective variant
- regional on-call technician variant
- state-lab liaison variant
- later-stage prosecutor intake variant

**Audit and reporting policy**

- command dashboard monthly
- first-wave audit at day 30
- transfer-integrity audit at day 60
- drift audit at day 120

### 15. Local role-variant stack

Approved local variants:

- county sheriff or rural deputy patrol variant
- generalist detective variant
- regional on-call technician variant
- patrol-sergeant / small-office supervisory variant
- state or regional lab-liaison variant
- later-stage file-intake prosecutor variant

Key local pressure points:

- one deputy holding the scene alone
- long evidence-degradation windows
- same person performing patrol and follow-up functions
- thin observation coverage

### 16. Dashboard snapshot

**Reporting window:** pilot month 1  
**Coverage confidence:** Medium  

**Command dashboard summary**

- Adoption Integrity: `Amber`
- Transfer Integrity: `Amber`
- Training and Readiness: `Amber`
- Specialist Alignment: `Gray`
- Drift and Risk: `Amber`
- Corrective Action: `Amber`

**Top reported indicators**

- explicit closure rate: 4 / 7 sampled scenes
- named deferral ownership rate: 9 / 11 unresolved items
- transfer-ready package rate: 3 / 6 sampled packages
- oral rescue dependency rate: 2 / 6 sampled packages
- structural question discipline rate: 4 / 5 supervisory reviews
- downstream boundedness rate: Gray, sample too thin

**Narrative strip**

- `Strengthened:` deputies are marking disturbance and low-visibility conditions more honestly than before.
- `Drifted:` scenes with delayed specialist arrival still tempt early practical closure when the event feels stable.
- `Next action:` require role-shift marking and late-arrival humility review on all rural suspicious-death scenes for the next cycle.

### 17. Audit snapshot

**Audit packet used:** Packet C. Handoff and Transfer Integrity Audit  
**Disposition:** Proceed with Conditions  

**Top fault lines**

1. Patrol-to-investigator transfer is improving, but same-person role compression still blurs where observation ends and interpretation begins.
2. Regional technician arrival delay is not always being marked as a structural limitation in later summaries.
3. External-lab visibility remains gray because liaison documentation is inconsistent.

### 18. Training and signoff snapshot

**Training dashboard summary**

- clear passes: 7
- passes with conditions: 5
- remediation assignments: 3
- not-yet-ready: 0

**Signoff posture**

- no full downstream legal certification yet
- county attorney review remains advisory and supervised

### 19. Immediate corrective-action ledger

| Fault line | Layer | Corrective action | Owner | Due date | Evidence for closure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Role compression blurring patrol and follow-up logic | Training / supervision | Add role-shift strip to all pilot suspicious-death forms and review in sergeant briefings | Implementation lieutenant | Day 40 | 5 cases with explicit role-shift marking |
| Technician delay not carried into summaries | Document design | Add late-arrival humility note to regional technician packet summary | Regional scene support lead | Day 45 | 3 packets showing preserved delay effects |
| External-lab visibility inconsistent | Role interface | Require liaison note for every pilot submission to state lab | County attorney liaison / investigations sergeant | Day 50 | 4 submissions with structured liaison notes |

### 20. Why this packet is structurally sound

This packet works because it does not lie about low-resource conditions.

It uses:

- amber and gray honestly
- role-compression marking
- delay-aware interpretation
- narrower but still real dashboard coverage

That is what a rural packet should do.

It should not imitate a large-city staffing model.

It should preserve the method honestly under constraint.

## Exemplar Packet C. Campus or Institutional Safety Agency

### 21. Agency profile

**Agency type:** campus public safety / institutional safety  
**Archetype:** Campus or institutional pattern  
**Pilot environment:** campus patrol, housing liaison interface, facilities and access-control liaison, external scene support, university counsel and prosecutor liaison  
**Primary risk environment:** heavy community familiarity and strong administrative smoothing pressure  

### 22. Local rollout objective

The objective of this packet is to show how a campus-like environment can preserve structural honesty where community knowledge and administrative culture would otherwise push the agency toward early normalization.

### 23. Fixed-core commitments preserved

This packet preserves:

- interpretive-boundary protection
- access-system anchors without overreading them
- distinction between community knowledge and lawful evidence
- downstream boundedness
- explicit administrative-pressure tracking

### 24. Local policy package summary

**Adoption directive**

- pilot scope applies to campus patrol, on-call command staff, housing liaison channels, and local prosecutor review on serious incidents
- vice president for public safety is executive sponsor
- implementation owner is director of campus investigations

**Pilot operating order**

- pilot duration: 75 days
- scenes in scope: suspicious student or staff death, serious in-building assaults, access-control-intensive incidents, cases with strong rumor pressure

**Role and supervision policy**

- community familiarity may inform scene-management logistics but shall not function as substitute anchor support
- supervisors must control interpretive leakage through housing, facilities, and campus administration channels

**Observation and local variant policy**

- campus patrol variant
- supervisory variant
- later-stage legal-review variant
- technician or regional support variant depending on scene complexity

**Dashboard and reporting policy**

- command dashboard monthly
- rumor-control and interpretive-boundary flags carried as visible local indicators

### 25. Local role-variant stack

Approved local variants:

- campus or institutional safety patrol variant
- supervisory variant with administrative-pressure note
- later-stage file-intake legal variant
- regional technician support variant

Key local pressure points:

- people think they already know the actors
- access logs feel more definitive than they are
- internal messaging channels spread explanation quickly
- local leadership may prefer a normalized story early

### 26. Dashboard snapshot

**Reporting window:** pilot weeks 1-3  
**Coverage confidence:** High  

**Command dashboard summary**

- Adoption Integrity: `Amber`
- Transfer Integrity: `Green`
- Training and Readiness: `Amber`
- Specialist Alignment: `Amber`
- Drift and Risk: `Red`
- Corrective Action: `Amber`

**Top reported indicators**

- explicit closure rate: 5 / 6 sampled scenes
- named deferral ownership rate: 11 / 13 unresolved items
- transfer-ready package rate: 5 / 6 sampled packages
- interpretive-boundary failure flags: 4 events in window
- structural question discipline rate: 5 / 6 supervisory reviews
- downstream boundedness rate: 3 / 4 legal reviews

**Narrative strip**

- `Strengthened:` campus patrol and supervisors are documenting access and facilities information without automatically treating it as causal truth.
- `Drifted:` rumor and administrative smoothing pressure remain the strongest live threat in serious incidents.
- `Next action:` formalize housing and facilities communication limits inside pilot scope and retrain command staff on interpretive-boundary discipline.

### 27. Audit snapshot

**Audit packet used:** Packet E. Laboratory and Downstream Legal Alignment Audit plus local interpretive-boundary review  
**Disposition:** Hold and Remediate  

**Top fault lines**

1. Internal administrative channels are still circulating scene meaning before the structural package stabilizes.
2. One local legal-review lane accepted an access-log-heavy narrative that outran the package.
3. Campus command staff still sometimes ask for a normalized account before residue has been bounded.

### 28. Training and signoff snapshot

**Training dashboard summary**

- clear passes: 9
- passes with conditions: 4
- remediation assignments: 2

**Downstream legal signoff summary**

- one warrant-review pass with conditions
- one charging-review remediation

### 29. Immediate corrective-action ledger

| Fault line | Layer | Corrective action | Owner | Due date | Evidence for closure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Internal message channels spreading scene meaning | Governance / supervision | Issue interim communication rule for housing and facilities leadership during in-scope incidents | VP public safety | Day 14 | No repeat interpretive-leakage event in next 3 sampled incidents |
| Access-log overreading in legal review | Downstream legal | Run bounded-access-log review drill with legal and investigative leads | Director of campus investigations | Day 21 | 2 reviewed outputs showing corrected threshold language |
| Command normalization pressure | Supervision | Add red-flag prompt to command review sheet: "What is still live and unowned?" | Chief of public safety | Day 10 | 3 command reviews showing changed question pattern |

### 30. Why this packet is structurally sound

This packet is structurally sound because it refuses to treat community familiarity as investigative clarity.

It:

- carries interpretive-boundary failures as real red items
- keeps access-system information bounded
- uses a hold-and-remediate disposition when administrative culture is still too strong

That is what a campus packet should do.

It should not hide the exact pressures that make this environment distinctive.

## Exemplar Packet D. Regional Task-Force Environment

### 31. Agency profile

**Agency type:** regional task force / multi-agency major-case environment  
**Archetype:** Regional task-force pattern  
**Pilot environment:** two municipal departments, one sheriff office, regional crime-scene unit, state lab liaison, joint prosecutorial review  
**Primary risk environment:** cross-agency terminology drift and hidden translation work  

### 32. Local rollout objective

The objective of this packet is to show how the method can be deployed across agencies without letting one experienced coordinator become the hidden infrastructure that makes the packet look stable.

### 33. Fixed-core commitments preserved

This packet preserves:

- one shared residue ledger
- one shared metric dictionary
- one shared audit posture
- cross-agency interface visibility
- documented translation rules

### 34. Local policy package summary

**Adoption directive**

- signed by participating agency heads for designated major-case pilot incidents
- regional implementation board named

**Pilot operating order**

- pilot runs on designated cross-agency major cases for 6 months
- one common observation and dashboard language required

**Observation and variant policy**

- cross-role bundle used as the starting form
- agency attachments permitted only where the fixed core remains intact

**Audit and reporting policy**

- one shared metric dictionary
- one shared drift review
- task-force command dashboard monthly

### 35. Dashboard snapshot

**Reporting window:** first 45 days  
**Coverage confidence:** Medium  

**Command dashboard summary**

- Adoption Integrity: `Amber`
- Transfer Integrity: `Red`
- Training and Readiness: `Amber`
- Specialist Alignment: `Amber`
- Drift and Risk: `Red`
- Corrective Action: `Amber`

**Top reported indicators**

- transfer-ready package rate: 2 / 6 sampled cross-agency packages
- oral rescue dependency rate: 4 / 6 sampled packages
- shared terminology compliance rate: 3 / 6 sampled case reviews
- repeat interface fault-line count: 3

**Narrative strip**

- `Strengthened:` agencies are using one residue ledger and one audit format.
- `Drifted:` cross-agency oral rescue remains too high and one senior coordinator is still functioning as the live translator.
- `Next action:` enforce shared package-readability review and prohibit final task-force transfer without translation-independent package check.

### 36. Audit snapshot

**Audit packet used:** Packet C. Handoff and Transfer Integrity Audit  
**Disposition:** Hold and Remediate  

**Top fault lines**

1. Shared terminology is not yet stable across agencies.
2. Oral rescue by one coordinator is masking weak package standardization.
3. Cross-agency legal and lab references are not yet consistently bounded.

### 37. Corrective-action ledger

| Fault line | Layer | Corrective action | Owner | Due date | Evidence for closure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Terminology drift | Governance / document design | Issue shared metric and package dictionary, then run joint review drill | Regional implementation board chair | Day 20 | 4 sampled packages using common language |
| Hidden translator dependence | Role interface | Require blind downstream-readability check without coordinator present | Task-force audit lead | Day 25 | 3 package checks completed without oral rescue |
| Boundedness inconsistency in specialist notes | Specialist alignment | Standardize lab and legal summary strip fields for pilot cases | Joint specialist leads | Day 30 | 3 specialist outputs using shared bounded format |

### 38. Why this packet is structurally sound

This packet is structurally sound because it does not confuse cross-agency enthusiasm with cross-agency standardization.

It keeps the pressure on:

- shared language
- transfer without translators
- real package readability

That is what a task-force packet should do.

## What These Packets Show Across Archetypes

Across all four packets, the main lesson is the same:

- a strong rollout packet makes local pressure visible
- it does not flatten agency differences
- it does not let those differences delete the fixed core

The packet should always show:

- what this agency is at risk of hiding
- what the first reporting cycle actually revealed
- what action is required next

If those three things are missing, the packet is still too abstract.

---

## Packet Assembly Guide

When building a real local rollout packet from these exemplars, assemble:

1. local policy package summary
2. approved role-variant list
3. dashboard summary for the first reporting cycle
4. first audit disposition
5. signoff and remediation summary if training or legal review is in scope
6. corrective-action ledger
7. next review date

This is the minimum packet.

Anything less is usually still a planning memo.

## Common Exemplar Misreadings

### Misreading 1: The example numbers are the standard

They are not.

They are examples of how a structurally honest packet looks.

### Misreading 2: A green-heavy packet is always better

Not necessarily.

An honest amber or red packet is often stronger than a falsely reassuring green packet.

### Misreading 3: The local packet should look identical across agencies

It should not.

The structure should repeat.

The pressure points should differ.

### Misreading 4: The packet is only for leadership

It is also a working coordination tool for implementation owners, training leads, audit leads, and supervisors.

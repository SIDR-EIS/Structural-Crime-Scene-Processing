# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Role-Specific Completed Field-Form Examples

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document adds production-style completed examples for the shared field forms as used by specific roles.

The existing field-form packet provides blank forms.

The role-specific variants companion explains how each role should use those forms.

The earlier exemplar companion shows strong first-pass completion for selected scenario sets.

This document answers a different question.

It shows what the forms look like when they are actually completed from inside a role perspective rather than from a general project perspective.

Its purpose is to help instructors, trainees, supervisors, and implementation leads see:

- what patrol completion looks like when patrol stays in patrol's lane
- what scene-technician completion looks like when graph and collection logic stay structural
- what detective completion looks like when hypothesis and deferral discipline stay bounded
- what laboratory completion looks like when intake, limit-honesty, and reprocessing logic are made visible
- what supervisory completion looks like when review replaces comfort rather than reinforcing it
- what prosecutor completion looks like when downstream reading stays lawful rather than rhetorical

## How to Use These Examples

Use these examples in four ways:

1. as an instructor model before a drill begins
2. as a debrief comparison after a learner completes a role-specific exercise
3. as a calibration artifact for judging whether a role stayed inside its lawful scope
4. as an implementation reference when building local training packets, role books, or quick-use libraries

Do not copy these examples mechanically into live work.

They are examples of structure, bounded language, and ownership discipline.

They are not canned answers for unrelated cases.

---

## Conventions Used in These Examples

The wording is intentionally concise.

Each example tries to show the actual burden of the role rather than a polished literary version of the form.

When uncertainty remains, it is named directly.

When deferral exists, it is assigned directly.

When a role should not make a claim, the example avoids making it.

That restraint is part of the model.

## Example Set A. Patrol and First Response

### Source scenario

- Apartment Hallway Collapse

### Role focus

This set models patrol completing the earliest structural record without slipping into technician, detective, or prosecutor language.

### Example A1. Form 1. Initial Scene Boundary and Stabilization Sheet

#### Immediate priorities

- Immediate life threat: no active assailant observed on arrival; injured subject unresponsive; EMS access opened.
- Immediate medical action: hallway entry made to subject location; one resident moved aside to clear EMS path.
- Immediate safety risk: stairwell traffic uncontrolled for approximately two minutes before outer boundary extended.
- Immediate communication task: dispatch advised of likely death scene, requesting supervisor and scene technician response.

#### Boundary status

- Boundary on arrival: no formal boundary; hallway shared by residents; apartment doors open on east side.
- Boundary created by patrol: inner boundary at subject end of hallway; outer boundary at stairwell landing and elevator exit.
- Boundary uncertainty: unclear whether two residents crossed near subject before first officer reached body location.
- Boundary correction needed by next role: hallway camera coverage, if any, and full resident-movement log need reconstruction.

#### Access and control

- Known entry before control: two residents, one building porter, EMS team, first two officers.
- Access points active on arrival: stairwell door propped, elevator active, one apartment door standing open.
- Access points later controlled: stairwell secured by third officer; elevator traffic redirected after supervisor arrival.
- Access still uncertain: whether any resident briefly entered from west unit before hallway lockout.

#### Earliest perishable anchors to capture

- Subject position first seen: prone near west wall, head toward stairwell, right arm under torso.
- Lighting state: hallway overhead lights active on arrival.
- Sound state: no alarm sounding; TV audible from open east unit.
- Environmental state: floor dry except localized liquid near subject head area; source unknown at arrival.
- Time anchors: dispatch time, first officer arrival, EMS entry, witness separation start.

#### Early contamination vectors

- EMS path crossed within two feet of subject before cones or tape were placed.
- One resident verbally identified self as caller while standing inside inner hallway space before separation.
- Officer foot traffic near stairwell-side floor marks before full scene freeze.
- Apartment doors remained partly open during first minute, creating line-of-sight contamination risk.

#### Likely explanation-density carriers already active

- Resident statements about a "fall" began before separation.
- EMS asked whether subject had jumped or stumbled, creating early category pressure.
- Building porter described hallway as normally empty, which may later be overused as certainty.

#### Immediate next transition

- Patrol phase can transfer once resident locations, EMS actions, and uncertain access points are logged and handed forward by name.
- Scene technician should audit hallway disturbance load before accepting focal point as original event center.

### Example A2. Form 2. Scene Phase Closure Contract

#### Current closure status

- Patrol phase not analytically complete.
- Patrol phase conditionally transferable once early disturbance, witness separation, and access uncertainty are recorded and acknowledged.

#### Resolved questions

- Immediate threat not active at time patrol established control.
- EMS necessity separated from investigative convenience after first-minute access.
- Known responder actions during first minutes identified at a coarse level.

#### Unresolved questions

- Exact pre-control resident movement near subject location.
- Whether west-unit occupant entered hallway before officer arrival.
- Whether visible liquid near subject existed before EMS path activity.
- Whether early "fall" framing came from caller observation or hallway rumor spread.

#### Live inhibitors

- Resident traffic log incomplete.
- Full body-worn camera synchronization not yet reviewed.
- Hallway access-control history not yet requested from building management.

#### Owned deferrals

- Resident movement reconstruction: detective owner after technician boundary audit.
- Building access or camera review: detective owner with supervisor tracking.
- Scene disturbance audit: scene technician owner on intake.

#### Release / transfer conditions

- Transfer may occur only after patrol logs the names of all known early entrants and states what remains uncertain.
- Patrol may not characterize event type in transfer statement.

### Example A3. Form 10. Deferral Ownership Table

| Deferred item | Why deferred | Current owner | Trigger / due condition | Consequence if ignored |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full resident movement reconstruction | Patrol lacked lawful time and visibility during first control phase | Detective | Begin immediately after witness separation review | Early boundary assumptions may harden into false sequence |
| Access-control and hallway camera confirmation | Patrol had no direct system access on scene | Detective with supervisor visibility | Request within same operational period | Unknown entrant problem remains hidden |
| EMS intervention map tied to body-worn camera timing | Patrol documented at coarse level only | Scene technician | Complete before technician anchor hierarchy is finalized | Responder-made disturbance may be mistaken for scene-native state |
| Caller-origin language audit around "fall" description | Patrol priority was control and separation, not statement parsing | Detective | Review first statements before hypothesis ranking | Early narrative contamination may be treated as anchor |

#### Unowned deferral check

- No deferral should remain ownerless at patrol transfer.
- If detective assignment cannot be confirmed, supervisor must take temporary ownership rather than leaving the item in general awareness.

### Example A4. Form 12. Integrated Scene Package Cover Sheet

#### Section checklist

- Patrol first-contact summary: complete
- Early disturbance summary: complete
- Known entrants list: partial but named as incomplete
- Transfer restrictions: complete
- Cross-role warning note: complete

#### Current package summary

- Patrol established a usable but not clean hallway boundary after an uncontrolled first two minutes.
- Emergency access and resident proximity created meaningful disturbance load.
- Early category pressure around a possible fall is present and should not be treated as anchored event type.
- Most important unresolved issue at transfer is pre-control hallway movement.

#### Transfer statement

- This package is safe for technician intake only if the incoming role treats the early hallway state as partially disturbed and partially unverified.
- Patrol is transferring a boundary and disturbance record, not an event explanation.

## Example Set B. Scene Technicians

### Source scenario

- Office Quiet Death

### Role focus

This set models the technician's structural burden: inherited-scene audit, anchor ranking, scene graphing, and collection by discriminating value rather than by visual drama.

### Example B1. Form 1. Initial Scene Boundary and Stabilization Sheet

#### Immediate priorities

- Confirm inherited office boundary against patrol assumptions.
- Freeze workstation, desk surface, chair position, device states, and access-control interfaces before broad photography expands.
- Identify whether adjacent office and corridor should be included as secondary search zones.

#### Boundary status

- Boundary inherited from patrol: office doorway sealed, corridor limited, neighboring office not yet frozen.
- Boundary correction: include printer alcove and badge-reader side of corridor due to possible propagation path.
- Boundary uncertainty: patrol did not document whether supervisor entered before officer control; office entry log not yet reconciled.

#### Access and control

- Known entrants before technician control: responding officer, EMS evaluation team, site manager, one coworker who located subject.
- Potential secondary interface points: badge reader at corridor turn, networked workstation wake events, printer queue access.
- Control issue: office looked quiet but digital and access interfaces may carry more sequence value than obvious focal objects.

#### Earliest perishable anchors to capture

- Screen state on workstation when first seen by technician.
- Chair position relative to desk and body position.
- Badge-reader status and visible hallway clock relationship.
- Beverage temperature state and lid position if still meaningful.
- Phone orientation, charge state, and visible lock-screen timing if present.

#### Early contamination vectors

- EMS moved chair edge slightly to access subject.
- Site manager reportedly touched light switch before patrol arrival.
- Coworker may have leaned over desk during first discovery.

#### Likely explanation-density carriers already active

- Repeated use of "quiet natural death" language from workplace personnel.
- Office cleanliness being treated as if it proves absence of prior disturbance.
- Lack of obvious struggle being used as a substitute for sequence analysis.

#### Immediate next transition

- Technician phase should prioritize structural anchor ranking and graph construction before any broad collection expansion.

### Example B2. Form 4. Anchor Hierarchy Sheet

| Rank | Anchor | Type | Strength | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Badge-reader event at office corridor turn | machine time anchor | high | needs log validation against server time offset |
| 2 | Workstation wake and lock sequence | machine time anchor | high | user attribution not automatic |
| 3 | Body position relative to chair and desk | scene-condition anchor | medium-high | EMS moved chair edge slightly |
| 4 | Beverage container warmth and lid state | scene-condition anchor | medium | temperature decay window broad |
| 5 | Coworker discovery statement | witness anchor | medium-low | statement confidence high but timing precision weak |
| 6 | Site manager statement about lights and office normalcy | witness / interpretive anchor | low | may import routine assumptions rather than event-state anchors |

#### Anchor notes

- Machine-timed access and device activity should discipline sequence before witness confidence does.
- Chair movement is usable but must remain marked as partially responder-affected.
- "Quiet office" is not an anchor and should not be allowed to outrank logged or measured state.

### Example B3. Form 5. Scene Graph Summary Sheet

#### Key nodes

- Office interior
- Desk and workstation
- Chair and body location
- Corridor badge-reader point
- Printer alcove
- Site manager access path
- Coworker discovery path

#### Key edges

- badge access -> corridor movement toward office
- workstation wake -> desk interaction window
- chair orientation -> body location relation
- printer alcove -> possible secondary activity path
- site manager entry -> light-switch disturbance path

#### Explicit unknowns

- Whether desk interaction preceded body collapse or followed earlier office activity.
- Whether printer alcove matters to sequence or is routine background noise.
- Whether coworker entry changed any small-object position before patrol control.

#### Major interface points

- badge-reader to office threshold
- workstation state to user action inference
- witness discovery path to scene disturbance path

#### Highest-uncertainty node or relation

- relation between machine activity and actual human presence inside the office at the relevant interval.

### Example B4. Form 7. Collection Priority Log

| Priority | Item / trace | Why it matters | Hypothesis split value | Packaging / preservation note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Access-control log acquisition request | anchors office-entry timing | high | preserve source time standard and export metadata |
| 2 | Workstation activity capture | may split self-directed office use from outside interaction | high | preserve native log context and offset notes |
| 3 | Chair, desk-edge, and nearby floor relationship documentation | preserves scene-condition sequence logic | medium-high | photography and measurement before movement |
| 4 | Beverage container documentation and collection if justified | may refine recent-presence window | medium | preserve lid state and temperature note if still meaningful |
| 5 | Printer alcove paper or device status | may test secondary path relevance | medium-low | do not elevate unless graph review keeps node live |

#### Collection expansion justification

- Broad office-item collection is not yet justified.
- Current priority is machine-timed sequence and focal-condition preservation.

#### High-risk packaging distinctions to preserve

- separate direct scene-condition documentation from items gathered after desk area disturbance
- distinguish native device data from later export copies

### Example B5. Form 8. Laboratory Discrimination Request

#### Question to the laboratory

- If latent or biological transfer work is pursued on selected desk-edge or chair-contact surfaces, can any result class help separate routine occupant interaction from a more recent external-contact hypothesis, or would method limits make that distinction structurally weak?

#### Relevant anchors

- badge-reader timing
- workstation wake sequence
- chair and body relation documented prior to technician movement
- witness statements are not primary anchors for this request

#### Requested tests in order

1. consultative review on whether the requested distinction is method-supportable at all
2. if supportable, prioritized limited processing on desk-edge and chair-contact interfaces only
3. report result classes with explicit warning if routine-occupancy background prevents meaningful split

#### Submission cautions

- Do not frame null or mixed results as support for natural or external-cause conclusions.
- This request is about narrowing interaction class, not proving event type.

### Example B6. Form 12. Integrated Scene Package Cover Sheet

#### Section checklist

- inherited-boundary audit: complete
- anchor hierarchy: complete
- scene graph summary: complete
- ranked collection logic: complete
- lab request framing: complete

#### Current package summary

- Scene presents low visual drama but high risk of false closure.
- Strongest sequence anchors are machine-based, not witness-based.
- Main technician value came from correcting quiet-scene assumptions and protecting graph honesty.
- Main remaining uncertainty concerns correlation between device activity and actual human interaction.

#### Transfer statement

- This package should be read as a structural office-sequence package, not as a presumptive natural-death package.
- Detective and laboratory readers should preserve the difference between machine activity, scene condition, and workplace narrative.

## Example Set C. Detectives

### Source scenario

- Warehouse Loading Bay Reconciliation Failure

### Role focus

This set models the detective's burden: ranked hypotheses, explicit contradiction handling, visible excuse pressure, and honest transfer into the next narrowing step.

### Example C1. Form 2. Scene Phase Closure Contract

#### Current closure status

- Investigative phase not ready for broad stabilization.
- Current detective phase may transfer forward only as a bounded alternative field with named contradictions.

#### Resolved questions

- Inventory discrepancy is real and not a bookkeeping typo alone.
- Loading-bay camera interruption window overlaps documented movement gap.
- At least one routine workflow explanation has been demoted by anchored timing conflict.

#### Unresolved questions

- Whether discrepancy arose from insider manipulation, opportunistic unauthorized removal, or staged reconciliation error after the fact.
- Whether camera interruption reflects direct concealment or unrelated maintenance overlap.
- Whether vehicle movement evidence can be tightly linked to the missing inventory window.

#### Live inhibitors

- full badge and vehicle correlation incomplete
- lab or specialist review on packaging traces pending
- witness sequence language still partially contaminated by shift-routine assumptions

#### Owned deferrals

- badge and vehicle cross-correlation: detective owner
- targeted lab clarification on packaging transfer distinctions: laboratory owner after detective request refinement
- camera-maintenance verification: detective owner with supervisor visibility

#### Release / transfer conditions

- Detective phase may not be summarized as theft proved, insider proved, or camera tampering proved.
- Transfer must preserve at least two live explanatory branches.

### Example C2. Form 6. Ranked Hypotheses Table

| Rank | Hypothesis | Current support | Known conflicts | Kill condition | Smallest next narrowing move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Insider-assisted unauthorized removal during camera interruption window | badge pattern irregularity, workflow familiarity indicators, reconciliation timing | no direct anchor yet tying named individual to removal path | badge and vehicle correlation fails to align with interruption window | complete badge, vehicle, and dock-door sequence merge |
| 2 | Opportunistic external removal during temporarily weakened control | camera gap and loading-bay exposure window | current access pattern still leans toward controlled familiarity | reliable internal access trail becomes dominant | verify perimeter and delivery-route anomalies |
| 3 | Post-event record manipulation hiding an earlier workflow discrepancy | reconciliation edits and reporting lag | physical movement indicators suggest event-state change, not record-only problem | physical sequence anchors confirm real removal event in active window | test whether movement anchors and packaging traces require actual load movement |

#### Eliminated hypotheses

- simple inventory-counting error with no event-state change: eliminated by physical and timing discrepancies already anchored
- purely accidental camera outage with no relevance to discrepancy: not eliminated, but demoted as standalone explanation because timing coincidence remains too strong

### Example C3. Form 9. Excuse Ledger

| Insider theory | "Only staff know this workflow" | familiarity pressure can overstate insider certainty | preserve external and staged-path alternatives until split | Detective |
| Missing direct witness | "No one saw anything, so the logs will tell us" | silence does not automatically upgrade logs to full explanation | define exactly what the logs can and cannot split | Detective |
| Lab reliance | "The lab will sort out whether this was tampered with" | vague outsourcing of thinking | write targeted discrimination question before submission | Detective |

#### Current explanation-space summary

- Strongest current pressure is toward insider closure because it is narratively efficient.
- Most dangerous current excuse is treating camera interruption as self-explaining.
- Team must resist using routine familiarity to bypass actual sequence proof.

#### Unowned deferral check

## Example Set D. Laboratory Personnel

### Source scenario

- Office Quiet Death

### Role focus

This set models the laboratory role as an intake and limit-discipline role rather than a passive processing unit.

### Example D1. Form 8. Laboratory Discrimination Request

#### Question to the laboratory

- Does any targeted processing on selected office-contact surfaces meaningfully separate routine occupant handling from a more recent external-contact hypothesis, or would expected background signal make the distinction structurally weak?

#### Relevant anchors

- badge-reader timing window
- workstation wake and lock sequence
- technician-documented chair and desk relationship
- site-manager and coworker statements are contextual only and do not control the request

#### Requested tests in order

1. intake consultation on whether the distinction is supportable using available substrates
2. if supportable, narrow processing on desk-edge, chair-back contact zone, and one selected device interface only
3. return result classes with explicit statement of whether routine-background contact prevents useful narrowing

#### Submission cautions

- This request should not be converted into a generic print or DNA sweep.
- A null or mixed result does not resolve cause of death, event type, or presence class without further context.

#### Current explanation-space summary

## Example Set E. Supervisors and Review Boards

### Source scenario

- Apartment Hallway Collapse

### Role focus

This set models the supervisory burden: testing closure honesty, reading the hypothesis field, forcing redesign, and protecting the packet against false stability.

### Example E1. Form 2. Scene Phase Closure Contract

#### Current closure status

- Scene phase not ready for administrative closure without explicit acknowledgment of early disturbance and pre-control movement uncertainty.
- Specialized intake may continue, but the current package does not justify language implying stable event sequence.

#### Resolved questions

- immediate threat and medical necessity phases have ended
- known early entrants named at least at first-pass level
- patrol-to-technician transfer risk identified rather than hidden

#### Unresolved questions

- pre-control resident movement near body location
- whether early "fall" language came from witness observation or hallway rumor
- which visible floor or object conditions are scene-native versus responder-affected

#### Live inhibitors

- access-control verification outstanding
- technician audit of inherited disturbance not yet complete
- no lawful ranked sequence claim yet available

#### Owned deferrals

- access verification: detective
- inherited disturbance audit: scene technician
- narrative contamination review: detective with supervisor follow-up

#### Release / transfer conditions

- No one may mark the scene package as structurally processed until disturbance and access uncertainty are surfaced in the cover sheet and anchor hierarchy.
- Closure approved only for phase transfer, not for sequence stabilization.

### Example E2. Form 6. Ranked Hypotheses Table

| Rank | Hypothesis | Current support | Known conflicts | Kill condition | Supervisor review note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Subject collapsed in hallway before any later body movement | body location, witness discovery timing, responder path record | early disturbance still clouds floor-state interpretation | strong evidence of post-collapse repositioning | keep live but do not let it become a finished sequence |
| 2 | Event began elsewhere and hallway is visibility point, not initiation point | shared hallway access, disturbance uncertainty, open-unit interface | no strong carry-path anchor yet | scene graph and access review fail to support alternate path | lower-ranked does not mean negligible |
| 3 | Early witness framing distorted later reading of a more complex sequence | repeated fall language and hallway rumor risk | physical anchors may still support simple collapse | machine and scene anchors align tightly with simple path | supervisor should protect this branch until narrative audit completes |

#### Eliminated hypotheses

- none eliminated solely for convenience or morale

### Example E3. Form 11. Post-Scene Redesign Review

#### Process residue review

- First two minutes of uncontrolled traffic remain the major preventable diagnosability loss.
- Patrol did not fail by entering quickly; patrol risk came from incomplete early entrant logging.
- Building staff were not physically separated fast enough from explanatory conversation.
- Shared-hallway scenes need a standard outer-boundary expansion rule earlier than currently written.

#### Redesign opportunities

- add first-five-minute entrant log prompt to patrol response card for shared residential corridors
- require explicit technician inherited-boundary audit before first collection sweep on shared-access scenes
- add witness-language contamination check to detective intake on all communal-space death scenes
- make supervisor review of early disturbance mandatory before any public-facing case summary language is used

#### Ownership

- patrol-card change proposal: training supervisor
- technician inherited-boundary audit rule: scene-tech lead
- communal-space narrative contamination check: detective-unit trainer
- early-disturbance review requirement: supervisory review board lead

### Example E4. Form 12. Integrated Scene Package Cover Sheet

#### Section checklist

- closure honesty review: complete
- ranked alternatives review: complete
- redesign review: complete
- transfer fragility note: complete

#### Current package summary

- Package remains usable, but only if early hallway disturbance is treated as a live structural constraint rather than as a footnote.
- Main supervisory concern is false stabilization around a simple hallway-collapse story.
- Main redesign target is early shared-space boundary control and entrant logging.

#### Transfer statement

- This package may move forward only as a bounded, disturbance-aware packet.
- Any summary that suppresses the early shared-space uncertainty should be treated as a supervisory failure, not as a stylistic choice.

## Example Set F. Prosecutors and Downstream Legal Teams

### Source scenario

- Quiet Office Warrant Review

### Role focus

This set models lawful downstream reading: what can be said, what cannot be said, what reentry is required, and what transfer fragility must remain visible.

### Example F1. Form 2. Scene Phase Closure Contract

#### Current closure status

- Investigative phase may support limited downstream legal review.
- Investigative phase does not yet support maximal certainty language about event type, outside involvement, or precise internal office chronology.

#### Resolved questions

- office access and device timing matter more than workplace narrative impressions
- technician and investigative materials support a bounded machine-and-scene anchor reading
- quiet-scene appearance does not justify natural-cause or noncriminal closure by itself

#### Unresolved questions

- exact relation between machine activity and physical human interaction
- whether any external-contact hypothesis remains materially live after targeted review
- whether requested legal move would outrun current anchor stack

#### Live inhibitors

- supportability of any requested contact-trace distinction still partly consultative
- testimony foundation for some office-normalcy claims weak
- alternative sequence path not yet killed cleanly

#### Owned deferrals

- legal reentry question on whether additional clarification is needed before warrant language: prosecutor owner
- supporting clarification on machine-time offsets: detective owner
- laboratory consultative response on contact distinction supportability: laboratory owner

#### Release / transfer conditions

- Any warrant or charging language must preserve uncertainty about precise sequence where the anchor stack remains layered.
- Downstream legal review may proceed only as a bounded claim exercise, not as an advocacy expansion exercise.

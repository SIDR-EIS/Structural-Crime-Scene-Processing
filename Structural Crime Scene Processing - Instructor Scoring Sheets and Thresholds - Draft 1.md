## Academy Evaluation Principles

### Principle 1: Score structure, not style

A polished narrative with weak anchors should score lower than a rougher packet with strong structural discipline.

### Principle 2: Do not punish explicit residue

The learner should not lose points merely for leaving uncertainty visible.

### Principle 3: Penalize premature closure heavily

The most dangerous failure mode is false certainty disguised as competence.

### Principle 4: Separate doctrine weakness from pressure weakness

Some learners understand the model in discussion but collapse into legacy habits under speed or ambiguity. That requires a different remediation path than simple conceptual misunderstanding.

### Principle 5: Require transferability

If the output cannot survive handoff without live narration, the learner is not ready for independent use.

### Automatic flags

Check any that apply:

- Premature category closure
- Quiet misread as completion
- Activity misread as progress
- Anchor hierarchy missing or flat
- Visible focal point treated as causal center
- Witness or supervisor framing outranked stronger anchors
- Hidden residue not stated
- Output depends on oral explanation

### Severity note

If any automatic flag is present at a severe level, the learner should not receive an unqualified pass even with a numerically acceptable total.

## Fast Threshold Table

### Score bands

`20-24`

Pass, assuming no severe automatic flag.

`16-19`

Pass with targeted follow-up if no severe automatic flag is present.

`12-15`

Remediate and re-run.

`0-11`

Not ready. Return to foundational retraining before re-assessment.

### Severe automatic flags that override score

Any of the following should override a simple pass:

- learner states a strong causal or role conclusion with no lawful anchor basis
- learner collapses the scene into a familiar case category despite explicit conflicting anchors
- learner treats silence as proof of either completion or hidden action without bounded transitions
- learner produces an output that cannot be understood without live oral rescue
- learner ignores explicit contamination or explanation-density carriers that the scenario makes central

These are not minor polish issues. They indicate the learner is still structurally unsafe under pressure.

## Pass / Remediate Decision Matrix

### Pass

Use when:

- total score is 20 or higher
- no severe override flag is present
- output is transferable
- learner stays within lawful inference

### Pass with targeted follow-up

Use when:

- total score is 16-19
- the learner is structurally sound overall
- the weaknesses are bounded to one or two dimensions
- the learner does not create dangerous false closure

Follow-up should be assigned within the next training cycle.

### Remediate and re-run

Use when:

- total score is 12-15
- or one severe automatic flag appears
- or the learner understands the doctrine but collapses under pressure

Re-run should occur after targeted remediation rather than simple repetition.

### Not yet ready for independent field use

Use when:

- total score is 11 or below
- or multiple severe automatic flags appear
- or the learner repeatedly converts uncertainty into narrative performance

These learners need a reset, not a confidence boost.

## Remediation Routing Guide

Use the learner's weakest dimensions to assign remediation.

### Weak in closure discipline

Symptoms:

- vague "done when done" language
- administrative release treated as analytical closure
- no distinction between resolved and deferred

Assign:

- closure-contract drills
- package-cover-sheet exercises
- compare weak and strong closure statements side by side

### Weak in state and transition discipline

Symptoms:

- cannot name current state
- cannot name next expected transition
- reads calm or noise as proof

Assign:

- state-map repetition drills
- timed transition-identification drills
- stall-detection case reviews

### Weak in anchor discipline

Symptoms:

- witness or appearance outranks stronger anchors
- anchor list is flat and unordered
- no statement of what anchors do not constrain

Assign:

- anchor-ranking exercises
- anchor-conflict scenarios
- "what this anchor does not prove" writing drills

### Weak in carrier detection

Symptoms:

- ignores patrol, supervisor, or witness framing as contamination
- misses hidden experts or explanation load
- treats interpretation pathways as neutral

Assign:

- carrier-tracing exercises
- oral-history dependency audits
- hidden-expert detection workshop

### Weak in propagation reasoning

Symptoms:

- visible focal point treated as the full cause
- no path analysis
- weak initiation / propagation distinction

Assign:

- threshold and interface case exercises
- resistance-path mapping drills
- alternate-path enumeration tasks

### Weak in collection prioritization

Symptoms:

- broad, prestige-driven collection
- weak justification for order
- no attention to perishability or distinction-preserving packaging

Assign:

- ranked-collection worksheets
- packaging-distinction drills
- side-by-side comparison of legacy and structural collection plans

### Weak in lawful inference / residue honesty

Symptoms:

- polished story outruns evidence
- ambiguity treated as failure
- explicit residue avoided

Assign:

- residue-writing drills
- lawful-elimination rewrite exercises
- excuse-ledger exercises

### Weak in transferability

Symptoms:

- output requires spoken explanation
- notes assume hidden context
- logic is implied instead of explicit

Assign:

- handoff packet reconstruction drills
- blind peer-review exercises
- rewrite work for unknown downstream reader

## Academy Pass / Remediate Thresholds

Use these thresholds only after at least:

- two scenario exercises
- one timed drill
- one handoff / transferability check

### Ready for independent field use

Requirements:

- no severe override flag in the most recent two assessments
- average score of 20 or higher
- no dimension below 2 in the latest final assessment
- successful transferability check

### Ready with supervision

Requirements:

- average score of 16 or higher
- no more than two dimensions below 2
- no repeated severe false-closure pattern
- supervisor aware of weak dimensions

### Needs targeted remediation

Requirements:

- average score between 12 and 15
- or repeated weakness concentrated in one to three dimensions
- or pressure performance materially below classroom performance

### Needs foundational retraining

Requirements:

- average score below 12
- or repeated severe override flags
- or persistent inability to distinguish anchors from stories

### Promotion rule

No learner should move to independent field use merely because the average is high if severe false-closure behavior remains present.

The method is designed to protect against dangerous overconfidence, not to reward it.

## Recommended Instructor Workflow

For each learner:

1. score the exercise using the Master Exercise Scoring Sheet
2. complete the Exercise-Specific Addendum Sheet
3. assign a threshold status
4. route remediation by weakest dimension
5. record cohort status
6. re-run under pressure after remediation

This keeps evaluation from becoming anecdotal.

## Common Instructor Failure Modes

### Passing charismatic learners too early

If the learner sounds field-ready but outruns anchors, the instructor is grading presence instead of structure.

### Using the total score without override judgment

The score is necessary but not sufficient. Severe false closure must override the number.

### Remediating by repetition alone

Repeating the same exercise without changing the failure pattern usually produces performance adaptation, not structural learning.

### Treating slow careful work as weakness

In early training, careful structural honesty is usually preferable to fast legacy confidence.

## Purpose

This document provides fully populated dashboard examples for multiple agency archetypes using the reporting logic already established elsewhere in the project.

It is not a new metric system.

It is a demonstration layer.

Its job is to show what a finished reporting surface looks like when command, pilot, training, specialist, drift, and scale-up dashboards are actually filled in with structured signals instead of placeholders.

---

## Why Completed Dashboard Examples Are Needed

Blank dashboard templates are necessary, but they do not solve the last mile problem.

Leaders often understand the tiles in theory and still build weak dashboards in practice because they:

- overcount document completion
- flatten red conditions into averages
- replace structural indicators with productivity counts
- omit uncertainty bands
- strip out the narrative line that explains what the numbers actually mean

Completed examples are needed because they show:

- how signal should roll upward
- how uncertainty should remain visible
- how red conditions stay red even when other areas are improving
- how local constraint should be reported without becoming an excuse
- how a dashboard can remain brief without becoming shallow

## How to Read These Examples

Each archetype contains six completed dashboards:

1. Command Sponsor Dashboard
2. Pilot Operations Dashboard
3. Training and Certification Dashboard
4. Laboratory and Downstream Legal Dashboard
5. Drift and Recovery Dashboard
6. Scale-Up Dashboard

Each completed dashboard includes:

- the reporting window
- the coverage confidence
- the populated sections or tiles
- the key indicators leadership should read first
- a short narrative strip
- the immediate implication for leadership

These examples are meant to be interpreted structurally.

That means:

- a green-heavy dashboard is not automatically healthy if transfer or drift remains red
- a gray condition is not a failure, but it must remain visible
- an amber condition is not neutral; it is a live warning that something important is not yet secure
- red conditions should remain readable at a glance and should not be averaged away

---

## Dashboard Example Architecture

All completed examples in this document preserve the following rules:

- denominator and sample size stay visible where they matter
- tiles and sections remain tied to source evidence
- a short narrative strip accompanies the numbers
- corrective action remains implied or explicit
- local variation affects context, not metric meaning

The examples below are therefore not decorative.

They are meant to model disciplined leadership reading.

## What Changes Across Archetypes

These examples show that real dashboards should change shape across agency types.

What changes:

- coverage confidence
- specialist visibility
- likely drift source
- supervisory leverage
- pacing of scale decisions
- density of local variants and policy attachments

Large municipal departments usually show:

- stronger specialist visibility
- more handoff surfaces
- higher risk of hidden oral rescue in complex internal systems

County or rural offices usually show:

- more gray conditions where visibility is genuinely thin
- more role compression
- slower remediation cycles

Campus or institutional agencies usually show:

- stronger package readability than cultural containment
- unique pressure from rumor, familiarity, and administrative smoothing
- high risk of polished overreading from institutional data systems

Regional task-force environments usually show:

- translation problems as a primary failure mode
- interface fragility as the most important scale blocker
- gaps between formal convergence and live interoperability

---

## What Must Not Change Across Archetypes

Local dashboards may differ in composition, but the fixed core should not move.

The following should remain stable:

- red conditions stay visible
- gray conditions remain acceptable when visibility is honestly limited
- narrative strips stay short and concrete
- tiles remain tied to evidence sources
- transfer integrity is never buried under production or activity metrics
- corrective action always has an owner and a closure standard

If a local dashboard changes those rules, the agency is not adapting the system.

It is replacing it.

## Common Misreadings

### Misreading 1: The numbers are the model

They are not.

The structure is the model.

These numbers exist to show what a finished dashboard looks like, not to supply a universal benchmark.

### Misreading 2: More green is always better

It is not.

A dashboard can become more truthful by adding amber, red, or gray where local leaders previously hid instability.

### Misreading 3: Gray means failure

It does not.

Gray means visibility is not sufficient for a credible color call.

That is often the most honest available report.

### Misreading 4: The narrative strip is optional

It is not.

Without the narrative strip, leaders tend to invent their own interpretation and drift back toward comfort metrics.

### Misreading 5: The dashboard exists to reassure command

It does not.

It exists to preserve structural visibility long enough for command to make good decisions.

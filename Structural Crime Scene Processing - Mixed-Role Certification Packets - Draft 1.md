# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Mixed-Role Certification Packets

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document adds the bridge between single-role proficiency and mixed-role operational readiness.

It exists because a learner can be competent inside one role and still become dangerous the moment the work has to cross a role boundary.

Single-role drills test whether a learner can carry the method inside a job.

Mixed-role certification tests whether multiple learners can carry the method across jobs without laundering residue, collapsing uncertainty, or forcing the next role to guess what the previous role actually meant.

This companion is therefore not optional if the goal is adoption architecture rather than isolated role training.

It is the point where the institution decides whether role-specific strength survives transfer, compression, disagreement, and handoff pressure.

---

## What This Document Does

This companion provides:

- a formal mixed-role certification grammar for moving from isolated role drills to cross-role work
- explicit status codes for mixed-role clearance, conditional clearance, restriction, remediation, deferral, and revocation
- standard packet structures for adjacent-role handoffs, three-role chains, multi-role operational chains, downstream legal transfer, and reentry after failure
- cross-role clearance review sheets and convergence notes
- failed-handoff diagnosis tools
- promotion gates from single-role proficiency to mixed-role readiness
- bridge sheets for supervised live observation and supervised live role use
- certification-board summary sheets for larger clearance decisions
- cohort tracking tools so mixed-role readiness is documented rather than assumed

This document should prevent several recurring failures:

- treating strong single-role work as proof of safe team performance
- certifying a role handoff that only works when an instructor interprets it for the team
- allowing one dominant learner to cover for weaker structural work by the others
- treating team agreement as proof of correctness when the agreement came from rhetorical pressure
- confusing coordinated motion with lawful convergence
- sending learners into supervised live settings without any formal cross-role clearance logic

## Why Mixed-Role Certification Must Be Separate

Institutions often behave as if mixed-role readiness appears automatically once each participant passes a role-specific drill.

That assumption is false.

A patrol learner may write an acceptable first-pass record in isolation and still poison the scene-tech handoff once another person starts asking for clarification under time pressure.

A scene technician may produce a strong graph in isolation and still collapse into overstatement when a detective pushes for a quick explanation.

A detective may maintain bounded hypotheses alone and then silently compress them when a prosecutor asks for a courtroom-ready version.

A laboratory learner may write a careful structured response and then let imprecise language pass once a cross-role meeting starts moving too fast.

A supervisor may ask the right questions on paper and still reward false stability once the team wants closure.

Mixed-role certification is needed because cross-role failure does not look exactly like single-role failure.

It often appears as:

- silent convergence around an unstated assumption
- transfer language that sounds smooth but hides uncertainty
- a strong participant doing interpretive work for everyone else
- a learner abandoning role boundaries in the name of teamwork
- polite agreement replacing documented contradiction
- one role accepting an unsafe package because it trusts the speaker rather than the record

Those are not edge cases.

They are exactly the pressures that define institutional work.

## When to Use These Packets

Use these packets when any of the following decisions must be made:

- whether learners who passed single-role drills may enter mixed-role drills
- whether a role pair is safe for adjacent handoff work
- whether a three-role chain is safe under normal conditions
- whether a multi-role chain is safe under compressed or timed conditions
- whether a learner may move from mixed-role drills into supervised live observation
- whether a learner or team may move from supervised live observation into supervised live role use
- whether a previous mixed-role clearance should be restricted, remediated, or revoked after failure or drift

Do not use this document for casual classroom collaboration.

Use it only when the team needs a written decision about cross-role readiness.

## Mixed-Role Certification Principles

### Principle 1: Certify transfer, not summary skill

The issue is not whether the team can produce a convincing overview.

The issue is whether each role leaves the next role with a cleaner rather than dirtier thinking problem.

### Principle 2: Mixed-role pressure changes the failure pattern

A learner who is stable in isolation may become unsafe once another role asks for compression, speed, or interpretive convenience.

### Principle 3: Convergence must preserve residue

If the team only achieves agreement by deleting uncertainty, the certification event failed even if the team sounded aligned.

### Principle 4: Role boundaries matter more under pressure, not less

A mixed-role environment does not justify role drift.

It increases the need for role discipline.

### Principle 5: Oral rescue is a certification failure

If the packet, handoff, or review only works because the evaluator reconstructs what the team intended, the team's transfer structure is not yet safe.

### Principle 6: A mixed-role pass can be asymmetric

The team as a whole may be provisionally usable while one role remains restricted.

Mixed-role certification should allow bounded asymmetry when it is explicit.

### Principle 7: Team calm is not the same as team safety

A calm room may only mean that no one is challenging the hidden assumption.

### Principle 8: Mixed-role readiness is not the same as live readiness

A team can be safe for a controlled mixed-role drill and still not be safe for supervised live use.

### Principle 9: Promotion gates must be explicit

Movement from single-role proficiency to mixed-role work, and from mixed-role work to supervised live settings, must be documented as a bounded gate rather than a cultural impression.

## Mixed-Role Status Codes

### Cleared for Mixed-Role Drill Entry

The learner or team may participate in the named mixed-role scope without special restriction beyond ordinary instructional oversight.

### Cleared for Mixed-Role Work with Restrictions

The learner or team may participate in the named mixed-role scope only with the documented limits, task restrictions, or supervision requirements.

### Remediate and Re-Run

The learner or team is not safe for the requested mixed-role scope yet, but the main failure pattern appears specific enough for targeted remediation.

### Not Yet Ready for Mixed-Role Work

The learner or team is not safe for the requested mixed-role scope, and the weakness is broad enough that simple targeted remediation is not sufficient by itself.

### Deferred Certification

Certification cannot yet be decided because evidence is incomplete, required roles are missing, or the needed packet sequence has not been completed.

### Revoked Pending Review

A prior mixed-role clearance is being restricted or withdrawn because later work revealed unsafe convergence, unsafe transfer, or unresolved role drift.

---

## Severe Override Triggers

Any of the following should override a raw score band or a generally positive instructor impression:

- the team reaches agreement by deleting documented residue
- a role output only works after oral repair by another participant or evaluator
- one role routinely performs interpretive rescue for another role without documenting the resulting distortion
- a handoff hides uncertainty, ownership, or deferral in order to appear more complete
- one participant dominates convergence in a way that suppresses lawful disagreement
- the team uses time pressure as a justification for undocumented narrowing
- a downstream role accepts or amplifies upstream overclaim without challenge
- a supervisor or prosecutor stabilizes the room by converting unresolved state into implied closure
- the team cannot identify where a breakdown occurred once the chain is challenged
- a previously remediated failure reappears under compression

A severe override trigger does not always require permanent restriction.

It does require that the certification record state the problem explicitly and define the next control step.

## Evidence Required Before Mixed-Role Certification

A mixed-role certification packet should normally include:

- all relevant individual role signoff records
- the mixed-role drill output or outputs
- the score sheets or evaluator notes for the mixed-role event
- any timed-drill scorecard if compression was part of the assessment
- handoff records, package excerpts, or transfer notes used during the event
- remediation records from earlier role-specific or mixed-role failures if relevant
- observation notes if the certification decision depends on supervised live observation rather than classroom-only work

Do not certify mixed-role readiness from memory or from a general sense that the team "worked well together."

## Standard Mixed-Role Certification Workflow

### Stage 1: Define the mixed-role scope

Specify whether the packet concerns an adjacent-role handoff, a three-role chain, a downstream legal transfer, a compressed operational run, or a supervised-live bridge decision.

### Stage 2: Confirm single-role prerequisites

Verify that each participant has the required role-specific status for the scope being considered.

### Stage 3: Review the evidence packet

Read the outputs, handoff documents, score sheets, remediation notes, and observation records.

### Stage 4: Identify the main convergence pattern

Determine whether the team converged lawfully, converged by suppression, failed to converge, or produced only rhetorical convergence.

### Stage 5: Judge transfer safety role by role

Ask whether each role made the next role safer, weaker, or dependent on verbal rescue.

### Stage 6: Determine the certification band

Choose Clear, Restricted, Remediate, Not Yet Ready, Deferred, or Revoked.

### Stage 7: Define the exact scope granted or withheld

Do not issue a mixed-role pass without saying which chain, which pressure level, and which supervision level are included.

### Stage 8: Record follow-up

If the result is restricted, remediated, deferred, or revoked, the review trigger and next packet must be named.

## Mixed-Role Scope Categories

### Adjacent-Role Handoff Pair

A two-role event focused on one handoff boundary.

Examples include patrol to scene technician, scene technician to detective, or detective to laboratory.

### Three-Role Chain

A bounded three-role sequence where each role must inherit the prior role's work without oral repair.

### Full Operational Chain

A larger cross-role event that includes collection, interpretation, review, and transfer.

### Supervisor-Mediated Review Chain

A mixed-role event centered on supervisory questioning, review-board logic, or closure challenge.

### Downstream Legal Transfer Chain

A mixed-role event that tests whether an integrated package can survive movement into charging, warrant, transfer, or court-facing preparation.

### Compressed or Timed Mixed-Role Run

A multi-role event where time pressure is part of the assessment rather than incidental background.

### Supervised Live Bridge Scope

A gate packet used when considering movement from mixed-role drills into supervised live observation or supervised live role use.

## Packet Set A. Patrol to Scene Technician Adjacent-Role Handoff

### Use this set for

Use this packet when certifying patrol and scene-technician learners on the earliest handoff boundary, especially in disturbances where the first role can easily damage diagnosability before the second role establishes structure.

### Recommended scenario base

- Apartment Hallway Collapse
- any equivalent first-arrival disturbance scene with witness traffic and perishable state

### Roles included

- patrol or first response
- scene technician
- evaluator or facilitator

### Evidence packet

- patrol role-specific signoff
- technician role-specific signoff
- first-pass handoff note
- technician anchor capture or graph output
- any timed-drill note if compression was used

### Must be true for clear pass

- Patrol handed forward perishable state, disturbance load, and witness separation status without implied solution language.

- Patrol merged witness narratives before technician intake.
- Technician accepted interpretive claims as if they were anchors without challenge.
- The pair produced a smooth record that concealed what was actually uncertain.

### Adjacent-role packet clearance

- lead evaluator

- lead evaluator
- role lead or training supervisor

### Downstream legal transfer certification

- lead evaluator
- supervisory or legal lead as appropriate

### Compressed-run or live-bridge certification

- lead evaluator
- training supervisor
- receiving supervisor if live bridge is involved

### Revocation or major restriction

- training supervisor or role lead
- secondary evaluator when possible

## Common Mixed-Role Certification Failure Modes

### Passing a team because one person carried everyone else

That is hidden centralization, not safe transfer.

### Mistaking agreement for lawful convergence

Agreement that comes from suppressed residue is not a pass.

### Ignoring weak boundaries because the room felt collaborative

Mixed-role warmth is not a substitute for role discipline.

### Allowing oral repair to count as transfer success

If the packet fails without speech, the packet failed.

### Treating conditional clearance as a quiet full pass

If the restricted scope is not explicit, it will vanish in practice.

### Jumping from mixed-role drills to live use without a bridge decision

Mixed-role readiness and live readiness are different gates.

### Using remediation as a formality instead of a targeted repair loop

If the actual failed boundary is not named, the same failure will return.

## Recommended Use Sequence

1. Confirm single-role signoff for all participants.
2. Use the promotion gate sheet to decide entry into the first mixed-role scope.
3. Run the appropriate packet set for the actual boundary or chain under review.
4. Complete the cover sheet, clearance review sheet, and any convergence or failed-handoff notes required.
5. Issue a mixed-role decision using the relevant certification or restriction form.
6. If the learner or team is progressing upward, use the next packet set rather than assuming automatic expansion.
7. Use the bridge sheet before moving to supervised live observation or supervised live role use.
8. Track the result in the cohort sheet and recertify or revoke if later evidence requires it.

## Closing Note

Single-role certification answers whether a learner can hold the method inside a job.

Mixed-role certification answers whether the institution can trust that method to survive movement across jobs.

That is the difference between a training stack and an operational system.

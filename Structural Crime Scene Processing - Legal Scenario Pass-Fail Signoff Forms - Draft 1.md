# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Legal Scenario Pass-Fail Signoff Forms

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document turns the downstream legal training track into a formal signoff system.

It is written for:

- prosecutors
- legal reviewers
- case coordinators
- legal supervisors
- academy instructors
- mixed investigator-prosecutor facilitators
- implementation leads
- audit leads reviewing downstream readiness

Its job is to answer a specific question:

how should an institution formally sign off, conditionally sign off, deny signoff, or defer signoff for downstream legal scenario work so that readiness is judged by bounded legal structure rather than by persuasive fluency, confidence, or social comfort.

---

## What This Document Does

This document provides:

- pass / fail signoff forms for downstream legal scenario work
- conditional-clearance forms
- remediation signoff forms
- signoff rules for mixed-role legal review
- scenario-specific signoff sheets
- cohort and program tracking forms
- signoff override and revocation guidance

It is not:

- another scoring rubric
- a substitute for instructor judgment
- a substitute for scenario answer keys
- a jurisdiction-specific charging or warrant policy manual
- a shortcut around calibration

Scoring and signoff are related.

They are not the same thing.

Scoring asks:

- how strong was the work
- where did the learner drift
- what dimensions need attention

Signoff asks:

- is this learner, team, or review function safe for independent downstream legal use
- if not, what exactly blocks signoff
- if partially yes, under what conditions

That is a different decision.

## Why Signoff Forms Are Needed

Without explicit signoff forms, most organizations drift toward one of four weak substitutes:

- informal instructor comfort
- senior-lawyer reassurance
- numeric score alone
- a vague statement that the learner is "basically ready"

Those substitutes are dangerous.

A learner can score decently and still be structurally unsafe if they:

- overclaim under pressure
- erase residue for cleanliness
- rely on oral rescue
- demand stronger story instead of stronger structure

Likewise, a learner can look cautious and still be unsafe if their caution is confused, untransferable, or incapable of producing a legally usable bounded output.

Signoff forms are needed because the downstream legal layer is where structural honesty is most often pressured to become rhetorical closure.

If readiness is not documented carefully here, the whole project can still regress even after strong field adoption.

---

## When to Use These Forms

Use these forms:

- at the end of downstream legal scenario exercises
- at the end of timed charging, warrant, transfer, challenge, or demonstrative drills
- during mixed investigator-prosecutor certification events
- during remediation completion checks
- before clearing a learner or team for independent downstream legal review in the pilot
- when reassessing a learner after a serious overclaim or transfer failure
- when a legal supervisor needs to restrict or revoke prior readiness status

Do not use signoff forms merely as attendance records.

Use them only when a real readiness decision is being made.

---

## Signoff Principles

Every downstream legal signoff in this project should follow eight principles.

### Principle 1: Sign off bounded safety, not rhetorical talent

A learner should not be cleared simply because their legal writing is forceful, concise, or professionally familiar.

### Principle 2: Severe overclaim defeats simple passing

Some failures are so structurally dangerous that they override a numerically acceptable performance.

### Principle 3: Residue honesty is a positive sign

Do not deny signoff merely because the learner kept meaningful unresolveds visible.

### Principle 4: Transferability matters

If the output only works when the learner explains it orally, readiness is not yet real.

### Principle 5: Conditional signoff must actually be conditional

A conditional pass is not a polite full pass.

It must name restrictions, follow-up, and the next review point.

### Principle 6: Use signoff to document risk, not hide it

If a learner is fragile under pressure, the form should say fragile under pressure.

Do not soften the risk to protect morale.

### Principle 7: Mixed-role signoff is not the same as individual signoff

A team can converge well together even while one person remains unsafe independently.

### Principle 8: Readiness can be revoked

If later work shows structural drift, signoff status should be revisited rather than preserved ceremonially.

## Signoff Status Codes

Use the following status terms throughout this document:

- `Clear Pass`
- `Pass with Conditions`
- `Remediate and Re-Run`
- `Not Yet Ready for Independent Downstream Legal Use`
- `Deferred Signoff`
- `Revoked Pending Review`

These terms mean:

### Clear Pass

The learner or team may perform the defined downstream legal task independently within the assessed scope.

### Pass with Conditions

The learner or team may perform the defined task only with named restrictions, coaching, or review requirements until follow-up is complete.

### Remediate and Re-Run

The learner or team is not yet ready, but the failure pattern is bounded enough for targeted remediation followed by reassessment.

### Not Yet Ready for Independent Downstream Legal Use

The learner or team remains structurally unsafe and should return to foundational retraining before reassessment.

### Deferred Signoff

The signoff decision cannot yet be made because the evidence base is incomplete, the assessment was interrupted, or the packet scope was insufficient.

### Revoked Pending Review

An earlier clearance is suspended because later evidence suggests unsafe downstream drift.

---

## Severe Override Triggers

Any of the following should block `Clear Pass` even if the numeric score is otherwise acceptable:

- actor action, motive, intent, or exact sequence stated as settled without lawful anchor basis
- meaningful residue deleted because it weakens the memo, affidavit, charging paragraph, or demonstrative
- oral rescue treated as normal transfer method
- warrant, charging memo, or trial visual used to rewrite the package rather than reflect it
- structural weakness answered with demand for stronger story rather than stronger support
- a learner repeatedly converting conditional support into declarative fact
- mixed-role convergence achieved only because unresolveds were smoothed away

These are not style errors.

They are downstream safety failures.

## Evidence Required Before Signoff

Every signoff decision should identify the evidence reviewed.

The evidence set may include:

- the completed scenario output
- the instructor scoring sheet
- the instructor threshold decision
- notes on automatic flags
- exemplar comparison if used
- rewrite attempts
- remediation history
- mixed-role calibration notes if relevant
- timed-drill conditions if relevant

If a signoff is being made without a named evidence base, the decision is weak even if it comes from a respected senior lawyer.

## Standard Signoff Workflow

Every formal signoff event should follow eight stages.

### Stage 1: Define scope

Write:

- learner or team name
- role being assessed
- scenario or drill name
- assessment type
- independent or mixed-role scope

### Stage 2: Confirm evidence set

List the exact materials reviewed.

### Stage 3: Check threshold status

Record:

- total score
- severe flags
- dimension weaknesses
- instructor confidence level

### Stage 4: Determine signoff band

Choose the provisional band:

- clear pass
- conditional pass
- remediation
- not ready
- deferred

### Stage 5: Identify scope of clearance

Write what the learner is being cleared to do, or not cleared to do.

### Stage 6: Write conditions or blockers

Every non-clear decision must name:

- the main blocker or condition
- the risk created if ignored
- the next required action

### Stage 7: Obtain signoff signatures

The reviewer and, where relevant, the learner or supervisor should sign or acknowledge.

### Stage 8: Set next review point

Every non-clear status needs a date or trigger for reassessment.

Every signoff should state the precise scope being cleared.

Use one or more of these scope categories:

- charging review
- warrant review
- trial-transfer review
- pretrial challenge and reentry review
- demonstrative audit
- mixed investigator-prosecutor legal review
- downstream legal supervisor review

This matters because a learner may be ready for one type of downstream work and not another.

## Common Signoff Failure Modes

### Treating a pass with conditions as a full pass

This is one of the most common breakdowns.

If the conditions are never read again, the institution has simply renamed a weak pass.

### Allowing score totals to outrank severe structural flags

A learner can produce a numerically respectable performance and still remain unsafe.

### Letting senior comfort replace documentation

If a respected prosecutor says "they're fine" but the form is incomplete, the system is drifting back toward personality rule.

### Using signoff to avoid difficult remediation conversations

If leadership fears saying not ready, signoff becomes theater.

### Failing to define scope

A learner signed off for bounded charging review may later be treated as cleared for demonstrative audit or challenge response without ever having earned that scope.

### Never revoking fragile clearances

If later drift is obvious but prior signoff remains untouched, the signoff layer becomes politically symbolic rather than operational.

## Recommended Use Sequence

Use these forms in this order:

1. individual scenario signoff forms
2. remediation or conditional signoff forms as needed
3. mixed-role signoff forms where relevant
4. cross-scenario final signoff form
5. supervisor downstream-clearance acknowledgment
6. revocation or reassessment forms only when later evidence requires them

This sequence keeps:

- exercise-level judgment
- then remediation logic
- then team-level convergence
- then final readiness
- then operational acceptance

in the right order.

## Closing Note

The downstream legal layer is where a structurally honest case is most likely to be asked to become rhetorically comfortable.

That is why signoff matters here.

A strong signoff system does not ask only:

- can this learner write persuasively
- can this team sound aligned
- does this memo feel prosecutable

It asks:

- can this person or team preserve boundedness under pressure
- can they keep residue visible without collapsing usefulness
- can they refuse overclaim even when clarity is being demanded
- can they transfer the file without hidden rescue

If the answer is yes, signoff should say so clearly.

If the answer is no, signoff should say that just as clearly.

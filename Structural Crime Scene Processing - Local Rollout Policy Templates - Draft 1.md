# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Local Rollout Policy Templates

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document converts the Structural Crime Scene Processing project from a library of guidance into a set of local policy templates an agency can adapt and adopt.

It is written for:

- command sponsors
- executive sponsors
- implementation owners
- legal and policy units
- training leads
- audit leads
- supervisory leadership
- agency project managers
- local working groups drafting directives, orders, and standing instructions

Its job is to answer a specific question:

how should an agency translate the project's doctrine, audit logic, observation system, dashboard logic, and role adaptations into actual local rollout policies without diluting the method.

---

## What This Document Does

This document provides:

- local rollout policy templates
- policy clauses agencies can adapt
- fixed-core protections for local policy drafting
- policy sections for launch, pilot use, governance, observation, audit, training, dashboards, signoff, specialist alignment, and scale-up
- local adoption notes and drafting cautions

It is not:

- jurisdiction-specific legal advice
- a substitute for local labor, HR, procurement, or legal review
- a software policy
- a final signed policy for any specific agency

These are templates.

They are designed to be adapted lawfully and carefully.

They are not designed to be copied blindly.

---

## Why Local Policy Templates Are Needed

Many reforms die in the gap between guidance and policy.

The documents exist.

Training occurs.

People talk about the new model.

But the standing rules of the institution still reward:

- speed over structure
- confidence over boundedness
- narrative polish over transferability
- local comfort over visible drift

Without explicit local policy, the project remains vulnerable to three failures.

First, adoption becomes discretionary.

Second, supervisors fall back to legacy judgment because the policy layer never changed.

Third, local agencies improvise policy language that sounds aligned while quietly changing the meaning of the method.

These templates exist to prevent that.

## Policy Drafting Rule

Local policy may adapt:

- agency titles
- review cadence
- local reporting references
- local specialist names
- local incident thresholds
- local approval pathways

Local policy may not adapt away:

- explicit uncertainty
- deferral ownership
- transferability
- structural supervision
- bounded legal and investigative language
- the obligation to expose false stability

If a local policy changes those, it is not adopting the project.

It is replacing it.

---

## Fixed Core Policy Commitments

Every local policy package built from this document should preserve these commitments:

1. scene handling is treated as structured hypothesis reduction, not narrative closure
2. scene or phase closure must be observable, not merely administrative
3. unresolved items must remain visible and owned
4. handoffs must become more transferable, not more oral
5. supervisors must reward structural honesty over comfort
6. downstream legal and lab layers may not repair weak structure by rhetorical inflation
7. drift must be observed, audited, and acted on
8. local adaptation must preserve the fixed core

If a draft policy does not clearly protect these commitments, it is incomplete.

## How to Use These Templates

Use the templates in four passes.

### Pass 1: Structural drafting

Choose the policy sections the agency needs and adapt:

- titles
- authority lines
- local systems
- role names

without altering the core meaning.

### Pass 2: Local legal and labor review

Run the draft through local counsel, labor, and policy review as needed.

### Pass 3: Implementation reconciliation

Check the policy against:

- observation variants
- audit packets
- dashboard population guides
- training and signoff logic

### Pass 4: Pilot validation

Use the draft policy during pilot rollout and revise before permanent adoption.

Policy should follow lived pilot evidence, not fantasy org charts.

---

## Policy Package Architecture

Most agencies will need a policy package with six parts:

1. adoption directive
2. pilot operating order
3. role and supervision policy
4. observation, audit, and dashboard policy
5. training, certification, and signoff policy
6. scale-up and drift-recovery policy

These may live in one document or several.

The important point is that they operate together.

## Template A. Adoption Directive

### Purpose

Use this as the command-level order or memorandum that formally launches the method locally.

### Suggested title

`Agency Directive: Adoption of Structural Crime Scene Processing`

### Template text

**Section 1. Policy statement**

This agency adopts Structural Crime Scene Processing as the operating framework for designated scenes, scene reviews, investigative handoffs, specialist review, and associated training and audit functions within the scope defined below.

The agency adopts this framework to improve diagnosability, transferability, lawful boundedness, and institutional visibility into unresolveds and drift.

**Section 2. Core rule**

Personnel operating under this directive shall not treat narrative coherence, evidence count, administrative closure, or polished legal language as substitutes for structural support.

**Section 3. Scope**

This directive applies to:

- designated pilot units
- designated supervisors
- designated specialist and downstream review units
- designated training and audit functions

**Section 4. Core obligations**

Within this directive's scope, personnel shall:

- preserve observable closure criteria
- preserve owned residue and deferrals
- preserve transferability
- use structural questions in review
- document drift and corrective action

**Section 5. Authority**

The following local roles are designated:

- executive or command sponsor
- implementation owner
- training lead
- audit lead
- lab integration lead if applicable
- downstream legal lead if applicable

**Section 6. Effective date**

This directive becomes effective on:

**Section 7. Review date**

This directive shall be formally reviewed on:

### Local adaptation notes

Adapt:

- agency name
- role titles
- scope language
- pilot-unit naming

Do not delete:

- structural support language
- explicit uncertainty protection
- drift-review commitment

## Template C. Role and Supervision Policy

### Purpose

Use this to formalize role expectations and supervisory duties.

### Suggested title

`Role and Supervision Policy for Structural Crime Scene Processing`

### Template text

**Section 1. Role expectations**

Personnel in pilot scope shall perform according to the obligations defined in the relevant role guides and any approved local role-specific observation variants.

**Section 2. Supervisory question standard**

Supervisors shall prioritize structural questions concerning:

- state
- closure criterion
- anchor logic
- residue
- deferral ownership
- what would break the leading hypothesis

**Section 3. Prohibited supervisory shortcuts**

Supervisors shall not rely primarily on:

- early theory demands
- motive demand absent support
- speed demand as closure proof
- comfort or fluency as maturity signal

**Section 4. Role-compression rule**

Where one person performs multiple operational functions, that role compression shall be marked explicitly in documentation and observation. Role compression shall not justify deletion of transfer or review logic.

**Section 5. Specialist interaction rule**

Scene, investigative, laboratory, and downstream legal functions shall not be treated as passive downstream cleanup for earlier weak structure.

### Local adaptation notes

Adapt:

- titles
- scope groupings
- local supervision hierarchy

Do not delete:

- supervisory question standard
- role-compression marking rule
- specialist interaction rule

## Template D. Observation and Local Variant Policy

### Purpose

Use this to formalize live observation and local observation variants.

### Suggested title

`Observation and Agency Variant Policy`

### Template text

**Section 1. Observation requirement**

Pilot scenes, reviews, and designated specialist or downstream events shall be observed according to the standard observation companion or an approved local role-specific variant.

**Section 2. Approved local variants**

Any local role-specific observation variant must:

- preserve the fixed core
- identify its agency archetype or local constraint
- map upward to audit and dashboard layers
- identify red-trigger escalation paths

**Section 3. Variant approval**

No local variant shall be used as standard practice until it has been:

- drafted
- piloted
- reviewed by the implementation owner and audit lead
- approved by the designated authority

**Section 4. Forbidden local deletions**

Local variants shall not delete:

- ownership of unresolveds
- transferability checks
- interpretive-boundary concerns
- severe warning signals

**Section 5. Observation integrity**

Where work cannot be fully observed, personnel shall mark the limitation explicitly rather than infer green conditions from missing visibility.

### Local adaptation notes

Adapt:

- observation threshold language
- local approver titles
- local form references

Do not adapt away:

- fixed-core rule
- variant approval discipline
- explicit marking of low-visibility conditions

## Template E. Audit and Corrective-Action Policy

### Purpose

Use this to formalize audit packet use and corrective-action requirements.

### Suggested title

`Audit and Corrective-Action Policy for Structural Crime Scene Processing`

### Template text

**Section 1. Audit requirement**

The agency shall use designated live implementation audit packets to review readiness, live use, transfer integrity, supervisory and training integrity, specialist alignment, drift, and scale-up decisions.

**Section 2. Required dispositions**

Each completed audit packet shall end in one of the following dispositions:

- Proceed
- Proceed with Conditions
- Hold and Remediate
- Reset the Stage

**Section 3. Corrective-action requirement**

All material findings shall result in:

- named fault line
- assigned owner
- due date
- evidence required for closure

**Section 4. Repeat-fault rule**

Fault lines that recur across review periods shall be escalated and shall not be treated as closed simply because discussion occurred.

**Section 5. Red-indicator rule**

Red indicators tied to structural safety, transfer failure, overclaim, or hidden oral rescue shall receive named review and follow-up dates.

### Local adaptation notes

Adapt:

- audit cadence
- audit owner titles
- local review bodies

Do not delete:

- mandatory dispositions
- corrective-action evidence requirement
- repeat-fault escalation

**Section 1. Dashboard requirement**

The agency shall maintain the designated rollout dashboards or approved local equivalents aligned to the project dashboard templates.

Dashboard indicators shall be populated from named evidence sources and shall not rely primarily on recollection, local impression, or throughput proxies.

## Template G. Training, Certification, and Signoff Policy

### Purpose

Use this to formalize local training and readiness decisions.

### Suggested title

`Training, Certification, and Signoff Policy for Structural Crime Scene Processing`

### Template text

**Section 1. Training requirement**

Personnel assigned to pilot or adoption scope shall complete designated training and evaluation requirements before independent operation within the defined scope.

**Section 2. Certification standard**

Certification decisions shall be based on:

- completed assessments
- scoring sheets
- remediation status
- signoff forms where applicable

**Section 3. Conditional pass rule**

## Template I. Downstream Legal Review Policy

### Purpose

Use this when the local policy package includes prosecutors or downstream legal teams.

### Suggested title

`Downstream Legal Review Policy for Structural Crime Scene Processing`

### Template text

**Section 1. Review posture**

Downstream legal review shall read case packages for anchor logic, bounded sequence, residue, eliminations, threshold match, and transferability before seeking persuasive presentation.

**Section 2. Overclaim prohibition**

Charging language, affidavit language, transfer memoranda, reentry notes, and demonstratives shall not state more than the package lawfully supports.

**Section 3. Residue rule**

Meaningful unresolveds shall remain visible where their deletion would create structural distortion.

**Section 4. Signoff requirement**

Downstream legal readiness decisions shall use approved signoff forms where the agency has designated certification or pilot-readiness requirements.

**Section 5. Revocation and reentry**

Downstream readiness may be revisited where later events show severe overclaim, hidden rescue dependence, or demonstrative or affidavit distortion.

### Local adaptation notes

Adapt:

- reviewer titles
- local legal-product names
- approval pathways

Do not delete:

- overclaim prohibition
- residue rule
- signoff rule where certification exists

## Fixed-Core Preservation Checklist for Policy Drafters

Use this checklist before local approval.

- Does the draft preserve explicit uncertainty where lawful?
- Does the draft preserve deferral ownership?
- Does the draft preserve transferability requirements?
- Does the draft preserve structural supervision?
- Does the draft preserve severe-warning escalation?
- Does the draft preserve audit and corrective-action requirements?
- Does the draft preserve dashboard evidence-traceability rules?
- Does the draft preserve signoff revocation authority where signoff exists?

If any answer is no, the draft should not be approved yet.

## Local Policy Review Questions

Before issuance, ask:

1. What does this policy force people to do differently from the old model?
2. What false stability can still hide behind this language?
3. What would this policy allow a weak supervisor to keep doing?
4. What would this policy allow a weak downstream reviewer to overclaim?
5. What evidence would show that the policy is working?

If those questions cannot be answered, the draft is still too soft.

## Common Local Policy Failure Modes

### Publishing aspiration instead of rule

If the policy says what the agency values but not what it requires, the old model remains in force.

### Preserving legacy reward structure

If supervisors are still effectively graded on speed, polish, and low visible friction, local policy has not yet changed the system.

### Letting legal review remain outside the package

If downstream legal posture remains untouched, the local system can still force narrative inflation later.

### Creating policy without audit consequence

If policy breaches produce no observation, audit, or review consequence, the document is symbolic.

### Treating local adaptation as local exemption

If the policy turns "local difference" into permission to delete transferability, uncertainty, or residue, the package is unsafe.

### Failing to update the policy after pilot evidence

If the agency learns something in pilot but the policy never changes, the document becomes a memorial to an earlier design rather than a living control surface.

---

## Recommended Policy Review Cadence

Review the local policy package:

- before pilot launch
- 30-60 days into pilot use
- after the first formal drift audit
- before scale-up
- after any major red-level failure showing policy weakness

Policy that is never re-reviewed becomes decorative quickly.

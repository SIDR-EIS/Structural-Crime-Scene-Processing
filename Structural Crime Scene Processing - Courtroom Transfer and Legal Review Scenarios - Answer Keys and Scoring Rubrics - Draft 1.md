# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Courtroom Transfer and Legal Review Scenarios - Answer Keys and Scoring Rubrics

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document adds the evaluation layer for the courtroom transfer and legal review scenario set.

It is written to help instructors, legal supervisors, mixed prosecutor-investigator training teams, and review boards do four things consistently:

1. distinguish strong downstream structural reading from polished overclaim
2. score legal-stage outputs without rewarding narrative inflation
3. identify where learners are still letting urgency substitute for package discipline
4. make downstream legal debriefs concrete rather than impressionistic

These are not perfect answer sheets.

They are bounded instructor keys.

The point is not to train lawyers or legal reviewers to memorize one approved theory.

The point is to train them to preserve bounded sequence, anchor logic, residue, and transferability under downstream pressure.

---

## How to Use This Companion

Use this document with:

- the courtroom transfer and legal review scenarios
- the prosecutors and downstream legal teams role guide
- the supervisors and review boards role guide
- the field forms packet
- the existing general exercise-scoring companion

Best practice is:

1. let learners work the legal scenario first
2. score the output against the legal-specific rubric
3. compare the output to the bounded answer-key logic
4. debrief where clarity became overclaim

Do not hand this document to learners before first pass.

It exists to sharpen instruction, not to flatten legal review into template memorization.

## Master Scoring Model

Score each learner or team across eight downstream-legal dimensions.

Use a `0-3` scale for each dimension.

### Score meanings

`0` = absent, off-model, or actively legacy-driven  
`1` = partial recognition, but weak structural control  
`2` = solid structural legal reasoning with some gaps  
`3` = strong, disciplined, transferable legal-stage output

### The eight dimensions

1. Legal-move matching discipline
2. Structure-versus-rhetoric separation
3. Anchor and elimination discipline
4. Overclaim and grammar-inflation detection
5. Residue and reentry honesty
6. Transferability and handoff review
7. Bounded rewrite quality
8. Structure-request and redesign quality

### Global interpretation

`0-7`  
The learner is still operating mostly inside a legacy legal model built around clean-story appetite.

`8-13`  
The learner recognizes some downstream risks but still lets urgency and rhetoric outrun structure.

`14-19`  
The learner is functionally using the new model in legal review, though not yet consistently.

`20-24`  
The learner is producing strong legal-stage outputs that preserve package truth under pressure.

## Dimension-Level Guidance

### 1. Legal-move matching discipline

`0`

The learner assumes the contemplated legal move is justified because the scenario is already in legal review.

`1`

The learner questions the move weakly but still treats it as mostly self-validating.

`2`

The learner distinguishes between full, partial, and not-yet support with reasonable discipline.

`3`

The learner cleanly matches the legal move to the actual package state and says what would need to change for a stronger move.

### 2. Structure-versus-rhetoric separation

`0`

The learner blends observation, bounded sequence, conditional interpretation, and rhetoric into one layer.

`1`

The learner notices rhetorical overreach but cannot separate the layers consistently.

`2`

The learner separates most layers correctly.

`3`

The learner clearly distinguishes observed state, bounded sequence, conditionals, and rhetorical framing throughout.

### 3. Anchor and elimination discipline

`0`

The learner relies mainly on smooth summary, intuition, or institutional confidence.

`1`

The learner identifies some anchors but does not use them to constrain legal claims.

`2`

The learner names the main anchors and some eliminations with reasonable accuracy.

`3`

The learner builds a credible legal reading around anchor hierarchy, elimination logic, and the negative limits of those anchors.

### 4. Overclaim and grammar-inflation detection

`0`

The learner accepts overclaiming verbs, motive language, or exact sequence wording without challenge.

`1`

The learner notices some aggressive language but misses how grammar is doing evidentiary work.

`2`

The learner identifies most overclaim points and some hidden conditionals.

`3`

The learner cleanly detects where wording outruns support and can explain why each phrase is risky.

### 5. Residue and reentry honesty

`0`

The learner hides unresolveds or treats residue as something to delete.

`1`

The learner allows some residue to remain visible but still treats it as an embarrassment.

`2`

The learner keeps residue and reentry logic mostly visible.

`3`

The learner clearly identifies live residue, distinguishes it from preventable weakness, and preserves reentry conditions honestly.

### 6. Transferability and handoff review

`0`

The learner ignores whether the package can survive transfer or assumes oral rescue is normal.

`1`

The learner senses transfer weakness but cannot diagnose it clearly.

`2`

The learner identifies major transfer-fragility points.

`3`

The learner clearly reviews whether another legal reader could inherit the package without hidden rescue and names what must be stabilized.

### 7. Bounded rewrite quality

`0`

The learner either preserves the inflated language or rewrites so vaguely that the legal move becomes meaningless.

`1`

The learner reduces some certainty but leaves important inflation intact.

`2`

The learner produces a usable bounded rewrite with moderate discipline.

`3`

The learner produces clear, useful, legally functional language that remains faithful to the package limits.

### 8. Structure-request and redesign quality

`0`

The learner responds to weakness by demanding a cleaner story or more confidence.

`1`

The learner asks for follow-up, but mostly in broad or narrative-driven terms.

`2`

The learner requests additional structure or follow-up that would likely improve the package.

`3`

The learner asks for the highest-value structural clarification, rewrite, or reentry action without demanding unsupported closure.

## Exercise 1. Charging Review in the Apartment Hallway Case

### What a strong first legacy legal reading usually sounds like

"The 4A resident is in the building, the victim called him, there was prior conflict, and the threshold scene looks staged enough to charge now."

This is plausible charging appetite.

It is also structurally aggressive.

### What a strong structural answer should recognize

- the package may support a serious charging move only partially rather than fully
- presence in the building is not the same as closed violence sequence
- the threshold remains unresolved as initiation site, force site, and path geometry
- the quoted detective summary contains multiple overclaim verbs and category claims
- residue about motive, sequence, and stairwell path must remain visible

### Key expectations by learner task

#### 1. Does the package support the contemplated charging move fully, partially, or not yet

Best expected answer:

Partially.

Strong learners should explain that the package may support a narrowed charging posture or continued charging review, but it does not support the full smooth sequence contained in the detective summary.

Weak answers:

- "Fully, because the suspect is clearly there"
- "Not yet" without explaining what the package already does support

#### 2. Overclaim phrases in the detective summary

Expected strong answers should challenge phrases such as:

- "confronted"
- "inside 4C"
- "during a domestic dispute"
- "struck him"
- "fled toward the stairwell"
- "after staging"
- "to resemble a hallway collapse"

Good learners may note that even "the suspect" can carry too much closure if the surrounding passage is written as established full-sequence fact rather than as a working theory.

#### 3. Bounded-sequence rewrite

Strong rewrite should preserve:

- the 4A resident's presence in the building
- meaningful interaction pressure near the event window
- threshold significance
- unresolved initiation and motive issues

Strong example logic:

"Current evidence places the 4A resident in the building during the critical interval and supports significant interaction pressure between the resident and victim before the threshold event. The present package supports a homicide theory under active review, but initiation site, exact sequence through the threshold, and motive remain bounded rather than fully closed."

Weak rewrite:

Any version that simply swaps a few verbs while keeping the same exact story.

#### 4. Three strongest anchors and what they do not prove

Expected strong anchors:

- key-fob entry / no recorded front-door exit
- recovered victim phone with outgoing call to 4A resident
- threshold transfer pattern / threshold significance

Good alternate inclusion:

- documented post-event hand injury

Expected "does not prove" logic:

- entry does not prove violence sequence
- outgoing call does not prove motive or confrontation content
- threshold transfer pattern does not prove first decisive force occurred inside 4C
- hand injury does not prove causal mechanism

#### 5. Top live residues that must remain visible

Expected strong answers:

- initiation site remains unresolved
- exact threshold path geometry remains unresolved
- motive remains unresolved
- stairwell route remains unproven
- damp towel / sink significance remains unresolved

#### 6. Highest-value follow-up or narrowing request

Reward answers that ask for structure, not drama.

Best expected requests include:

- a bounded sequence memo focused only on threshold geometry and current support levels
- cleaner anchor hierarchy distinguishing presence, interaction pressure, and force sequence
- explicit elimination table showing what has and has not been ruled out

Penalize answers that simply ask investigators to "make the narrative stronger."

### Scoring notes specific to Exercise 1

Reward learners who separate charge sufficiency from narrative completeness.

Penalize learners who:

- treat motive language as already earned
- let domestic framing substitute for support
- erase threshold ambiguity
- delete residue because it complicates the charge memo

### Fast score cues

`20-24`

Learner allows a bounded charging posture but strips away the overclaimed sequence cleanly.

`14-19`

Learner spots most inflated phrases but still lets the charge theory read smoother than the package.

`8-13`

Learner notices some caution points but still treats the existing summary as basically acceptable.

`0-7`

Learner reproduces a polished charging story with little or no residue.

## Exercise 2. Warrant Review in the Quiet Office Case

### What a strong first legacy legal reading usually sounds like

"We know someone got in before the victim, and this employee had the right access and motive context. Tighten the affidavit and get the warrant."

This is exactly the sort of downstream pressure the scenario is built to expose.

### What a strong structural answer should recognize

- the package strongly supports a path problem
- the package does not yet strongly tie that path problem to the named employee
- the affidavit performs closure by grammar
- the contemplated warrant move may be too broad against the named employee on the present record
- urgency is making the legal reader treat plausibility as anchor

### Key expectations by learner task

#### 1. Clauses in the affidavit that exceed the package

Expected challenges:

- "knows"
- "the employee entered the building before the victim"
- "disabled the climate-control system to create cover"
- "waited inside the conference room"
- "killed the victim"
- "removed the victim's phone"
- "departed through a rear path"

Some learners may argue that "rear path not visible to the camera system" is directionally plausible. Strong learners should still note that the affidavit is presenting it as established actor sequence.

#### 2. Separate observation, bounded sequence, conditionals, and rhetoric

Strong answer should place:

Observed state:

- alarm disarm
- victim entry on camera
- elevator stop
- HVAC interruption
- lack of direct hallway / conference-room camera coverage

Bounded sequence:

- something may have occurred on the floor before victim entry
- the package contains a real path-gap problem

Conditional interpretation:

- if a hidden actor used the floor before 6:56, then the elevator / HVAC discontinuities may matter to access logic

Unsupported rhetoric:

- employee-specific hidden-entry murder sequence as stated in the affidavit

#### 3. Does the package support the contemplated warrant move

Best expected answer:

Not yet cleanly against the named employee on the present anchor field, or at most only partially if the legal team can articulate a narrower, actor-linkage basis not yet visible in the scenario.

Reward either of these positions if defended well:

- not yet for this target because actor linkage is underdeveloped
- partially for a narrower move focused on further path and communication clarification rather than a full hidden-entry murder theory

Penalize:

- automatic yes because the theory feels plausible
- automatic no without engaging what the package does support

#### 4. Bounded affidavit rewrite

Strong rewrite should preserve:

- unexplained pre-entry floor activity
- incomplete path closure
- relevance of victim phone / device and communication ecosystem if justified
- the difference between anomaly and actor attribution

Strong example logic:

"Current scene and building records reflect unexplained timing and access discontinuities on the relevant floor before the victim's documented entry. The present package does not yet resolve who generated those discontinuities, but it supports additional process to test whether communications, device activity, or linked access data further narrow the responsible actor set."

#### 5. Highest-value structure or follow-up request

Expected strong answers:

- request clearer actor-linkage anchors before targeting the employee's residence or full digital ecosystem
- request a bounded path-and-attribution table instead of a stronger murder narrative
- request preservation and review of victim communications or building-system interpretation first

#### 6. Why "everyone understands what this really means" is a risk signal

Best expected answer:

Because it signals tolerance for rhetoric that depends on shared intuition rather than package-explicit support. It is a transfer risk, a hidden-rescue warning, and a sign that legal comfort is being treated as substitute for anchor discipline.

### Scoring notes specific to Exercise 2

Reward learners who distinguish support for inquiry from support for the full actor theory.

Penalize learners who:

- let the affidavit keep one smooth hidden-entry sequence
- turn HVAC interruption into intent proof
- ignore absence of actor linkage
- confuse urgency with sufficiency

### Fast score cues

`20-24`

Learner cleanly rejects or sharply narrows the affidavit theory while preserving lawful usefulness.

`14-19`

Learner sees the overclaim problem but still allows too much actor-specific certainty.

`8-13`

Learner rewrites tone more than structure.

`0-7`

Learner largely accepts the affidavit as written.

## Exercise 3. Trial Transfer in the Warehouse Loading Bay Case

### What a strong first legacy legal reading usually sounds like

"The prior prosecutor had this under control, the technical package is dense, and the internal timeline is probably safe to inherit."

That is the transfer trap.

### What a strong structural answer should recognize

- technical density does not equal transferability
- the outgoing memo is a warning that the file depends on tacit explanation
- the polished timeline is cleaner than the integrated package
- the senior industrial analyst may be acting as hidden human reconciliation
- trial pressure makes oral rescue feel acceptable when it is actually dangerous

### Key expectations by learner task

#### 1. Main signs of transfer fragility

Expected strong answers:

- reliance on a polished timeline rather than the underlying package
- live ambiguity still present in causal mechanism
- outgoing memo saying the theory is clean only "once you understand"
- no live meeting with the prior prosecutor for several days
- repeated oral reliance on a senior industrial analyst

#### 2. Strongly supported, partially supported, and overclaimed parts of the timeline

Strongly supported:

- false continuity signal
- sequence deviation
- abnormal system / maintenance environment

Partially supported:

- bypass of normal loading sequence
- causal connection between the deviation and the fatal movement

Overclaimed:

- "intentionally"
- "triggered" if written as closed personal action rather than bounded causality
- "after ensuring the victim was in the danger zone"

#### 3. First five structure-recovery questions

Expected strong questions include:

- What exactly in the package supports intention as opposed to exploitation of instability?
- Which claims in the timeline depend on oral explanation rather than explicit anchor reference?
- What does the senior analyst know that the package itself does not currently externalize?
- Which causal link is most vulnerable if transferred cold to another reader?
- What reworked timeline would look like if every sentence had to cite explicit package support?

#### 4. What should happen to the internal timeline

Best expected answer:

It should be rebuilt or heavily annotated before trial use.

Strong learners will say the timeline should:

- mark support levels
- preserve conditional dependencies
- remove or narrow intention claims
- be made legible without hidden analyst rescue

#### 5. Role of the senior industrial analyst

Expected strong answer:

The analyst is likely functioning as a hidden explanation carrier or human reconciliation layer. That matters because the institution may be mistaking human memory and interpretation for a stable transferable case structure.

#### 6. Handoff-risk memo to supervision

Strong memo logic should include:

- the case is not unsafe, but it is transfer-fragile
- timeline and causal language need review before trial posture hardens
- hidden-expert dependence must be externalized
- supervision should support a structure-recovery pass rather than punish the new prosecutor for reopening complexity

### Scoring notes specific to Exercise 3

Reward learners who see transfer fragility as a substantive case risk, not as an inconvenience.

Penalize learners who:

- trust prior confidence as proof
- accept oral rescue as normal
- let technical density obscure overclaim
- keep the polished timeline mostly intact

### Fast score cues

`20-24`

Learner cleanly diagnoses hidden reconciliation and proposes a credible structure-recovery response.

`14-19`

Learner sees the transfer problem but still tolerates too much inherited timeline language.

`8-13`

Learner notices oral dependence but treats it as manageable background rather than structural defect.

`0-7`

Learner mostly accepts the inherited file as mature because it looks detailed.

## Exercise 4. Pretrial Challenge and Reentry in the Quiet Office Case

### What a strong first legacy legal reading usually sounds like

"The defense found some noise, but unless it destroys the whole theory we should hold posture and keep moving."

That is the reentry trap.

### What a strong structural answer should recognize

- the package itself anticipated this class of reentry
- the new material attacks dependency links, not necessarily the entire case
- prior repetition of the theory is creating lock-in pressure
- reentry is a structural duty, not a public-relations embarrassment
- narrowing the theory may strengthen rather than weaken the case

### Key expectations by learner task

#### 1. Does the new material trigger formal reentry

Best expected answer:

Yes.

Strong learners should explain:

- an explicit reentry condition already existed
- building-system reinterpretation and alternate-meeting evidence affect important dependency links
- the proper threshold is not total destruction of the case but meaningful change to the support structure of the current theory

#### 2. What the new material weakens, leaves untouched, and opens

Expected strong answers:

Weakens:

- hidden-entry certainty
- deliberate-HVAC-cover theory
- smooth lay-in-wait framing

Leaves more intact:

- importance of the conference room
- reality of unresolved path discontinuities
- need to explain victim phone / meeting context

Newly opens:

- possible scheduled or invited early contact
- alternate interpretation of building-system anomalies
- broader actor or access-set review

#### 3. Prosecution statements or demonstratives requiring revision

Expected strong answers:

- any statement claiming concealed early entry as closed fact
- any statement treating HVAC interruption as deliberate cover
- any demonstrative timeline built on a settled lay-in-wait sequence
- witness-preparation outlines that silently assume the older theory as established

#### 4. Bounded internal legal note

Strong note should:

- say the prior theory must be narrowed pending review
- separate what remains live from what is newly weakened
- preserve reentry rather than resist it
- identify what claims should be paused in outward-facing language

#### 5. Follow-up requests

Expected strong answers:

- renewed technical interpretation of elevator and HVAC data
- clarification of the confidential meeting lead
- review of charging and demonstrative language for overclaim
- package refresh showing current state after reentry

#### 6. Why "does this completely break the case" is the wrong threshold

Best expected answer:

Because it asks the wrong question. Structural review should ask whether the new material changes support for specific dependency links, not whether it annihilates the entire case at once.

### Scoring notes specific to Exercise 4

Reward learners who preserve reentry discipline without becoming vague or panicked.

Penalize learners who:

- defend the prior theory because of sunk cost
- ignore the documented reentry trigger
- treat the new material as irrelevant unless catastrophic
- fail to narrow outward-facing case language

### Fast score cues

`20-24`

Learner cleanly triggers reentry, narrows the theory responsibly, and protects package honesty.

`14-19`

Learner accepts some reentry but still clings too hard to prior posture.

`8-13`

Learner acknowledges the new material but treats it as mostly a litigation nuisance.

`0-7`

Learner prioritizes holding the line over rereading the package.

## Exercise 5. Demonstrative Audit Before Trial

### What a strong first legacy legal reading usually sounds like

"It is just a demonstrative. It needs to be clear, not perfect."

That sentence often marks the beginning of visual overclaim.

### What a strong structural answer should recognize

- visual tools can outrun the package faster than prose
- exact arrows, exact timing, and motive banners are making unresolved questions look settled
- the detective's discomfort is a transfer warning, not mere caution
- a demonstrative can still be effective if rebuilt around bounded sequence

### Key expectations by learner task

#### 1. Elements of the demonstrative that outrun the package

Expected strong answers:

- the exact red-arrow path from 4A through 4C to stairwell
- the smooth 90-second sequence compression if presented as exact
- "staged threshold collapse"
- "ongoing domestic escalation"

#### 2. What can remain, be narrowed, or be removed

Strong answer:

Can remain in narrowed form:

- floorplan showing relevant zones rather than exact path
- timing display as bounded interval or sequence window
- threshold significance marker

Must be narrowed or removed:

- exact movement arrow
- exact force-location implication
- motive banner
- staging conclusion as settled fact

#### 3. Central slide rewrite

Strong rewrite should preserve:

- threshold significance
- interaction pressure
- live ambiguity on exact path and first decisive force
- bounded sequence instead of theatrical certainty

Example logic:

"Current evidence supports a concentrated interaction and critical event sequence around the 4C threshold during a compressed interval. Exact movement path, exact first-force location, and motive remain more bounded than this simplified display would otherwise suggest."

#### 4. Why "only a visual aid" is not a defense

Best expected answer:

Because visual aids teach the audience what the case is. If the graphic is cleaner than the package, it is not merely illustrating argument. It is rewriting the structure through image.

#### 5. Review-board style note on demonstrative standards

Strong note should say demonstratives must:

- track anchor support
- preserve conditional dependencies
- avoid exact-path claims where only zone significance is supported
- avoid motive or staging claims that remain live interpretations

#### 6. What the scenario teaches about persuasion versus distortion

Expected strong answer:

Persuasion can simplify while staying faithful to bounded truth. Distortion begins when simplification converts live uncertainty into settled visual fact.

### Scoring notes specific to Exercise 5

Reward learners who take demonstratives seriously as part of package integrity.

Penalize learners who:

- exempt graphics from structural discipline
- preserve exact-path fiction
- treat motive banners as harmless framing
- dismiss the detective's concern

### Fast score cues

`20-24`

Learner rebuilds the demonstrative around bounded sequence and explicitly guards against visual overclaim.

`14-19`

Learner spots the main visual problems but still leaves one or two major overclaims in place.

`8-13`

Learner trims wording but preserves most of the exact-path and motive structure.

`0-7`

Learner accepts the demonstrative as a normal advocacy tool.

## Instructor Debrief Pattern

For all legal scenarios, run debrief in this order:

1. ask what legal pressure felt strongest
2. ask what part of the package that pressure tried to overwrite
3. ask which anchors were strongest and what they did not prove
4. ask where grammar, memo language, or visuals added certainty
5. ask what residue remained necessary even in strong answers
6. ask what redesign signal should travel back upstream

This order prevents debrief from becoming hindsight rhetoric or courtroom-style point scoring.

## Common Grading Mistakes

### Rewarding clean prose over bounded prose

A learner who writes beautifully but silently erases residue should score lower than a learner who writes more awkwardly but preserves structural truth.

### Mistaking decisiveness for sufficiency

Legal learners are often socially rewarded for confident answer delivery. That confidence should not mask weak package reading.

### Penalizing reentry

If a learner triggers reentry or narrows a theory honestly, do not grade that as weakness merely because it slows the legal move down.

### Overrewarding legal vocabulary

A learner can say "probable cause," "charging posture," or "demonstrative discipline" while still reasoning in the old clean-story model.

Score the structure, not the professional fluency.

### Treating transfer dependence as normal

If a learner casually assumes someone can just call the prior prosecutor, the analyst, or the detective later, transfer discipline is weak.

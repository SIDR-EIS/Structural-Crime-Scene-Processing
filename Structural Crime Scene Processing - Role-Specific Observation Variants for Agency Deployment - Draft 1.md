# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Role-Specific Observation Variants for Agency Deployment

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document turns the live-observation layer into a deployment-ready adaptation system for different agency types.

It is written for:

- implementation owners
- pilot-site leaders
- patrol supervisors
- scene-unit supervisors
- investigative supervisors
- laboratory supervisors
- downstream legal supervisors
- audit leads
- training leads
- agencies adapting the method to local structure without rewriting it

Its job is to answer a specific question:

how should an agency tailor patrol, scene, detective, supervisory, laboratory, and downstream legal observation to its own structure, geography, staffing model, and reporting systems without breaking the core method.

---

## What This Document Is

This document is:

- an adaptation guide for local deployment
- a library of role-specific observation variants
- a control against agency drift disguised as customization
- a bridge between the observation companion and local rollout policy
- a way to preserve one method across different local operating environments

It is not:

- permission to simplify away the method
- a replacement for the main observation companion
- an invitation to make every agency invent its own theory
- a local politics document
- a request to add endless local exceptions

The point of a variant is not to produce local style.

The point is to preserve core structural visibility under different local conditions.

---

## Why Agency Variants Are Needed

The main observation companion defines what should be seen in live work.

That is necessary.

It is not always sufficient for local rollout.

Different agencies operate with different:

- staffing models
- call volumes
- response distances
- scene-specialist availability
- lab access models
- prosecutor integration patterns
- dispatch and records systems
- supervision layers

If those differences are ignored, one of two problems usually appears.

First, local leaders say the method is unrealistic because it does not acknowledge their operating environment.

Second, local leaders "adapt" the method by deleting the most important parts of it.

This document is designed to prevent both failures.

It gives agencies a lawful way to tailor observation while preserving one support standard across the project.

---

## Core Rule

Local variation may change:

- observation emphasis
- field names
- role combinations
- timing windows
- escalation routing
- evidence-source labels

Local variation may not change:

- the core behaviors being protected
- the difference between observation and explanation
- the need for owned residue
- the need for transferability
- the need to expose false stability
- the requirement that uncertainty remain explicit where it is still lawful

If a local variant weakens any of those, it is not a variant.

It is a regression.

---

## Fixed Core Versus Local Layer

Every local observation variant should be divided into two layers.

### Fixed core

The fixed core contains the non-negotiables:

- boundary behavior
- disturbance accounting
- anchor logic
- transferability
- residue visibility
- lawful boundedness
- ownership of unresolveds
- structural supervision
- protection against narrative inflation

### Local layer

The local layer contains things that may be adapted:

- whether patrol or deputies are primary first responders
- whether a scene technician is full-time, on-call, regional, or hybrid
- whether detectives are specialists or generalists
- whether the lab is local, state, regional, or outsourced
- whether prosecutors are embedded early or receive files later
- what logs, systems, or reporting tools exist locally
- what timing windows are realistic for observation and review

Every local form should visibly distinguish these two layers.

If they blend together, the fixed core will eventually get negotiated away.

## Local Adaptation Rules

Every agency-level observation variant in this project should follow nine rules.

### Rule 1: Preserve the observation target

The local form may rename a field, but it may not delete the behavior the field was designed to expose.

### Rule 2: Adapt role combinations explicitly

If one person locally performs several roles, the variant should state that explicitly instead of silently compressing the logic.

### Rule 3: Keep transfer logic intact

Even if a single person carries work longer in a small agency, the form should still ask whether the package can survive transfer.

### Rule 4: Protect low-resource honesty

Low-resource agencies should not be forced to pretend they can observe everything.

They should be required to mark what was not observable and why.

### Rule 5: Preserve severe warnings

Local forms should still flag:

- premature category closure
- hidden oral rescue
- unowned residue
- rhetorical overclaim
- confirmation-service pressure

### Rule 6: Add local systems only if they improve visibility

CAD, RMS, body camera, access control, jail, dispatch, camera, or facility systems may be added to local variants only if they improve structural observation.

### Rule 7: Limit optional additions

If the local variant grows too long, observers stop using it honestly.

### Rule 8: Route findings locally without changing the meaning

A county office, city department, campus safety unit, and transit agency may route review findings differently.

The meaning of the finding should not change.

### Rule 9: Recalibrate after local changes

Any local variant should be tested through observation, audit, and training review before it becomes standard.

## What Can Be Adapted

The following are usually legitimate local adaptations:

- agency title for the role
- local dispatch-system references
- local evidence-system references
- who receives the handoff first
- what major incident thresholds trigger full observation
- whether a regional or state partner is the lab-facing unit
- local legal-review point names
- whether watch commander, sergeant, lieutenant, or board is the first oversight layer

The following are usually not legitimate local adaptations:

- removing ownership of unresolveds
- replacing boundedness with confidence language
- deleting transferability because the agency is small
- removing interpretive-boundary checks because people know each other
- replacing structural questions with outcome questions
- allowing local culture to outrank evidence

## Agency Archetypes

Use these archetypes to choose a starting variant set.

### Archetype 1. Large municipal department

Common conditions:

- separate patrol, detectives, and scene units
- moderate to high call volume
- multiple supervisors
- access to more formal specialist layers

Variant pressure:

- handoff complexity
- hidden expert rescue
- presentation pressure from multiple layers

### Archetype 2. County sheriff or rural office

Common conditions:

- large geography
- long travel times
- patrol and investigation overlap
- delayed specialist arrival

Variant pressure:

- one person holding multiple functions
- longer uncertainty windows
- low-resource temptation to declare closure early

### Archetype 3. Small municipal generalist agency

Common conditions:

- limited staffing
- generalist patrol and detectives
- partial regional support
- low administrative depth

Variant pressure:

- role compression
- documentation shortcuts
- high dependence on a few experienced people

### Archetype 4. Regional task-force or major-case environment

Common conditions:

- mixed agencies
- rotating command
- many specialists
- variable terminology and doctrine history

Variant pressure:

- interface drift
- cross-agency ambiguity
- hidden translation work

### Archetype 5. Campus or institutional safety agency

Common conditions:

- nontraditional responder culture
- heavy access-control systems
- facility and student or employee context
- strong pressure toward internal narrative smoothing

Variant pressure:

- overreading community knowledge
- access-log distortion
- administrative pressure to normalize events

### Archetype 6. Transit, port, airport, or transportation agency

Common conditions:

- heavy camera and access systems
- rapid movement through bounded spaces
- complex responder overlaps
- strong operational tempo

Variant pressure:

- overconfidence in camera coverage
- quick scene turnover pressure
- path contamination through active infrastructure

### Archetype 7. Tribal, cross-jurisdictional, or sovereignty-sensitive environment

Common conditions:

- layered authority
- multiple legal and operational interfaces
- varying reporting rules
- cross-boundary sensitivity

Variant pressure:

- authority ambiguity
- handoff uncertainty
- interpretive and legal-interface drift

### Archetype 8. Agency with no local lab or no integrated legal-review capacity

Common conditions:

- external specialist dependence
- longer review loops
- fewer local checks

Variant pressure:

- weak submissions
- passive transfer assumptions
- low visibility into downstream effects

## Variant Build Workflow

Use this seven-step workflow when building a local role variant.

### Step 1: Choose the role and archetype

State:

- role
- agency archetype
- local operational constraints

### Step 2: Preserve the fixed core

List the non-negotiables that must remain in the local form.

### Step 3: Identify local pressure points

Examples:

- travel distance
- role compression
- body-camera overreliance
- supervisory span
- outside-lab delay
- prosecutor access delay

### Step 4: Add local fields

Add only the fields needed to expose those pressure points honestly.

### Step 5: Define local escalation path

State who receives:

- red indicators
- unresolved role-interface failures
- repeat drift signals

### Step 6: Pilot the variant

Run it on a small sample before wider use.

### Step 7: Reconcile with dashboard and audit layers

Make sure local data still feeds:

- observations
- audits
- dashboards
- corrective-action ledgers

If the local form cannot feed upward, it will become an isolated local paper habit.

## Part I. Patrol and First-Response Observation Variants

### 1. Patrol variant design question

For patrol, the local question is:

what can the first responder realistically preserve and communicate in this environment without pretending the scene is more stable, resourced, or bounded than it is.

### 2. Patrol fixed core

Every patrol variant must preserve:

- life safety with disturbance accounting
- physical, informational, authority, and interpretive boundaries
- observational radio language
- witness contamination awareness
- perishable-state awareness
- handoff structure

### 3. Large municipal patrol variant

Use this when:

- patrol units are numerous
- scene technicians and detectives often arrive separately
- perimeter control is possible but interpretive leakage is high

Emphasize:

- informational-boundary enforcement
- witness separation
- route-memory preservation
- radio discipline
- handoff clarity across multiple arriving units

Add local fields for:

- unit identifiers
- body-camera reference
- local CAD event number
- assigned perimeter sectors

Watch especially for:

- too many people knowing a theory too early
- officers briefing each other with certainty before anchors exist
- perimeter appearance masking meaning leakage

### 4. County sheriff or rural deputy patrol variant

Use this when:

- one deputy may hold the scene alone for an extended period
- response times are long
- specialist arrival is delayed

Emphasize:

- long-window disturbance accounting
- route and environment documentation
- preserved uncertainty over long intervals
- explicit marking of what could not yet be protected

Add local fields for:

- travel time to scene
- estimated time to specialist arrival
- weather or environmental degradation window
- unprotected zones due to staffing limits

Watch especially for:

- early closure caused by isolation
- scenes treated as resolved because nobody else is arriving soon
- quiet contamination caused by practical necessity but never documented

### 5. Small generalist patrol variant

Use this when:

- patrol may later become the primary investigator
- role boundaries are thin

Emphasize:

- separating current patrol observation from later investigative interpretation
- protecting against memory-based laundering
- recording early uncertainty before the same person later develops the case

Add local fields for:

- anticipated role continuity
- whether the same responder may conduct later follow-up
- items that require later independent review

Watch especially for:

- one person silently merging patrol and detective logic too early

### 6. Campus or institutional safety patrol variant

Use this when:

- the agency has heavy access controls and community familiarity
- the environment creates pressure to normalize unusual events

Emphasize:

- access-system anchors
- avoiding overreading community familiarity
- controlling internal rumor channels
- preserving the distinction between policy knowledge and scene evidence

Add local fields for:

- access badge or card system references
- resident assistant, staff, or building-manager contact controls
- institution-specific communication channels already active

Watch especially for:

- "we know who would do this" logic
- access logs treated as full causal truth
- interpretive leakage through internal messaging networks

### 7. Transit or transportation patrol variant

Use this when:

- the environment is fast-moving
- coverage systems are dense
- path contamination risk is high

Emphasize:

- approach path mapping
- movement restrictions around active infrastructure
- camera-coverage assumptions versus actual coverage
- preserving timing and access anchors before they cycle out

Add local fields for:

- platform, gate, terminal, vehicle, or track identifiers
- camera retention risk
- active-operations pressure level

Watch especially for:

- false comfort created by a large camera footprint
- rapid scene reopening before closure logic exists

### 8. Patrol local variant checklist template

Local patrol variants should include:

- role label
- response context
- first observable priorities
- local systems to preserve
- likely contamination points
- local handoff route
- local escalation path
- local non-observable fields

### 9. Patrol forbidden local shortcuts

Do not allow a local patrol variant to:

- delete interpretive-boundary checks
- replace disturbance accounting with "medical handled"
- replace handoff structure with informal verbal summary
- infer closure from low staffing or long distance

## Part II. Scene Technician Observation Variants

### 10. Scene technician variant design question

For scene technicians, the local question is:

how should observation adapt to the agency's technician model without losing anchor logic, graph logic, ranking logic, and transfer discipline.

### 11. Technician fixed core

Every technician variant must preserve:

- deliberate first movement
- anchor-first behavior
- graph formation
- ranked collection
- distinction-preserving packaging
- lab-facing transfer quality

### 12. Full-time CSI unit variant

Use this when:

- a dedicated scene unit exists
- technicians work frequent scenes

Emphasize:

- ranking discipline over ritual thoroughness
- transferability under high specialist volume
- graph honesty rather than polished documentation alone

Add local fields for:

- scene-unit case number
- technician team composition
- specialized equipment or processing sequence references

Watch especially for:

- professionalism masking ritual collection
- dense documentation masking weak anchor hierarchy

### 13. Hybrid patrol-CSI variant

Use this when:

- the same person may do patrol and scene work
- full-time technician availability is limited

Emphasize:

- explicit shift between responder and technician posture
- preservation of what the responder already disturbed
- later packaging that does not hide early contamination

Add local fields for:

- responder-to-technician role shift time
- self-created disturbance note
- secondary review trigger

Watch especially for:

- self-rescue through memory instead of documentation

### 14. Regional on-call technician variant

Use this when:

- technicians come from outside the immediate responding agency
- arrival delay is common

Emphasize:

- what the first agency preserved or failed to preserve
- late-arrival anchor salvage
- boundary refinement after delayed entry
- honest marking of already-lost distinctions

Add local fields for:

- arrival delay
- prior responder summary quality
- already-changed scene elements

Watch especially for:

- technician documentation implying greater scene freshness than actually existed

### 15. Major-case technician variant

Use this when:

- large or complex scenes involve multiple technicians
- many zones and subteams exist

Emphasize:

- cross-technician graph coherence
- shared anchor hierarchy
- consistent packaging distinctions
- subteam handoff quality

Add local fields for:

- zone ownership
- graph integration owner
- cross-zone unresolveds

Watch especially for:

- excellent local zone work with weak integrated package logic

### 16. Technician local variant checklist template

Local technician variants should include:

- arrival condition summary
- first movement record
- top anchors to preserve locally
- graph components required locally
- local packaging risks
- lab-transfer path
- secondary-review trigger

### 17. Technician forbidden local shortcuts

Do not allow a local technician variant to:

- delete anchor hierarchy
- collapse graph logic into photo count
- treat collection volume as the success metric
- omit what the lab needs to preserve distinctions

## Part III. Detective Observation Variants

### 18. Detective variant design question

For detectives, the local question is:

how should observation adapt to detective staffing and case-flow realities without allowing narrative gravity, witness laundering, or package fragility to reappear as "practical case handling."

### 19. Detective fixed core

Every detective variant must preserve:

- structural entry posture
- witness handling discipline
- live-hypothesis integrity
- bounded reconstruction
- deferral ownership
- reentry logic
- transferability

### 20. Dedicated detective-bureau variant

Use this when:

- detectives are specialized
- formal package review exists

Emphasize:

- hypothesis control
- reconstruction syntax
- cross-role interface with technicians and lab
- package maturity before legal handoff

Add local fields for:

- case-review meeting identifier
- supervisor review point
- investigative-assignment splits

Watch especially for:

- polished case summaries outrunning package reality

### 21. Generalist investigator variant

Use this when:

- one investigator manages many case types
- specialization is thin

Emphasize:

- resisting category-first reasoning
- using fixed question sets
- documenting eliminations and residue explicitly
- preserving structure despite workload pressure

Add local fields for:

- case-load pressure note
- outstanding external dependency
- abbreviated but required package fields

Watch especially for:

- heuristic case categorization replacing live structural review

### 22. Major-case or task-force detective variant

Use this when:

- multiple agencies and investigators are involved

Emphasize:

- one shared hypothesis ledger
- one shared residue ledger
- control of explanation density across briefings
- protection against agency-specific narrative habits

Add local fields for:

- contributing agency
- joint-briefing identifier
- cross-agency unresolved owner

Watch especially for:

- one agency's confident narrative becoming the task force's operating truth

### 23. Long-delay detective-entry variant

Use this when:

- detectives often receive scenes after significant elapsed time

Emphasize:

- what was not preserved
- what remains live despite delay
- lawful humility about missing anchors
- clean distinction between package weakness and affirmative inference

Add local fields for:

- detective arrival delay
- evidence lost before entry
- unresolveds caused by delay

Watch especially for:

- backfilling a clean story into an evidentiary gap created by time

### 24. Detective local variant checklist template

Local detective variants should include:

- entry posture check
- witness handling check
- live hypothesis check
- bounded reconstruction check
- deferral ownership check
- handoff-readiness check

### 25. Detective forbidden local shortcuts

Do not allow a local detective variant to:

- collapse hypotheses into one likely story
- treat witness confidence as equivalent to anchor strength
- remove residue because local prosecutors prefer clean files
- convert reentry into memory-dependent follow-up

## Part IX. How to Pilot a Local Variant

### 62. Sample before standardizing

Use the local variant on a small sample first:

- 3-5 observed scenes or reviews in a small agency
- 5-10 in a larger agency
- at least one cross-role handoff if possible

### 63. Compare the local form to the standard form

The pilot question is not:

- did staff like the local form more

The real question is:

- did the local form preserve the warning signal

### 64. Ask three review questions after pilot use

1. What did the local variant reveal that the standard form missed?
2. What did the local variant accidentally soften or hide?
3. What should return to the fixed core if local tailoring weakened it?

### 65. Run local calibration before full rollout

Before wider use, local instructors, supervisors, and audit leads should review:

- the variant
- sample completed forms
- what counts as green, amber, red, and gray locally
- what still escalates beyond local discretion

### 66. Feed the variant into the dashboard layer

The local variant should map explicitly to:

- command dashboard
- pilot operations dashboard
- training dashboard where relevant
- specialist dashboard where relevant
- drift and recovery dashboard

If no dashboard mapping exists, the local variant will become a local notebook rather than part of the operating system.

## Part X. Variant Review Cadence

### 67. Initial review

Review the local variant:

- within 30 days of first use

### 68. Second review

Review again:

- after the first meaningful audit packet touching that role

### 69. Ongoing review

After stabilization, review:

- quarterly
- after any major incident revealing a local blind spot
- after supervisory, staffing, or technology changes

### 70. Triggered review events

Review the local variant immediately if:

- red indicators repeat
- the variant no longer feeds dashboard or audit reporting cleanly
- a hidden expert becomes the actual carrier of the local adaptation
- people begin saying "this is how we do it here" without showing how it preserves the fixed core

## Part XI. Common Variant Failure Modes

### 71. Local realism as cover for deletion

This is the most common failure.

Someone says a field is unrealistic locally, but what they really mean is that the field reveals something uncomfortable.

### 72. Role compression without explicit marking

Small agencies often combine roles.

That is workable only if the form explicitly marks where one role ends and another begins.

### 73. Tool worship

Local variants sometimes overcenter the local system:

- CAD
- RMS
- body camera
- access system
- camera network

Those may help.

They are not the method.

### 74. Local culture replacing evidence

Statements like:

- "everyone knows him"
- "this building is always like that"
- "our campus works differently"
- "we can tell when this is nothing"

should appear on the observer's warning list, not as substitute anchors.

### 75. Dashboard breakage

If each agency variant names the same phenomenon differently, leadership dashboards become meaningless.

### 76. Hidden regional translation

Regional task forces often rely on one person to translate between local forms.

That person is a hidden expert unless the translation rules are documented.

### 77. Permanent provisionality

Some agencies pilot a local variant forever and never stabilize it.

That leaves the method in permanent optional status.

## Part XII. Recommended Rollout Sequence for Variants

### 78. Start with observation, not policy

Local variants should first be used to observe real work.

Do not start by rewriting policies on day one.

### 79. Start with the highest-friction role

Choose the first local variant by likely risk.

In many agencies that is:

- patrol
- detective
- prosecutor/legal review

depending on where the old model is strongest.

### 80. Add the next role by interface, not by prestige

After the first local variant, choose the next role that most affects the same handoff.

### 81. Link every variant to one audit packet

Each local role variant should be explicitly reviewed in at least one live implementation audit packet.

### 82. Link every variant to one dashboard section

Each local role variant should also map to one dashboard surface so it stays visible above the local level.

## Closing Note

The method should be adaptable.

It should not be negotiable in its essentials.

That is the purpose of a good local variant.

A strong local variant says:

- this is what our environment changes
- this is what it does not change
- this is what we need to watch harder because of who we are
- this is what we are forbidden to soften even if local culture prefers it

That is how local realism becomes a support for the method instead of an excuse to abandon it.

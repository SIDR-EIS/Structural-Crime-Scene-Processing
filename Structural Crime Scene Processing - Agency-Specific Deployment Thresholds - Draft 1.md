# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Agency-Specific Deployment Thresholds

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document turns the Structural Crime Scene Processing rollout stack into explicit deployment thresholds for the major local agency archetypes used across the project.

It is written for:

- command sponsors
- implementation owners
- audit leads
- pilot-site leaders
- training leads
- policy drafters
- supervisors and review-board members
- laboratory and downstream legal leaders participating in local rollout
- steering groups deciding whether deployment should proceed, hold, reset, or scale

Its job is to answer a specific question:

what evidence is enough for a given agency type to launch, continue, constrain, hold, reset, or scale Structural Crime Scene Processing without falling back into local comfort, local prestige, or local storytelling.

---

## What This Document Does

This document provides:

- archetype-specific deployment thresholds
- go / go-with-conditions / hold / reset logic
- stage thresholds for launch, supervised live use, handoff maturity, downstream legal use, and scale-up
- threshold rules tied to existing observations, signoffs, dashboards, and audits
- archetype-specific threshold posture for large municipal, rural or county, campus or institutional, and regional task-force environments
- cross-archetype reading rules so local agencies do not copy the wrong numbers for the wrong environment

It is not:

- a generic performance target document
- a substitute for audit packets
- a substitute for local policy review
- a promise that every agency should hit the same raw counts
- a public-relations checklist designed to make rollout look smoother than it is

The point of deployment thresholds is not to create another layer of paperwork.

The point is to stop leadership from confusing motion with readiness.

---

## Why Thresholds Are Needed

By this stage the project contains:

- doctrine
- role guides
- forms
- exercises
- scoring and signoff
- mixed-role certification
- handoff packet examples
- supervised live deployment records
- observation checklists
- audit packets
- dashboard templates and population guides
- local policy templates and completed local policy examples
- completed local variant-form and observation-record examples

That is enough to build a serious deployment stack.

It still leaves one recurring institutional failure unresolved.

Many agencies can now produce the documents and still ask:

- when are we actually ready to launch
- when is a pilot mature enough to widen
- when should live use stay bounded
- what does a real hold condition look like
- when does regression require reset rather than coaching
- how should a rural office or a campus agency read the same rollout evidence differently from a large city department

This document exists to answer those questions.

Without threshold rules, leadership usually drifts into one of five errors.

First, it treats document existence as launch readiness.

Second, it mistakes a few smooth scenes for cross-role maturity.

Third, it copies threshold counts from a different agency type and either overstates or understates readiness.

Fourth, it lets respected local performers substitute for structural reliability.

Fifth, it uses "we need more time" as a permanent holding pattern instead of naming what evidence is still missing.

Thresholds exist to keep deployment honest.

## Threshold Design Rule

Every threshold in this project should help leadership answer five questions:

- Is the fixed core present in practice, not just on paper?
- Is live use bounded tightly enough to learn without hiding drift?
- Are handoffs becoming structurally real rather than personality-dependent?
- Is downstream legal use staying bounded instead of inflating narrative certainty?
- Is scale happening because the system is stabilizing, not because pressure is rising?

If a threshold does not help answer those questions, it is probably a disguised productivity target rather than a deployment threshold.

---

## Threshold Status Codes

Every local threshold decision in this project should resolve to one of four status codes.

### `GO`

Use when the threshold has been met with no unresolved structural blocker inside the reviewed stage.

`GO` does not mean the institution is finished.

It means the institution may proceed to the next bounded stage without pretending the current stage is perfect.

### `GO WITH CONDITIONS`

Use when the threshold has been met strongly enough to proceed, but one or more named conditions must remain active.

Examples:

- supervised live use remains mandatory for one role
- downstream legal review is restricted to one trained unit
- scale is allowed only within one district or one team
- a hold-level metric is tolerated only because the relevant corrective action is already underway and tightly owned

`GO WITH CONDITIONS` is not a soft `GO`.

It is a controlled temporary state with named restrictions.

### `HOLD`

Use when the threshold has not been met, but the current deployment stage may remain in place while corrective action runs.

`HOLD` means:

- do not widen scope
- do not relax supervision
- do not claim maturity
- do not translate local fluency into institutional readiness

### `RESET`

Use when the deployment stage has become structurally unreliable enough that continuation would teach the wrong system.

`RESET` is required when:

- oral rescue is masking repeated failure
- unowned residue is being relabeled as local judgment
- live use is proceeding without bounded visibility
- cross-role transfer has become personality-dependent again
- downstream legal work is inflating certainty beyond the package

A reset is not punishment.

It is damage containment.

## Shared Fixed-Core Threshold Floor

No local archetype may waive the following fixed-core threshold floor.

1. The local policy stack must preserve the closure-contract logic, owned deferral logic, and no-silence-as-proof rule.
2. At least one role-specific completed field-form set must be in local use before unsupervised claims are made about field adoption.
3. Observation must cover live behavior, not only exercise behavior.
4. Mixed-role certification must exist before agencies claim cross-role maturity.
5. Handoff evaluation must inspect packet quality, not just interpersonal smoothness.
6. Downstream legal use must remain bounded by package reality rather than rhetorical confidence.
7. Dashboard indicators must be sourced from named evidence inputs rather than impression.
8. Audit packets must be capable of producing `HOLD` or `RESET` outcomes in practice.
9. Local variants may add prompts, routing, and role emphasis, but may not delete the fixed core without documented approval.
10. Scale-up decisions must be evidence-based and stage-based.

If a local deployment cannot satisfy those ten conditions, no archetype-specific threshold may override that deficiency.

## Threshold Layers Used in This Document

Every archetype in this document is evaluated across six threshold layers.

### Layer 1: Launch readiness

Can the agency begin bounded deployment at all?

### Layer 2: Supervised live use

Can the agency move from exercises into observed real work without pretending that early live fluency equals maturity?

### Layer 3: Handoff maturity

Can the agency transfer work across roles without oral rescue and hidden expertise carrying the package?

### Layer 4: Downstream legal and review maturity

Can the agency hand the work into supervisory, warrant, charging, or trial-preparation review without narrative inflation outrunning the record?

### Layer 5: Drift and corrective-action posture

Can the agency detect and contain regression before it becomes the new local normal?

### Layer 6: Scale-up readiness

Can the agency widen scope without reproducing the old system at larger scale?

---

## How to Use These Thresholds

Use these threshold sets in the following order.

1. Identify the local archetype that most closely matches the pilot environment.
2. Apply the shared fixed-core floor first.
3. Apply the archetype-specific thresholds for the current stage.
4. Name the disposition as `GO`, `GO WITH CONDITIONS`, `HOLD`, or `RESET`.
5. Record the evidence source for every threshold judgment.
6. Record the condition, hold item, or reset trigger exactly as written rather than replacing it with narrative comfort language.

Do not average threshold results into one soft impression.

If a stage contains one true reset trigger, the stage is not rescued by six strong-looking indicators elsewhere.

## Archetype A. Large Municipal Department

### Archetype summary

This archetype assumes:

- moderate to high call volume
- dedicated or semi-dedicated scene personnel
- multiple supervisory layers
- recurring downstream legal review
- enough personnel that local inconsistency can hide behind throughput and specialization

Its main risk is not scarcity.

Its main risk is false stability produced by volume, specialization, and reputation.

### Threshold posture

A large municipal department should use higher cross-role and observation thresholds than smaller environments because it has more places for drift to hide.

It should not use high thresholds as an excuse for endless pilot delay.

The correct posture is: harder proof of cross-role reliability, not endless study.

### Launch readiness thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core policies | adoption directive, pilot order, observation rule, audit rule, and dashboard rule all approved | one support policy still pending but interim command order is active and bounded | multiple core policy gaps remain | local policy deletes fixed-core elements or treats them as optional |
| Role preparation | patrol, scene, detective, supervisor, and one downstream legal unit all have initial signoff coverage | one role enters under explicit supervised-live restriction | two or more core roles are unready | live launch begins with no signoff path for a core role |
| Forms and variants | local field-form variants exist for all pilot roles and preserve the fixed core | one minor variant set incomplete but default forms are mandated | pilot roles are improvising local forms | local variants delete closure, deferral, or transfer structure |
| Observation readiness | observers assigned for all pilot shifts and handoff windows | one observation gap covered by named on-call backup | observation coverage exists only for daytime or selected showcase events | live launch begins without observation capacity |
| Dashboard readiness | baseline indicators can be populated for four consecutive weeks of pilot-prep activity | population logic works for command and pilot dashboards only | reporting exists but source-to-indicator logic is unstable | dashboard indicators are based on impressions or manual storytelling |
| Audit readiness | launch audit packet completed with no unresolved reset items | one hold item remains with owner and due date | launch audit not completed | launch proceeds without audit or despite unresolved reset findings |

### Supervised live use thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Live scene sample | at least 12 supervised live scenes across at least two shifts and two supervisors | 8 to 11 scenes with strong role spread but one shift underrepresented | fewer than 8 scenes or same small team carrying all evidence | live claims are made from anecdotal standout scenes only |
| Role spread | patrol, scene, detective, and supervisor behavior all directly observed in the live sample | one role remains bounded to shadow status | one core role has no live observation record | a core role is treated as exempt from live observation |
| Oral rescue rate | oral rescue is rare, named, and trending downward | oral rescue persists in one role but is openly logged | oral rescue appears repeatedly without corrective action | oral rescue is masking structural failure and not being recorded |
| Closure discipline | most live packages show explicit closure logic and owned residue | closure logic holds but deferral ownership is inconsistent | closure contracts are partial or missing in too many live files | live scenes are being closed administratively with no structural closure logic |
| Supervision quality | supervisors ask state, transition, and residue questions consistently | one supervisory team needs tighter coaching | supervisors revert to comfort or confidence review repeatedly | supervisors validate narrative smoothness instead of structure |

### Handoff maturity thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patrol to scene | handoff packets are usable without oral reconstruction in most observed cases | one district still needs prompt reinforcement | scene staff still depend on verbal rescue too often | patrol handoff is structurally absent and hidden by senior intervention |
| Scene to detective | detectives can read the package without re-narrating the scene from scratch | one detective squad remains under extra review | detectives still rebuild meaning from oral briefings | scene graph and collection logic are not surviving transfer |
| Detective to lab | submissions specify discriminating purpose, not generic wish lists | some requests still need supervisory rewrite | labs are receiving broad undirected requests | lab work is being driven by narrative hope rather than bounded discrimination |
| Detective to prosecutor | charging or warrant review is package-bounded and residue-aware | one legal team remains restricted to coached review | legal intake still inflates beyond the package | downstream legal work is outrunning the record repeatedly |

### Drift and corrective-action thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Observation drift | no major drift pattern across the last four weeks of observed work | one named drift pattern with active corrective action | repeated drift without improved ownership | drift is normalized as local style |
| Dashboard signals | hold and warning indicators are stable or improving | one dashboard remains noisy but interpretable | dashboard signals contradict field reality and are unexplained | local reporting is hiding obvious regression |
| Remediation closure | hold items close on schedule with evidence | one overdue item with strong evidence of active correction | several overdue hold items remain open | corrective action exists only as language with no evidence trail |

### Scale-up thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope expansion | one additional district or specialist unit may be added after one stable quarter | scale allowed only to one adjacent unit with restricted supervision | pilot remains too personality-dependent to widen | scale is being pursued to meet command pressure rather than readiness |
| Training capacity | instructors and observers can absorb the next scope step | scale allowed only if outside instructor support is borrowed | training demand exceeds local trainer capacity | scale would eliminate observation or audit discipline |
| Legal and lab stability | downstream review and lab routing remain bounded after pilot pressure | one specialist area remains protected from expansion | downstream units are not yet ready for higher volume | scale would force narrative shortcuts into lab or legal review |

### Why these thresholds fit this archetype

Large municipal agencies can absorb weak structure for longer because volume and specialization disguise it.

That is why this threshold set emphasizes:

- stronger cross-role spread
- stronger observation coverage
- stronger dashboard validity
- explicit protection against oral rescue and specialist prestige

The numbers are higher because the concealment capacity is higher.

## Archetype B. County Sheriff or Rural Office

### Archetype summary

This archetype assumes:

- lower call volume
- fewer dedicated specialists or more blended roles
- greater dependence on cross-training
- longer travel times and occasional regional laboratory reliance
- a higher risk that one respected generalist becomes the hidden carrier of the method

Its main risk is under-structured blending of roles.

### Threshold posture

A rural or county agency should not copy the raw counts from a large municipal department.

Its thresholds should be lower in volume but tighter in chain integrity.

The question is not whether it can generate 12 or 20 observations quickly.

The question is whether the same few people are quietly doing all the thinking for everyone else.

### Launch readiness thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core policies | local adoption order, observation rule, and corrective-action routing are active | one support policy remains provisional but command direction is explicit | policy remains verbal or informal | launch begins with no written fixed-core preservation |
| Cross-trained role readiness | blended patrol-investigative or patrol-scene roles have explicit bounded forms and signoff paths | one blended role enters only under shadowed use | blended roles are assumed competent without structured signoff | role blending is used to waive structure |
| Observation coverage | at least 6 live observed events across more than one supervisor or reviewer | 4 to 5 observed events with high-quality records and restricted scope | too few observed events to distinguish luck from repeatability | live use begins with no observation record at all |
| External dependencies | laboratory and prosecutor partners know the package format and bounded expectations | one external partner remains in coached introduction mode | external partners have not been oriented | local agency launches while external partners still operate entirely on old assumptions |

### Supervised live use thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Live sample | at least 6 supervised live events with more than one call type or event class | 4 to 5 live events with narrow event spread | all evidence comes from one event type or one strong performer | live use claims are built on one memorable case |
| Generalist dependence | no one person is required to orally rescue most packages | one senior generalist is still overused but logged and being offloaded | same person is repeatedly carrying the package | the method collapses when one person is absent |
| Handoff integrity | blended roles still produce visible handoff structure even when personnel overlap | handoff structure is compressed but still explicit | overlapping personnel eliminate documented handoff logic | informal continuity replaces actual transfer records |
| Residue ownership | deferrals and unknowns are explicitly owned despite small staffing | ownership works but due dates need improvement | residue is left to "we'll remember" | residue disappears into local memory |

### Handoff and downstream review thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sheriff or investigator to lab | submissions are discriminating and bounded despite low volume | one submission stream still needs supervisory rewrite | low volume is being used to justify generic submissions | labs receive broad requests with no structural question |
| Local investigator to prosecutor | charging or warrant review stays package-bounded and residue-aware | one prosecutor remains in coached review mode | downstream legal review depends on verbal framing | prosecutor confidence is outrunning the file repeatedly |
| Mutual-aid or regional handoff | outside agencies can read the package without local translation ritual | one partner needs a translation cover note | outside handoffs are still personality-dependent | mutual-aid transfer fails unless a local carrier is physically present |

### Drift and scale thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drift visibility | observation and dashboard evidence still detect regression despite low event volume | one indicator remains sparse but interpretable | sparse volume is being used as an excuse not to measure | no one can tell whether drift is occurring |
| Local expansion | method may widen to all eligible investigators or patrol shifts after one stable review cycle | expansion allowed only with continued monthly observation | small staff means scale would remove all supervision | expansion would make the method depend entirely on memory |
| Regional reliance | external partner dependencies are named and stable | one dependency remains fragile but visible | regional dependence is inconsistent and untracked | the local method survives only when outside partners rescue it verbally |

### Why these thresholds fit this archetype

Rural and county environments need lower raw counts because volume is lower.

They need stricter anti-carrier logic because the same few people often carry everything.

That is why the threshold set emphasizes:

- anti-hero dependence
- bounded blended-role use
- explicit residue ownership
- structurally visible transfer even when the same people wear multiple hats

## Archetype C. Campus or Institutional Safety Agency

### Archetype summary

This archetype assumes:

- a narrower event scope than a full municipal department
- frequent coordination with outside police or prosecutors
- strong internal administrative pressure to classify quickly
- a risk of mistaking procedural neatness for structural readiness
- a high chance that downstream transfer to external agencies becomes the real stress point

Its main risk is premature classification and overstatement inside a constrained environment.

### Threshold posture

Campus or institutional agencies should use thresholds that strongly weight:

- boundary discipline
- documentation quality
- transfer readiness to outside agencies
- bounded language in legal or administrative review

They should not pretend to be miniature municipal departments.

They should prove they can preserve diagnosability and transfer integrity inside their actual scope.

### Launch readiness thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope definition | local policy clearly names which event classes enter the new method and which transfer immediately | one edge case remains under temporary command memo | scope is vague or left to shift judgment | local rollout claims universal readiness with no clear scope boundary |
| Boundary and classification training | patrol or safety staff show explicit early-boundary and non-premature-classification discipline | one watch team enters under heavy coaching | early classification habits remain strong | incidents are being labeled before anchor review |
| External transfer partner orientation | host police, lab, or prosecutor partner has been briefed on package format and bounded expectations | partner orientation is pending but a bridge memo exists | local team launches without partner orientation | external transfer remains wholly oral and personality-dependent |
| Observation coverage | at least 5 observed live or live-equivalent events including one transfer out | 4 observed events with one more scheduled | no transfer-out event has been observed | live use claims are made without any real transfer event review |

### Supervised live use thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Live sample quality | live sample includes both internal-only and external-transfer events where possible | internal-only sample strong but transfer sample still small | sample is too narrow to test transfer logic | campus team claims maturity before any real external transfer is tested |
| Documentation discipline | forms and packages remain bounded, concise, and structurally explicit | one team needs tighter residue ownership | documentation is neat but vague | administrative formatting is masking missing structure |
| Command pressure handling | local leaders tolerate bounded uncertainty without demanding premature certainty | one leader needs coaching but accepts the constraint | command repeatedly pushes for categorical closure | local pressure is forcing narrative inflation into the package |

### Handoff and review thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Internal to external handoff | external partner can read the record without a translation performance | one partner still needs a structured cover note | external partner relies on oral rescue | the record cannot survive handoff without local narration |
| Administrative review | internal review uses bounded language and does not outrun the package | one review body still mixes conduct and criminal language | internal review repeatedly overstates what the package can support | administrative certainty is rewriting investigatory uncertainty |
| Warrant or charge referral | referrals are narrow, evidence-bounded, and residue-aware | one referral stream is still co-signed by trained outside counsel | referrals are broad or premature | referral documents routinely overclaim beyond the record |

### Drift and scale thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer drift | external partners report the package is readable and bounded | one partner flags repeated clarification needs | transfer clarity is deteriorating | the agency is reverting to oral translation as the norm |
| Local expansion | method may widen to additional campus units or event types only after one stable external-transfer cycle | expansion remains bounded to current scope with added coaching | pressure to widen is outpacing evidence | scope widens before transfer discipline is stable |
| External credibility | outside partners continue to treat the package as usable structure | credibility is mixed but improving | outside partners bypass the package and ask for verbal rescue | the local record has lost downstream credibility |

### Why these thresholds fit this archetype

Campus and institutional agencies do not need municipal-scale counts.

They need strong proof that the record survives classification pressure and transfer outward.

That is why the threshold set emphasizes:

- explicit scope definition
- transfer-out testing
- bounded administrative language
- resistance to premature certainty

## Archetype D. Regional Task-Force Environment

### Archetype summary

This archetype assumes:

- multi-agency participation
- inconsistent legacy procedures entering the same scene or case stream
- shifting authority lines
- cross-jurisdiction handoffs
- a high risk that every partner says it supports the method while still operating a different version of it

Its main risk is false alignment.

### Threshold posture

A task-force environment should use the strictest thresholds for common language, common packet structure, and authority clarity.

It should not treat partner goodwill as interoperability.

The correct question is whether the record survives a change in agency, not whether the meeting sounded collaborative.

### Launch readiness thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Common rule set | participating agencies sign a fixed-core adoption memorandum or equivalent | one partner signs a provisional bridge agreement with bounded scope | partners are proceeding on verbal understanding | task force launches with no shared fixed-core agreement |
| Common forms and routing | shared handoff packet, closure logic, and residue routing are adopted for task-force cases | one partner uses a local supplement but not a local substitute | partners are still using incompatible packet structures | common work is being translated manually case by case |
| Observation and audit authority | observers and auditors have cross-agency access to the relevant work | one partner requires escorted or delayed access but process is formalized | observers cannot see the full chain | task-force claims rollout while each agency hides its own segment |
| Mixed-role certification | cross-agency pairings have been certified, not just intra-agency pairings | one partner enters under restricted joint use | partner agencies have not demonstrated shared transfer competence | joint work begins with no cross-agency certification path |

### Supervised live use thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-agency live sample | at least 6 supervised live or live-equivalent events involving more than one agency | 4 to 5 events with one more scheduled and bounded | all evidence comes from one partner or one command lane | interoperability claims are made with no real cross-agency live sample |
| Authority clarity | role ownership and deferral ownership remain explicit despite multi-agency involvement | one authority seam still needs bridge notes | ownership becomes muddy at transition points | ambiguity about authority is being hidden by personality or rank |
| Translation burden | partner agencies can read the same packet language with minimal gloss | one partner still needs a reference sheet | translation burden remains high | the method exists only as bilingual oral rescue across agencies |

### Handoff, review, and scale thresholds

| Category | `GO` threshold | `GO WITH CONDITIONS` threshold | `HOLD` trigger | `RESET` trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-jurisdiction handoff | package survives movement from one agency to another without loss of structure | one jurisdiction still requires coached transition | handoff success depends on known individuals | packet collapse occurs when personnel rotate |
| Lab and prosecutor routing | joint cases use bounded routing logic that all partners understand | one partner path remains restricted | routing is inconsistent or locally reinterpreted | joint legal or lab review is being rebuilt from oral summary |
| Scale beyond pilot lanes | additional partner agencies or case classes may be added only after one stable audit cycle | one new lane may be added under heavy supervision | current partners are not yet structurally aligned | scale is being driven by politics, funding, or optics rather than evidence |
| Drift containment | drift is visible by partner, role, and junction point | one partner remains borderline but transparent | drift exists but is being blurred into team language | the task force cannot tell which partner is regressing |

### Why these thresholds fit this archetype

Regional task forces are where reforms most often become symbolic.

Every partner adopts the vocabulary.

Few adopt the same operating system.

That is why this threshold set emphasizes:

- shared rule sets
- cross-agency packet survival
- authority clarity at seams
- explicit rejection of goodwill as proof of interoperability

## Cross-Archetype Reading Rules

Use the following reading rules before copying a threshold from one archetype into another.

1. Higher volume does not automatically mean stronger readiness. It usually means more places to hide drift.
2. Lower volume does not justify low structure. It requires tighter anti-carrier safeguards.
3. Narrower institutional scope does not reduce the need for transfer discipline. It often increases it.
4. Multi-agency participation should raise interoperability thresholds, not lower them for convenience.
5. If a local team says, "that threshold is too hard for our environment," ask whether the threshold is actually exposing a dependency they do not want to name.

## Common Threshold Errors

### Error 1: Treating raw counts as universal

A rural office should not be failed for not producing municipal volume.

A large municipal department should not be excused because it can cite high activity.

### Error 2: Using `GO WITH CONDITIONS` as a hiding place

Conditions must be:

- explicit
- time-bounded
- owned
- auditable

If conditions are vague, the agency is on `HOLD`, not `GO WITH CONDITIONS`.

### Error 3: Calling a strong performer a strong system

If one person repeatedly rescues the package, the threshold is not met.

### Error 4: Confusing familiarity with interoperability

If external or partner agencies still need verbal translation, the threshold is not met.

### Error 5: Confusing survival with scale readiness

A pilot can survive while still being too fragile to widen.

That is why scale thresholds are separate.

### Error 6: Letting downstream legal smoothness overrule upstream weakness

If charging or warrant work looks clean only because prosecutors are silently repairing the record, the threshold is not met.

### Error 7: Treating dashboard neatness as evidence

If the reporting surface is tidy but the observation and audit layers disagree, the threshold is not met.

## Recommended Decision Sequence

When an agency uses this document, the decision sequence should be:

1. identify archetype
2. test fixed-core floor
3. test current stage threshold table
4. identify blockers
5. assign `GO`, `GO WITH CONDITIONS`, `HOLD`, or `RESET`
6. route corrective action through the relevant audit and policy channels
7. record when the threshold will be re-read

Never jump from local enthusiasm to scale.

The threshold layer exists to slow that reflex down.

---

## Minimum Evidence Record for Any Threshold Decision

No threshold decision should be recorded without at least the following:

- the archetype being used
- the stage being evaluated
- the threshold categories reviewed
- the evidence source for each category
- the disposition assigned
- any conditions or hold items
- the responsible owner
- the re-review date

If those eight items are missing, the decision is not a threshold decision.

It is a meeting impression.

## Closing Note

Most institutional rollouts fail at the moment when leadership has to decide whether evidence is truly strong enough to proceed.

That is where comfort, prestige, urgency, and fatigue all try to rename themselves as readiness.

Agency-specific deployment thresholds exist so the organization has one last structural defense against that mistake.

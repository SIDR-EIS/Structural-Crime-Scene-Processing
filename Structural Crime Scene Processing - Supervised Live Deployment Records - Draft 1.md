# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Supervised Live Deployment Records

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document adds completed supervised live deployment records for the transition from mixed-role drills into actual observed field or review work.

The mixed-role certification companion defines the bridge logic.

The live-observation companion defines what should be seen and recorded during live scenes and live review meetings.

The live implementation audit packets define how leadership should judge the pilot as a system.

This companion shows what the missing middle layer looks like.

It shows what a completed supervised live deployment record should look like when a learner or team has crossed out of drill-only work, entered bounded live use, and must now be judged on real transfer behavior rather than classroom performance alone.

Its purpose is to make the live-bridge layer concrete enough for trainers, supervisors, pilot leads, and auditors to use without inventing local logic from scratch.

---

## What This Document Does

This companion provides completed supervised live deployment record packages for:

- a large municipal department using supervised live observation only
- a county sheriff or rural office using bounded supervised live role use
- a campus or institutional safety agency using supervised live review observation
- a regional task-force environment using reentry-based supervised live observation and restricted live role use

Each package includes:

- a short deployment context note
- a completed local role-readiness bridge sheet
- a completed master live observation record
- a completed rapid field or rapid review-room strip as appropriate
- a completed post-event deployment disposition note
- a short explanation of why the record is structurally sound

These are not blank templates.

They are completed examples of how the bridge-to-live phase should actually be documented.

## How to Use These Records

Use these records in four ways:

1. as pre-brief models before learners enter supervised live observation or supervised live role use
2. as debrief comparison artifacts after a live deployment event has been observed
3. as calibration references when supervisors and trainers disagree about whether a live event proved readiness or exposed hidden repair
4. as implementation references when building local pilot record kits and audit evidence bundles

Do not treat these as scripts.

Treat them as examples of what must be documented when live deployment is real rather than symbolic.

---

## Record Conventions Used Here

Each package is written as a bounded live-use record, not as a narrative success story.

If the learner stayed inside scope, the record says so directly.

If the learner drifted and needed correction, the correction is visible.

If the event only proved readiness for observation and not for live role use, the record says that too.

The goal is not to reward confidence.

The goal is to show whether live exposure increased trust in the structure or revealed hidden repair work.

## Cross-Package Reading Notes

These packages show three different live truths.

First, supervised live observation is not a consolation prize.

It is where the institution learns whether classroom corrections survive contact with reality.

Second, bounded supervised live role use is not the same thing as generalized readiness.

A team may be safe under named conditions and still unsafe outside them.

Third, the most useful live deployment record is not the one that sounds most successful.

It is the one that most clearly shows whether the next expansion step is actually justified.

## Common Misreadings

### Misreading 1: A clean live observation should immediately convert into live role use

Not necessarily.

Observation proves what happened under one set of live conditions.

It does not erase all remaining known fragilities.

### Misreading 2: If a supervisor intervened, the live event failed

No.

An allowed, visible structural intervention can be evidence that the live-use boundary worked as designed.

Hidden repair is the failure, not visible governance.

### Misreading 3: A bounded live-use pass means the role is now generally trusted

No.

Bounded live use means trusted under the named scope and conditions only.

### Misreading 4: Review-room live deployment records matter less than field-scene records

They matter just as much.

Many systems fail more quietly in review rooms than they do at scenes.

### Misreading 5: These records replace audit packets

They do not.

They become evidence inputs for audit packets.

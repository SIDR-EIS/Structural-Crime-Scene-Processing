# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Role-Specific Exercise Variants - Pass / Remediate Signoff Forms

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document adds the formal certification layer for the role-specific exercise-variant track.

It exists so a learner's movement from role drill to conditional role use, supervised role use, remediation, or restriction is documented explicitly rather than left to instructor comfort.

It also exists so role-specific readiness is not confused with general intelligence, verbal fluency, seniority, or optimism.

These forms are designed to sit on top of the existing role-specific exercise, scoring, and instructor-threshold companions.

They do not replace scoring.

They convert scoring, observed failure patterns, and transfer-safety judgment into a formal signoff decision.

---

## What This Document Does

This companion provides:

- a shared signoff grammar for all role-specific drill tracks
- explicit pass, conditional-pass, remediation, deferral, and revocation status codes
- written forms for individual and mixed-role signoff decisions
- role-specific signoff sheets for patrol, scene technicians, detectives, laboratory personnel, supervisors and review boards, and prosecutors or downstream legal teams
- formal remediation intake and remediation-completion records
- timed-drill signoff strips for high-volume academy use
- cross-role final clearance tools for learners moving from isolated role drills into mixed-role or supervised live-use tracks
- a narrative template so signoff decisions describe structure, not personality

This document should prevent several common failures:

- passing someone because they sound persuasive even when their handoff is unsafe
- using raw score totals without reading severe structural flags
- issuing a vague "good enough" clearance with no defined scope
- hiding unresolved weakness behind informal coaching language
- letting conditional clearance function as an undeclared full pass
- allowing signoff decisions to drift across instructors or cohorts

## Why Role-Specific Signoff Forms Are Needed

Role-specific drills answer a different question than general exercises.

They do not ask whether a learner can talk coherently about the method.

They ask whether a learner can carry the method inside the real pressures, blind spots, and boundary temptations of a specific institutional role.

That means signoff must also become role-specific.

A patrol learner may be strong in early-scene containment but weak in handoff grammar.

A scene technician may be strong in anchor capture but weak in ranked collection rationale.

A detective may be strong in witness handling but weak in bounded reconstruction.

A laboratory learner may be technically competent but still convert a discriminating request into a generic service response.

A supervisor may speak doctrine fluently while still rewarding false stability.

A prosecutor may read the package intelligently while still inflating certainty downstream.

Without formal signoff forms, those differences are often flattened into one vague decision.

That is exactly how institutions claim to adopt a method while quietly certifying legacy reflexes.

---

## When to Use These Forms

Use these forms when any of the following decisions must be made:

- completion of a role-specific drill block
- completion of a role-specific timed drill
- decision about whether a learner may proceed to the next role-specific drill tier
- decision about whether a learner may enter mixed-role drills
- decision about whether a learner may participate in supervised live observation or supervised live role use
- decision about whether a previous conditional pass should be upgraded, extended, or revoked
- decision about whether remediation has actually corrected the failure pattern that triggered it

Do not use these forms as casual feedback notes.

Use them only when the decision changes the learner's operational status, training pathway, or allowed scope of use.

## Signoff Principles

### Principle 1: Sign off role-bounded safety, not generic fluency

The learner is being cleared for performance inside a role boundary.

The question is not whether the learner is impressive overall.

The question is whether the learner can execute the role without making the next role weaker.

### Principle 2: Severe overclaim defeats simple passing

A learner who hides residue, erases uncertainty, or inflates certainty beyond the record should not receive a simple pass even if other work is strong.

### Principle 3: Transfer safety outranks polish

A rough but honest handoff is safer than a polished but misleading handoff.

If the next role would inherit confusion, signoff should be limited or denied.

### Principle 4: Conditional signoff must actually narrow scope

A conditional pass is not a courtesy pass.

It must specify what the learner may do, what the learner may not do, and what review trigger ends the condition.

### Principle 5: Residue honesty is a strength, not a weakness

Learners should not be punished for accurately naming unresolved state, lawful ambiguity, or remaining structural risk.

### Principle 6: Oral rescue is evidence of signoff weakness

If the learner's work only becomes safe after the instructor explains what the learner meant, the written output is not yet carrying the role safely.

### Principle 7: Mixed-role readiness is different from individual readiness

A learner may be safe inside a role drill but not yet safe in a mixed-role environment where compression, ambiguity, and handoff load are higher.

### Principle 8: Signoff can be revoked

Role readiness is not permanent.

If later drills, live observation, or mixed-role work reveal hidden weakness, prior clearance may be restricted or withdrawn.

## Signoff Status Codes

### Clear Pass

The learner met the role-specific threshold with no severe structural blockers and can be cleared for the stated scope.

### Pass with Conditions

The learner is safe for a limited scope but still carries a named weakness that requires supervision, restricted tasking, or a scheduled review.

### Remediate and Re-Run

The learner's current work is not safe enough for the intended scope, but the failure pattern appears specific and correctable through targeted remediation.

### Not Yet Ready for Independent Role Use

The learner is not ready for the scope being considered, and the weakness is broad enough that simple targeted remediation is not sufficient by itself.

### Deferred Signoff

Signoff cannot be completed yet because evidence is missing, the drill sequence is incomplete, or another required decision point has not occurred.

### Revoked Pending Review

A previously issued signoff is being restricted or withdrawn because new evidence shows hidden weakness, unsafe transfer, or serious drift.

---

## Severe Override Triggers

Any of the following should override a raw score total and force at least conditional signoff, remediation, or denial depending on severity:

- hidden residue presented as closure
- transfer language that would mislead the next role about certainty, ownership, or sequence
- collapse into role escape language such as "someone else will figure that out"
- direct boundary violation inside the role's lawful scope
- oral rescue required for the work to make operational sense
- omission of a major known ambiguity when that omission makes downstream work unsafe
- conversion of a discriminating task into a generic ritual task
- false stability language that implies the case or scene is more settled than the record supports
- role output that creates contamination, distortion, or undocumented narrowing pressure on the next role
- repeated failure of the same type after prior remediation

A severe override trigger does not always require permanent restriction.

It does require that the signoff rationale name the problem explicitly and define the next control step.

## Evidence Required Before Signoff

A signoff packet should normally include:

- the learner's drill output or outputs
- the scored instructor sheet
- any addendum or pressure-comparison sheet used in evaluation
- any timed-drill scorecard if time pressure was part of the assessment
- relevant remediation notes from earlier attempts
- role-specific observation notes if the learner was observed in a supervised live or quasi-live setting
- any mixed-role note if the clearance decision depends on handoff safety rather than isolated role work alone

Do not issue role clearance from memory.

If the written packet is too thin to defend the decision later, the signoff packet is incomplete.

---

## Standard Signoff Workflow

### Stage 1: Define the scope being considered

Specify whether the decision concerns classroom progression, independent role drill use, mixed-role drill entry, supervised live observation, supervised live use, or another bounded scope.

### Stage 2: Confirm the evidence packet

Verify which drill outputs, score sheets, remediation records, and observations are being reviewed.

### Stage 3: Check threshold status and severe flags

Read the instructor-threshold decision and then confirm whether any override trigger changes the status band.

### Stage 4: Determine the signoff band

Choose Clear Pass, Pass with Conditions, Remediate and Re-Run, Not Yet Ready, Deferred, or Revoked.

### Stage 5: Define the exact scope of clearance or restriction

Do not use broad language such as "ready" or "not ready" without saying ready for what.

### Stage 6: Record conditions, blockers, or remediation path

The next action must be concrete.

### Stage 7: Obtain the appropriate signatures

The signer should match the seriousness of the decision and the scope being granted.

### Stage 8: Set the next review point

Conditional passes, deferrals, and revocations must always define a review trigger or date.

## Scope Categories for Role-Specific Signoff

Use one or more of these scope labels on the signoff packet.

### Classroom Progression Only

The learner may proceed to the next role drill inside the classroom sequence.

### Independent Role Drill Use

The learner may complete assigned role drills without immediate instructor rescue, though routine review still applies.

### Timed Drill Clearance

The learner may participate in timed or compressed role drills where pressure resistance is part of the evaluation.

### Mixed-Role Drill Entry

The learner may join multi-role exercises where handoff integrity matters across participants.

### Supervised Live Observation Readiness

The learner may observe or shadow live work in the role with explicit supervision and debrief.

### Supervised Live Role Use

The learner may perform bounded live tasks in the role under formal supervision.

### Restricted or Conditional Scope

The learner may perform only the named subset of tasks while conditions remain active.

### Independent role drill clearance

- lead instructor
- role-track coordinator or role lead

### Mixed-role drill entry clearance

- lead instructor
- mixed-role evaluator or role lead

### Supervised live observation or supervised live-use clearance

- lead instructor
- receiving role lead
- training supervisor when required locally

### Revocation or major restriction

- role lead or training supervisor
- secondary evaluator when possible

## Common Signoff Failure Modes

### Letting a score total replace structural reading

If the evaluator never reads the actual failure pattern, signoff becomes arithmetic rather than judgment.

### Treating a conditional pass as a courtesy full pass

If scope is not narrowed explicitly, the condition does not exist in practice.

### Signing off generic intelligence instead of role performance

A bright learner can still be unsafe inside a role boundary.

### Hiding weak handoff under polished language

Transfer safety must be judged from the packet, not from how confident the learner sounds.

### Refusing to revoke fragile clearances

If later evidence shows the original signoff was too generous, keeping the clearance for political comfort defeats the entire certification system.

### Confusing familiarity with readiness

Repeated exposure to the scenarios is not the same as stable role performance under pressure.

### Using signoff to avoid remediation conversations

If the learner clearly needs another loop, the form should say so directly.

## Recommended Use Sequence

Use this signoff companion in the following order:

1. Run the role-specific drill or drill set.
2. Score the output using the role-specific scoring companion.
3. Apply the instructor scoring sheets and thresholds.
4. Check for severe overrides or transfer-safety failures.
5. Complete the relevant role-specific signoff sheet.
6. If the learner is not passing cleanly, complete the conditional, remediation, deferral, or restriction form as appropriate.
7. If the learner is moving to a larger scope, complete the cross-role final signoff form or clearance acknowledgment form.
8. Update the cohort tracking sheet.
9. Revisit the signoff if later mixed-role or live-observation evidence contradicts it.

## Closing Note

Role-specific drills show whether a learner can carry the method inside a job.

Role-specific signoff shows whether the institution is willing to say, in writing, what that learner may safely do next.

That is the point where training rhetoric turns into operational permission.

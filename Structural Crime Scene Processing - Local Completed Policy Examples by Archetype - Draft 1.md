# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Local Completed Policy Examples by Archetype

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document provides completed local policy examples for different agency archetypes using the policy-template architecture already established elsewhere in the project.

It exists to solve the final drafting problem that often appears after leaders have:

- doctrine
- role guides
- dashboard templates
- audit packets
- rollout policies in blank-template form

At that point, many agencies still need to see what a finished local policy package actually looks like when it is populated for a real deployment environment.

That is the purpose of this document.

---

## What This Document Does

This document:

- turns the local rollout policy templates into completed example policy sets
- shows how policy language changes by agency archetype without changing the fixed core
- gives command, legal, training, and implementation leaders a concrete drafting reference
- bridges the gap between blank policy templates and fully assembled rollout packets

This document does not:

- provide legal advice
- dictate one mandatory local wording for every agency
- replace local labor, legal, or command review
- authorize deletion of the fixed core

---

## Why Completed Policy Examples Are Needed

Blank templates are necessary, but many agencies fail in the move from template to adoption because:

- their legal or policy office rewrites the document into vague aspiration
- they delete drift, audit, or revocation language because it feels uncomfortable
- they weaken role-interface rules to preserve legacy habits
- they treat local variation as a license to exempt themselves from difficult obligations

Completed examples are needed because they show:

- what a structurally honest directive sounds like
- how fixed-core commitments survive local adaptation
- how an agency can name real constraints without using them as excuses
- how policy can protect the method rather than cosmetically praise it

Each archetype includes a completed local package with:

2. an adoption directive

4. role and supervision rules
5. observation and variant rules
6. audit and corrective-action rules
7. dashboard and reporting rules
8. training, certification, and signoff rules
9. specialist alignment rules
10. downstream legal review rules where applicable
11. technology and AI governance rules
12. drift recovery and scale-up rules

These examples should be read as drafting models.

That means:

- copy structure, not exact names
- preserve obligations, not stylistic phrasing
- adapt local titles and units
- do not remove residue, drift, or revocation language simply because it is politically uncomfortable

---

## Fixed-Core Rule for All Examples

Every example in this document preserves the following non-negotiable features:

- observable closure remains distinct from administrative closure
- unresolveds and deferrals remain owned
- transferability remains a requirement
- structural review questions remain mandatory
- drift remains visible
- audit dispositions remain real
- conditional clearance remains distinct from full readiness
- revocation remains available when later evidence shows drift or hidden rescue

If a local draft deletes those features, it is no longer an adaptation example.

It is a regression example.

## Example Package A. Large Municipal Department

### 1. Agency Profile

**Archetype:** Large municipal department  
**Pilot environment:** one patrol district, one full-time scene unit, one detective bureau squad, one in-house legal review lane  
**Primary local risks:** multiple handoffs, premature briefing fluency, hidden oral rescue, subunit dialect drift

### 2. Completed Cover Memorandum

**Subject:** Issuance of District 2 Structural Crime Scene Processing Pilot Policy Package

This memorandum issues the Structural Crime Scene Processing pilot policy package for District 2 patrol, the central crime-scene unit, the violent-crimes squad, designated review supervisors, and assigned early-review prosecutors operating within pilot scope.

The package is issued to improve:

- diagnosability
- transfer integrity
- lawful boundedness
- ownership of unresolveds
- visibility into supervisory drift

This package shall be used as one operating set.

No individual section shall be interpreted to weaken:

- structural closure logic
- residue visibility
- transfer-readiness review
- audit consequences
- downstream boundedness

### 3. Completed Adoption Directive

**Policy statement**

The department adopts Structural Crime Scene Processing as the required operating framework for designated pilot scenes, associated review activity, transfer activity, specialist review, downstream legal review, training, observation, and audit functions within the scope named below.

**Core rule**

Personnel operating under this directive shall not treat evidence volume, confident briefing style, report smoothness, or persuasive legal language as substitutes for structural support.

**Scope**

This directive applies to:

- District 2 patrol field personnel
- District 2 patrol sergeants
- central crime-scene unit personnel assigned to pilot incidents
- violent-crimes squad detectives assigned to pilot incidents
- investigative lieutenants reviewing pilot incidents
- designated charging-review and warrant-review prosecutors assigned to the pilot
- pilot training and audit personnel

**Core obligations**

Personnel within scope shall:

- preserve observable closure criteria
- preserve named deferral ownership
- preserve transferability across units
- preserve structural supervisory review
- document drift and corrective action

**Authority**

The following designations are established:

- executive sponsor: deputy chief of investigations
- implementation owner: captain, reform and quality office
- training lead: academy lieutenant
- audit lead: quality assurance commander
- laboratory integration lead: crime-lab operations manager
- downstream legal lead: assigned major-cases prosecutor supervisor

**Effective date**

Effective date: July 1 of the pilot year.

**Review date**

Formal review date: 90 days after implementation start, with command check at day 45.

### 4. Completed Pilot Operating Order

**Pilot designation**

The pilot environment is District 2 patrol, the central crime-scene unit, violent-crimes squad B, and the designated prosecutorial review lane attached to major-case screening.

**Pilot duration**

Pilot begins: July 1  
Pilot ends or is formally reviewed: September 28

**Required documents**

The following documents are mandatory for pilot use:

- role guides for patrol, scene technicians, detectives, supervisors, lab personnel, and downstream legal teams
- approved municipal role-specific observation variants
- live-scene and live-review observation checklists
- live implementation audit packets
- field forms packet and approved local packet supplements
- training scoring sheets and signoff forms
- downstream legal scoring and signoff forms where applicable

**Parallel-run posture**

The pilot shall be treated as a structured transition environment. Legacy habits may be documented for comparison, but they shall not overrule the new method inside designated scope without express command authorization.

**Live-scene requirements**

Pilot scenes shall include where applicable:

- observable closure criterion
- named deferrals
- structural supervision
- observation or approved sampling
- after-action review for major incidents

**Escalation**

Immediate escalation to the implementation owner or designee is required for:

- repeat premature category closure
- hidden oral rescue in package transfer
- severe downstream overclaim
- unresolved specialist conflict
- red-level drift indicator

**Review cadence**

Pilot review shall occur:

- weekly within pilot operations
- after major incidents
- at day-21, day-45, and day-90 audit points

### 5. Completed Role and Supervision Policy

**Role expectations**

Personnel shall perform in accordance with the relevant role guides and approved local variants. No lane shall assume that a downstream lane will repair weak structure created earlier.

**Supervisory question standard**

Patrol sergeants, investigative lieutenants, and review-board members shall prioritize questions about:

- state
- closure criterion
- anchor logic
- residue
- deferral ownership
- what would break the leading hypothesis

**Prohibited supervisory shortcuts**

Supervisors shall not privilege:

- speed as proof of maturity
- clean briefing cadence as proof of structural quality
- early motive talk absent support
- prosecutor comfort as proof of package readiness

**Role-compression rule**

Where a supervisor temporarily performs review and operational functions on the same incident, that overlap shall be marked. Overlap shall not be used to bypass transfer or review requirements.

**Specialist interaction rule**

Laboratory and legal lanes shall be treated as structural review partners, not as confirmation services for upstream theory.

### 6. Completed Observation and Local Variant Policy

**Observation requirement**

All pilot scenes, designated reviews, legal review events, and selected lab interactions shall be observed under the standard observation companion or an approved municipal variant.

**Approved local variants**

Approved variants are:

- large municipal patrol variant for multi-unit arrival scenes
- full-time scene-unit technician variant
- detective-bureau variant
- patrol-sergeant and investigative-lieutenant supervisory variants
- in-house laboratory variant
- embedded early-review prosecutor variant

**Variant approval**

No additional local variant shall enter standing use until it has been:

- drafted
- piloted
- reviewed by the implementation owner and audit lead
- approved by the deputy chief of investigations

**Forbidden local deletions**

No variant may delete:

- unresolved ownership
- transfer-readiness checks
- interpretive-boundary concerns
- severe warning indicators

**Observation integrity**

When staffing prevents full observation, the limitation shall be marked and carried into the dashboard and audit layers.

### 7. Completed Audit and Corrective-Action Policy

**Audit requirement**

The department shall use the live implementation audit packets for:

- launch readiness
- first-wave live use
- transfer integrity
- training and supervisory integrity
- specialist alignment
- drift review
- scale-up decision review

**Required dispositions**

Every audit shall end in one of the following dispositions:

- Proceed
- Proceed with Conditions
- Hold and Remediate
- Reset the Stage

**Corrective-action requirement**

Each material finding shall produce:

- named fault line
- assigned owner
- due date
- evidence required for closure

**Repeat-fault rule**

Repeat transfer failures, repeat oral rescue, and repeat patrol-boundary leakage shall be escalated to command review and shall not be considered resolved merely because coaching occurred.

**Red-indicator rule**

Any red item tied to hidden oral rescue, severe overclaim, or category distortion shall include a follow-up date and named reviewer within the same review cycle.

### 8. Completed Dashboard and Reporting Policy

**Dashboard requirement**

The department shall maintain:

- command sponsor dashboard every two weeks
- pilot operations dashboard every week
- training and certification dashboard monthly
- specialist dashboard monthly
- drift and recovery dashboard at each audit point and after major incidents
- scale-up dashboard at formal expansion reviews

**Population rule**

Dashboards shall be populated from:

- observation records
- audit findings
- scoring sheets
- signoff forms
- corrective-action ledgers

They shall not be populated primarily from memory, volume counts, or throughput claims.

**Coverage and confidence rule**

Every dashboard cycle shall state:

- reporting window
- coverage confidence
- known evidence limitations

**Metric integrity**

Evidence count, report completion rate, and case speed shall not operate as primary adoption metrics inside this pilot.

**Red-item reporting**

Each red item shall display:

- a cause line
- an owner
- a next review date

**Metric dictionary**

The reform and quality office shall maintain the local metric dictionary aligned to project definitions.

### 9. Completed Training, Certification, and Signoff Policy

**Training requirement**

Personnel shall complete assigned pilot training and evaluation requirements before unsupervised work in pilot scope.

**Certification standard**

Certification decisions shall rely on:

- exercise performance
- scoring sheets
- remediation status
- signoff forms

**Conditional pass rule**

Conditional clearance shall specify:

- allowed work scope
- required supervision conditions
- next review date

Conditional clearance shall not be treated as full readiness.

**Remediation rule**

Material deficiencies in patrol-boundary control, transfer readiness, or downstream boundedness shall result in explicit remediation and re-review.

**Revocation rule**

Previously granted readiness may be suspended or revoked when later evidence shows:

- severe overclaim
- hidden oral rescue
- transfer failure
- unmanaged drift

### 10. Completed Specialist Alignment Policy

**Specialist scope**

This policy applies to:

- central crime laboratory sections engaged in pilot submissions
- scene specialists in pilot scope
- crime-lab managers supervising pilot submission quality
- designated prosecutorial reviewers where package interpretation crosses specialist boundaries

**Structural intake rule**

Pilot submissions shall be read as structural requests with discriminating purpose, not as routine evidence service tickets.

**Distinction-preservation rule**

Specialists shall preserve distinctions material to later interpretation and shall flag when upstream packaging or category decisions have already compromised them.

**Null and boundedness rule**

Null results and partial results shall remain visible and bounded.

**Confirmation-service prohibition**

No specialist lane shall operate as confirmation service for an already preferred narrative.

### 11. Completed Downstream Legal Review Policy

**Review posture**

Designated prosecutors shall read pilot case packages for:

- anchor logic
- bounded sequence
- residue
- eliminations
- threshold match
- transferability

before seeking persuasive framing.

**Overclaim prohibition**

Charging memoranda, affidavits, and demonstratives shall not state more than the package lawfully supports.

**Residue rule**

Material unresolveds shall remain visible where their deletion would distort threshold analysis.

**Signoff requirement**

Pilot charging, warrant, and demonstrative readiness shall use approved downstream legal signoff forms.

**Revocation and reentry**

Downstream legal readiness may be revisited where later review shows demonstrative distortion, affidavit overclaim, or hidden rescue dependence.

### 12. Completed Technology and AI Governance Policy

**Technology-use rule**

Any technology introduced during pilot use shall be assessed for its structural effect on the method, not merely for convenience or speed.

**Allowed AI-use categories**

The following bounded uses are permitted when approved:

- contradiction detection
- residue summarization
- structured comparison support
- prompt generation for review preparation

**Disallowed AI-use categories**

The following are prohibited:

- autonomous accusation
- suspect ranking presented as analysis
- unbounded narrative generation presented as investigative support
- confidence scores lacking named evidence support

**Review requirement**

Each approved tool shall identify:

- the problem addressed
- the failure class introduced
- the workflow changed
- the review owner

### 13. Completed Drift Recovery and Scale-Up Policy

**Drift review requirement**

The department shall conduct recurring drift review through:

- weekly pilot review
- day-45 transfer review
- day-90 drift audit

**Recovery triggers**

The following trigger recovery review:

- repeat fault lines
- worsening oral rescue dependency
- severe overclaim
- repeat premature category closure
- dashboard and audit misalignment

**Recovery action rule**

Recovery review shall classify the root problem as one or more of:

- training
- supervision
- document design
- governance
- role interface
- staffing
- tool misalignment
- metric distortion

**Scale decision rule**

No scale decision shall be made on the basis of enthusiasm, polished briefings, or local political preference alone. Scale decisions shall rely on the designated scale-up audit outputs.

**Receiving-wave readiness**

Any receiving district must have:

- named command sponsor
- named training support
- local review dates
- identified risk conditions

### 14. Why This Policy Package Works

This package works because it does not confuse organizational sophistication with structural maturity.

It:

- preserves hard review consequences
- keeps transfer integrity visible
- prevents supervisory fluency from becoming hidden rescue
- binds downstream legal review into the same discipline as field work

## Example Package B. County Sheriff or Rural Office

### 15. Agency Profile

**Archetype:** County sheriff or rural office  
**Pilot environment:** one patrol shift, generalist investigations, regional on-call scene support, state-lab liaison, county attorney consultation  
**Primary local risks:** role compression, thin observation, distance-delayed specialist arrival, practical closure pressure

### 16. Completed Cover Memorandum

**Subject:** Structural Crime Scene Processing Rural Pilot Policy Package

This memorandum issues the Structural Crime Scene Processing policy package for Patrol Shift B, generalist investigative follow-up, sheriff supervisory review, regional scene-support coordination, and county attorney consultation on designated in-scope incidents.

The package is adopted to improve:

- disturbance honesty
- role-shift visibility
- transferability despite staffing limits
- explicit marking of low-visibility conditions
- accountability for unresolveds

This package shall be read as a whole.

No section shall be interpreted as permission to convert resource limitation into undocumented structural deletion.

### 17. Completed Adoption Directive

**Policy statement**

The sheriff's office adopts Structural Crime Scene Processing as the operating framework for designated pilot incidents requiring structured scene handling, transfer, and review.

**Core rule**

Personnel shall not treat scene quietness, travel burden, practical familiarity, or staffing strain as substitutes for structural support.

**Scope**

This directive applies to:

- Patrol Shift B
- on-call supervisors
- generalist investigators handling pilot incidents
- regional scene-support coordination
- state-lab liaison documentation
- county attorney consultation within pilot scope
- local training and audit functions

**Core obligations**

Within scope, personnel shall:

- mark low-visibility conditions explicitly
- preserve owned unresolveds
- mark role compression
- preserve transfer logic
- document drift and recovery effort

**Authority**

The following roles are designated:

- command sponsor: sheriff
- implementation owner: lieutenant over patrol and investigations
- training lead: senior sergeant
- audit lead: implementation lieutenant
- lab liaison owner: designated investigations sergeant
- downstream legal coordination owner: county attorney liaison

**Effective date**

Effective date: August 1 of the pilot year.

**Review date**

Formal review date: 120 days from implementation start, with interim reviews at day 30 and day 60.

### 18. Completed Pilot Operating Order

**Pilot designation**

The pilot environment includes Patrol Shift B, designated suspicious-death and major-assault follow-up, and all rural scenes where delayed specialist arrival materially affects scene structure.

**Pilot duration**

Pilot begins: August 1  
Pilot formal review: November 29

**Required documents**

Required documents are:

- patrol and detective role guides
- rural patrol and generalist-investigator local variants
- regional technician and state-lab liaison variants
- observation and audit companions
- field forms and signoff forms appropriate to scope

**Parallel-run posture**

The office shall run this pilot as a structured transition process. Legacy local customs may be noted for comparison but shall not override required role-shift marking, unresolved ownership, or drift documentation.

**Live-scene requirements**

Pilot incidents shall include where applicable:

- marked disturbance window
- low-visibility note when visibility is partial
- explicit role-shift note when one person carries multiple functions
- named deferrals
- after-action review on suspicious death and high-friction scenes

**Escalation**

Escalation is required for:

- practical closure without owned unresolveds
- role compression left unmarked
- state-lab transfer without liaison note
- repeated downstream gray conditions caused by missing documentation

**Review cadence**

Pilot review shall occur:

- monthly at minimum
- after major suspicious-death scenes
- during day-30, day-60, and day-120 audits

### 19. Completed Role and Supervision Policy

**Role expectations**

Personnel shall follow applicable role guides and local variants. Where one person performs patrol, scene protection, and early follow-up functions, each shift in function shall be marked.

**Supervisory question standard**

Supervisors shall ask:

- what remained visible and what did not
- what disturbance occurred before later arrival
- where role compression occurred
- what unresolveds are still owned
- whether the current package can transfer without the creator present

**Prohibited supervisory shortcuts**

Supervisors shall not:

- excuse missing documentation because the scene felt straightforward
- treat calm local familiarity as support
- treat later memory as substitute for earlier marking

**Role-compression rule**

Role compression is sometimes unavoidable. It is never invisible.

**Specialist interaction rule**

Regional scene support, state-lab work, and county attorney review shall be treated as structural partners, not as backfill for undocumented early work.

### 20. Completed Observation and Local Variant Policy

**Observation requirement**

Observation shall occur where feasible on pilot incidents, supervisory reviews, and designated handoffs. Partial observation is permitted only when the limitation is marked explicitly.

**Approved local variants**

Approved variants are:

- rural deputy patrol variant
- generalist investigator variant
- regional on-call technician variant
- state-lab liaison variant
- later-stage prosecutor intake variant

**Variant approval**

New local variants shall require draft, pilot use, review by the implementation lieutenant, and approval by the sheriff.

**Forbidden local deletions**

No local variant may delete:

- low-visibility marking
- role-shift marking
- unresolved ownership
- transferability checks

**Observation integrity**

Missing observation shall produce gray or limited-confidence reporting, not green by default.

### 21. Completed Audit and Corrective-Action Policy

**Audit requirement**

The office shall use implementation audit packets for:

- first-wave live use
- handoff and transfer integrity
- drift review
- scale readiness

**Required dispositions**

Permitted audit dispositions are:

- Proceed
- Proceed with Conditions
- Hold and Remediate
- Reset the Stage

**Corrective-action requirement**

Each material fault line shall receive:

- owner
- due date
- closure evidence

**Repeat-fault rule**

Repeated technician-delay masking, repeated unmarked role compression, or repeated practical closure without explicit unresolved ownership shall be escalated to the sheriff and implementation lieutenant.

**Red-indicator rule**

Any red item tied to rural visibility failure, hidden oral rescue, or later overclaim shall receive named follow-up review.

### 22. Completed Dashboard and Reporting Policy

**Dashboard requirement**

The office shall maintain:

- monthly command dashboard
- monthly pilot operations dashboard
- training dashboard at each cohort review
- specialist dashboard when sufficient data exists
- drift dashboard at audit points and after major failures
- scale dashboard only at formal expansion review

**Population rule**

Dashboard population shall come from:

- observation sheets
- supervisor reviews
- liaison notes
- audit outcomes
- scoring and signoff documents

**Coverage and confidence rule**

Every dashboard shall state:

- reporting period
- coverage confidence
- known visibility limits

**Metric integrity**

Evidence count and call-handling speed shall not function as adoption indicators.

**Red-item reporting**

Every red item shall show:

- cause
- owner
- next review date

**Metric dictionary**

The implementation lieutenant shall maintain the local dictionary in alignment with project definitions.

### 23. Completed Training, Certification, and Signoff Policy

**Training requirement**

Personnel operating in pilot scope shall complete the designated training sequence before unsupervised use in suspicious-death and major-scene work.

**Certification standard**

Certification decisions shall rely on:

- exercise performance
- scoring sheets
- remediation completion
- signoff where applicable

**Conditional pass rule**

Conditional clearance shall identify:

- restricted scene class
- required supervisory presence
- required re-review date

**Remediation rule**

Material deficiencies in role-shift marking, transfer readiness, or low-visibility honesty shall trigger remediation and re-review.

**Revocation rule**

Readiness may be suspended where later review shows:

- repeated undocumented drift
- hidden oral rescue
- unsafe downstream overstatement

### 24. Completed Specialist Alignment Policy

**Specialist scope**

This policy applies to:

- regional on-call technician support
- state-lab submission liaison work
- county attorney consultation where structural support is reviewed

**Structural intake rule**

Submissions and later reviews shall be treated as structural requests tied to scene conditions, not as generic requests for confirmation.

**Distinction-preservation rule**

Where long travel time or delay has already altered the scene, that limitation shall be carried forward explicitly.

**Null and boundedness rule**

Null results and low-confidence outputs shall remain visible.

**Confirmation-service prohibition**

No external specialist function shall be asked to ratify a preferred narrative unsupported by documented structure.

### 25. Completed Downstream Legal Review Policy

**Review posture**

County attorney consultation on pilot incidents shall read the package for threshold match, residue, and boundedness before pursuing persuasive theory.

**Overclaim prohibition**

Charging or warrant language shall not exceed the documented package.

**Residue rule**

Where unresolveds remain material, they shall be preserved in later review.

**Signoff requirement**

No full downstream legal readiness designation shall be issued until the office has established sufficient sample depth and approved signoff practice.

**Revocation and reentry**

Any advisory approval may be revisited where later evidence shows overclaim or hidden rescue dependence.

### 26. Completed Technology and AI Governance Policy

**Technology-use rule**

Technology shall be adopted only where it improves structural visibility under constrained staffing and geography.

**Allowed AI-use categories**

Permitted bounded uses are:

- contradiction spotting in draft forms
- residue summarization for review preparation
- structured comparison support

**Disallowed AI-use categories**

Prohibited uses are:

- automated accusation
- opaque ranking
- narrative generation presented as analysis
- unsupported confidence claims

**Review requirement**

Each tool shall identify:

- the local problem addressed
- the failure class introduced
- the approving owner

### 27. Completed Drift Recovery and Scale-Up Policy

**Drift review requirement**

The office shall review drift at monthly pilot review and at each formal audit point.

**Recovery triggers**

Triggers include:

- repeat fault lines
- worsening oral rescue dependency
- repeated undocumented role compression
- practical closure patterns
- audit and dashboard mismatch

**Recovery action rule**

Recovery review shall classify the problem as one or more of:

- training
- supervision
- document design
- role interface
- staffing or capacity
- tool misalignment

**Scale decision rule**

Scale decisions shall be based on audit outputs, not on a desire to show rapid reform progress.

**Receiving-wave readiness**

Any next shift or unit must have:

- named supervisor
- local training plan
- review dates
- known risk conditions

### 28. Why This Policy Package Works

This package works because it preserves honesty under scarcity.

It does not pretend a rural office has urban staffing depth.

Instead, it:

- marks what cannot be fully seen
- preserves role-shift visibility
- keeps gray conditions legitimate
- stops staffing pressure from becoming silent policy deletion

## Example Package C. Campus or Institutional Safety Agency

### 29. Agency Profile

**Archetype:** Campus or institutional safety agency  
**Pilot environment:** campus patrol, housing and facilities interface, regional technical support, university counsel and prosecutor liaison  
**Primary local risks:** rumor pressure, administrative smoothing, overreading access records, familiarity distortion

### 30. Completed Cover Memorandum

**Subject:** Campus Structural Crime Scene Processing Pilot Policy Package

This memorandum issues the Structural Crime Scene Processing policy package for in-scope serious incidents handled by campus patrol, campus command, housing and facilities liaison channels, designated review personnel, and assigned legal review partners.

The package is adopted to improve:

- interpretive-boundary control
- access-system boundedness
- downstream legal honesty
- visibility into rumor pressure
- command-level accountability for normalization drift

This package shall be implemented as one set. No campus practice, liaison custom, or administrative preference shall weaken the fixed core without explicit pilot review and written approval.

### 31. Completed Adoption Directive

**Policy statement**

The institution adopts Structural Crime Scene Processing as the operating framework for designated serious incidents inside pilot scope.

**Core rule**

Personnel shall not treat community familiarity, access records, administrative calm, or internal messaging confidence as substitutes for structural support.

**Scope**

This directive applies to:

- campus patrol personnel responding to in-scope incidents
- on-call command staff
- housing and facilities liaison channels interacting with pilot incidents
- designated investigators
- regional scene-support requests
- designated university counsel and prosecutorial review interfaces
- training and audit functions within pilot scope

**Core obligations**

Personnel within scope shall:

- preserve interpretive boundaries
- keep access information bounded
- preserve owned unresolveds
- prevent administrative smoothing from deleting residue
- document drift and recovery action

**Authority**

The following designations are established:

- executive sponsor: vice president for public safety
- implementation owner: director of campus investigations
- training lead: captain of professional standards
- audit lead: policy and compliance manager
- facilities liaison owner: director of campus operations support
- legal coordination owner: designated university counsel liaison

**Effective date**

Effective date: September 1 of the pilot year.

**Review date**

Formal review date: 75 days from implementation start, with midpoint command review at day 30.

### 32. Completed Pilot Operating Order

**Pilot designation**

Pilot scope includes suspicious student or staff death, serious in-building assault, access-control-intensive incidents, and serious scenes with heavy rumor pressure.

**Pilot duration**

Pilot begins: September 1  
Formal review: November 15

**Required documents**

Required documents are:

- campus patrol and supervisory role guides
- campus or institutional observation variants
- field forms packet
- downstream legal scenario and signoff tools where applicable
- implementation audit packets
- dashboard templates and approved local reporting forms

**Parallel-run posture**

Legacy campus messaging habits may be tracked for comparison but shall not displace the structural package inside designated pilot incidents.

**Live-scene requirements**

Pilot incidents shall include where applicable:

**Review cadence**

Pilot review shall occur:

- weekly for high-friction incidents

### 33. Completed Role and Supervision Policy

**Role expectations**

Personnel shall perform in accordance with role guides and campus-specific variants. Housing, facilities, and administrative staff are not investigative meaning authorities unless formally designated and bounded by role.

Supervisors shall ask:

- what is known from the scene versus the community
- what access data shows and does not show
- what residue remains live
- what command or liaison communication could distort interpretation
- what would falsify the leading sequence

**Prohibited supervisory shortcuts**

Supervisors shall not:

- ask for a normalized account before residue review
- treat access-control data as automatic proof of action sequence
- allow housing or facilities familiarity to stand in for anchor support
- reward low-friction messaging over structural honesty

**Role-compression rule**

Where campus personnel hold overlapping operational and administrative functions, those overlaps shall be marked and bounded.

**Specialist interaction rule**

Technical and legal review lanes shall remain independent of administrative preference for calm resolution.

### 34. Completed Observation and Local Variant Policy

**Observation requirement**

Pilot incidents and command reviews shall be observed under the campus observation variant or the standard companion where no approved variant exists.

**Approved local variants**

Approved variants are:

- campus patrol variant
- supervisory variant with administrative-pressure note
- later-stage legal-review variant
- regional technician support variant

**Variant approval**

No additional campus variant shall be adopted until reviewed by the implementation owner, audit lead, and executive sponsor.

**Forbidden local deletions**

No local variant may delete:

- interpretive-boundary concerns
- rumor-pressure visibility
- unresolved ownership
- transferability checks

## Common Policy Failure Modes

### Failure mode 1: Turning obligation into aspiration

If the completed example is rewritten into aspirational language with no owner, no review date, and no consequence, the policy has already been weakened.

### Failure mode 2: Localizing away the hard parts

If a local draft keeps the title but deletes residue, drift, or revocation logic, it is not adapting the model honestly.

### Failure mode 3: Separating legal review from the policy package

When downstream legal review remains outside the package, later overclaim almost always returns through a different door.

### Failure mode 4: Treating scarcity as a silent exemption

Resource strain may justify marked limitation. It does not justify invisible deletion.

### Failure mode 5: Publishing policy before defining review consequence

If leadership cannot say what happens after a red audit or repeated fault line, the package is incomplete.

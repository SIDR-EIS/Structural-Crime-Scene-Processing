# Structural Crime Scene Processing

## Exercise Answer Keys and Scoring Rubrics

## Draft 1

---

## Purpose

This document adds the evaluation layer for the Structural Crime Scene Processing exercise set.

It is written to help instructors do four things consistently:

1. distinguish strong structural reasoning from polished legacy reasoning
2. score learners without rewarding premature certainty
3. identify where a learner is still being pulled back into the old model
4. make debriefs concrete rather than impressionistic

These are not "perfect answer" sheets.

They are bounded instructor keys.

The method should not train learners to memorize one correct story. It should train them to produce better structure under uncertainty.

---

## How to Use This Companion

Use this document with:

- the exercise scenarios
- the field forms packet
- the exemplar completed forms
- the instructor transition guide

The best practice is:

1. let learners work the scenario first
2. score their output against the rubric
3. compare their output to the bounded answer-key logic
4. debrief where narrative gravity overtook method

Do not hand this document to learners before first pass.

It is meant to sharpen instruction, not to flatten the exercises into test-prep.

---

## Master Scoring Model

Score each learner or team across eight dimensions.

Use a `0-3` scale for each dimension.

### Score meanings

`0` = absent, off-model, or actively legacy-driven  
`1` = partial recognition, but weak structural control  
`2` = solid structural reasoning with some gaps  
`3` = strong, disciplined, transferable output

### The eight dimensions

1. Closure discipline
2. State and transition discipline
3. Anchor discipline
4. Carrier / explanation-density detection
5. Propagation and resistance-path reasoning
6. Collection / action prioritization
7. Lawful inference and residue honesty
8. Transferability of output

### Global interpretation

`0-7`  
The learner is still operating mostly inside the legacy model.

`8-13`  
The learner recognizes parts of the new model but still collapses under narrative pressure.

`14-19`  
The learner is functionally using the new model, though not yet consistently.

`20-24`  
The learner is producing strong structural work that can survive transfer and review.

---

## Dimension-Level Guidance

### 1. Closure discipline

`0`

The learner never states closure or treats scene completion as self-evident.

`1`

The learner gestures at closure but phrases it as vague completion or administrative release.

`2`

The learner states a usable closure criterion but leaves some residue or ownership ambiguous.

`3`

The learner states an observable closure criterion and clearly separates resolved from unresolved.

### 2. State and transition discipline

`0`

No state model appears. The learner treats activity or calm as proof.

`1`

The learner names a state or next step, but not with enough precision to detect stall.

`2`

The learner identifies the current state and next transition with reasonable clarity.

`3`

The learner clearly identifies state, next expected transition, feasible window, and potential stall.

### 3. Anchor discipline

`0`

The learner relies mostly on witness drama, appearance, or assumption.

`1`

The learner notices some anchors but does not rank them or constrain interpretation with them.

`2`

The learner identifies and roughly ranks the main anchors.

`3`

The learner builds a credible hierarchy and explicitly states what each anchor does and does not constrain.

### 4. Carrier / explanation-density detection

`0`

The learner ignores interpretive contamination and human load-bearing.

`1`

The learner notices that people are shaping the scene but cannot name the structural problem clearly.

`2`

The learner identifies likely carriers and some explanation-density hotspots.

`3`

The learner names the major carriers and explains how they distort or substitute for structure.

### 5. Propagation and resistance-path reasoning

`0`

The learner treats the visible focal point as the causal center by default.

`1`

The learner suspects propagation complexity but stays vague.

`2`

The learner distinguishes initiation site from at least one plausible propagation path.

`3`

The learner clearly separates initiation from propagation and reasons about resistance geometry explicitly.

### 6. Collection / action prioritization

`0`

The learner collects broadly by habit or prestige.

`1`

The learner ranks actions, but mostly by familiarity rather than discriminating value.

`2`

The learner produces a mostly sound priority sequence.

`3`

The learner prioritizes perishable anchors and discriminators cleanly, with attention to contamination and packaging distinction.

### 7. Lawful inference and residue honesty

`0`

The learner overclaims, collapses uncertainty, or treats ambiguity as failure.

`1`

The learner leaves some uncertainty visible but still sneaks in unearned narrative confidence.

`2`

The learner is mostly disciplined and names some residue honestly.

`3`

The learner stays within lawful inference, names residue clearly, and avoids fake closure.

### 8. Transferability of output

`0`

The output would require live narration to be useful.

`1`

The output contains useful points but depends heavily on implied context.

`2`

The output is mostly reviewable by another trained reader.

`3`

The output is clear, explicit, and can survive handoff with minimal rescue.

## Exercise 1. Apartment Hallway Collapse

### What a strong first legacy reading usually sounds like

"Domestic fight in 4C spills into the hallway, victim tries to escape, offender runs down the stairs."

This is a highly plausible first narrative.

It is also structurally premature.

### What a strong structural answer should recognize

- the visible focal event sits on a threshold, which is analytically unstable
- the building has asymmetric access control
- witness speech is already cross-contaminated
- patrol interpretation is already acting as a carrier
- the stairwell claim is not yet stronger than lobby camera or fob data
- the sink / damp towel may be major posture signal or irrelevant domestic noise

### Key expectations by learner task

#### 1. Closure criterion for the initial scene phase

Strong answer:

The scene is not "closed" when the room is taped. The initial phase closes when threshold distinctions are preserved, early anchors are captured, boundary control is restored across all four boundary types, and the first bounded hypothesis set can be handed forward without oral rescue.

Weak answer:

"When all evidence is collected" or "when the scene is secure."

#### 2. Present state and next expected transition

Strong answer:

Current state is bounded or partially bounded, with anchoring still incomplete. The next transition is anchored and mapped. Strong learners may also mention a stall risk due to ongoing witness contamination and camera / access-data perishability.

Weak answer:

"Investigation in progress" or "processing."

#### 3. First anchor list

Expected high-value anchors:

- 6:42 p.m. emergency call and dispatch timeline
- 6:49 p.m. arrival and responder movement window
- lobby camera footage
- front-door key-fob access events
- responder path through threshold
- weather and wet transfer conditions

If learners rank neighbor assertions above these, anchor discipline is weak.

#### 4. Likely initiation site and plausible propagation paths

Strong answer:

The initiation site is not yet earned. Candidate initiation zones include 4C interior, threshold, or hallway adjacency. Plausible propagation paths should include at least:

- interior to threshold to hallway collapse
- building interface movement through front entrance and vertical path
- hallway / stairwell movement as potential propagation route rather than proven offender route

Weak answer:

Flat assumption that 4C is proven origin because shouting was reportedly heard there.

#### 5. Highest explanation-density carrier

Best expected answer:

Patrol / witness narrative framing is currently the highest carrier, especially the domestic and robbery labels, plus the 4A argument story if treated as causal center too early.

#### 6. First five collection or capture moves

Strong priority sequence:

1. secure lobby camera and fob data
2. preserve and map threshold geometry before more traffic
3. document EMS / patrol path contamination
4. capture kitchen sink / damp towel conditions
5. document phone and property state

Accept alternate ordering if the learner defends it by perishability and discriminating value.

#### 7. Legacy-model errors to suppress

Expected strong answers:

- domestic classification before anchor support
- robbery classification from non-visible wallet alone
- doorway as automatic causal center
- witness certainty outranking building-interface anchors
- broad apartment sweep before threshold / access distinctions are preserved

### Scoring notes specific to Exercise 1

Reward learners who keep multiple live hypotheses without becoming vague.

Penalize learners who:

- choose one story too early
- ignore threshold geometry
- ignore access asymmetry
- ignore patrol contamination
- rank witness statements above stronger anchors

### Fast score cues

`20-24`

Learner clearly resists the domestic / robbery trap and builds a bounded, anchor-first packet.

`14-19`

Learner sees the threshold and access issues but still drifts toward one favored story.

`8-13`

Learner notices some caution points but still lets witness or patrol framing lead.

`0-7`

Learner mostly reproduces a polished legacy narrative.

## Exercise 2. Office Quiet Death

### What a strong first legacy reading usually sounds like

"Victim came in alone early, entered conference room, and died there without external intrusion. Locked-room appearance plus credential and camera support make the scene mostly self-contained."

Again, plausible.

Again, structurally premature.

### What a strong structural answer should recognize

- quiet appearance is not closure
- credential use is not sole-agency proof
- front-door camera coverage is not full path coverage
- elevator and HVAC discontinuities create an alternative anchor field
- witness statements about routine use of the room are mutually inconsistent
- the missing phone may matter more than the room's tidiness

### Key expectations by learner task

#### 1. First legacy narrative

Strong learners should be able to name the trap cleanly without endorsing it. The most common legacy narrative is some version of:

- self-contained room event
- self-harm or medical-collapse shorthand
- no evidence of second-person involvement because camera and credential look clean

#### 2. Why that narrative is structurally premature

Strong answer:

Because the narrative overweights room appearance and partial access anchors while underweighting unresolved path gaps, pre-entry discontinuities, and the difference between front-door entry and full floor / conference-room sequence.

#### 3. Rank the anchors by authority

Expected high ranking:

1. alarm disarm by credential
2. internal camera entry
3. elevator stop on floor
4. HVAC interruption
5. overnight badge non-use
6. garage camera resemblance
7. employee habit statements

Reasonable alternate order between elevator and HVAC is acceptable if defended.

#### 4. Points where silence could be misread

Expected answers include:

- clean conference room appearance
- lack of visible struggle
- no one else seen at front door
- no hallway or conference-room footage being treated as if it were proof rather than absence

#### 5. Minimal discriminating test set for first eight hours

Strong answers usually include:

- preserve and timeline-lock laptop state
- pursue phone / device ecosystem review
- reconstruct building path gaps around 05:48-06:56
- align pathology / trace review to anchor windows

Weak answers usually jump to broad lab processing without an explicit discriminating reason.

#### 6. Likely top inhibitor classes

Expected strong answers:

- missing observability / path gap
- false closure induced by quiet scene appearance
- hidden coupling between access events and room interpretation
- premature classification risk

### Scoring notes specific to Exercise 2

Reward learners who distrust the room's calm without becoming conspiratorial.

Penalize learners who:

- treat locked-room appearance as a verdict
- overread credential use
- convert missing signal into proof of hidden action without anchors
- import motive from witness anxiety claims

### Fast score cues

`20-24`

Learner treats the room as analytically open despite visual calm and builds a clean anchor hierarchy.

`14-19`

Learner sees the main trap but still leans too hard on the credential / camera story.

`8-13`

Learner notices some anomalies but still describes the case as mostly self-contained.

`0-7`

Learner equates tidiness with closure.

## Exercise 3. Warehouse Loading Bay Reconciliation Failure

### What a strong first legacy reading usually sounds like

"A fatal forklift accident occurred in a congested low-visibility area. The safety issue is local line-of-sight control."

This is the narrow accident frame the scenario is designed to trigger.

### What a strong structural answer should recognize

- stable outbound flow may be false stability
- the experienced leads handling exceptions are likely system carriers
- the forklift event may be the visible collision point, not the true propagation center
- scanner and reconciliation distortions may have moved people and machines into a low-resistance failure path
- "operations unaffected" is not an observation; it is an explanation claim

### Key expectations by learner task

#### 1. Highest explanation-density carriers

Expected answers:

- experienced leads manually reconciling exceptions
- supervisor framing of software issues as minor and unrelated
- recurring same-day corrections functioning as invisible normalizers

#### 2. Initiation site versus propagation permissiveness

Strong answer:

The visible initiation site may be the loading-bay collision zone, but propagation permissiveness lies in the upstream routing, mismatch handling, scanner-sync failure, and human reconciliation structure that placed workers and forklift movement into that lane.

#### 3. False stability indicators present

Expected answers:

- throughput / outbound trailer stayed on schedule
- recurring exception subsystem
- normalized tolerance around mismatch corrections
- hidden human reconciliation
- low-severity classification of sync delay because operations appeared unaffected

#### 4. First five evidence and data captures

Strong priority sequence:

1. camera footage from aisle, bay edge, and surrounding intervals
2. system logs for scanner mismatches and routing corrections
3. handheld scanner state and victim assignment data
4. forklift route / movement / operator timeline
5. supervisor and lead-worker statements about exception workflow

Good answers may swap order slightly if they argue from perishability and discriminating value.

#### 5. "Operations unaffected" as observation or hypothesis

Correct answer:

It is a hypothesis or explanation claim, not a neutral observation.

The phrase should be forced into the excuse ledger and tested against recurrence, manual reconciliation load, and system routing behavior.

#### 6. Short excuse ledger

Strong answers should convert claims such as:

- "minor reconciliation issues"
- "operations unaffected"
- "nothing to do with inventory systems"
- "just a visibility accident"

into explicit claims with confirmers and falsifiers.

### Scoring notes specific to Exercise 3

Reward learners who see that the scene is both a local fatality scene and a system-diagnostic scene.

Penalize learners who:

- isolate the event entirely at the forklift
- praise throughput as stabilizing evidence
- miss human reconciliation as infrastructure
- accept managerial language as descriptive fact

### Fast score cues

`20-24`

Learner cleanly identifies false stability and traces the event through the warehouse system rather than stopping at local safety framing.

`14-19`

Learner sees the broader system contribution but still lets the accident frame dominate.

`8-13`

Learner notices manual workarounds but does not integrate them into causal logic.

`0-7`

Learner treats the case as a straightforward local accident.

## Instructor Debrief Pattern

For all exercises, run debrief in this order:

1. ask for the first story learners believed
2. ask what anchors that story outran
3. ask where explanation density entered
4. ask which propagation paths were initially ignored
5. ask what remained unresolved even in strong answers

This order prevents debrief from turning into hindsight theater.

---

## Common Grading Mistakes

### Mistaking confidence for competence

Learners who sound certain early are often still in the old model.

### Rewarding complete-looking stories

A learner who produces a polished narrative without residue should usually score lower than a learner who produces a bounded, disciplined, partially unresolved package.

### Penalizing honest uncertainty

The new model requires explicit residue. Do not punish learners merely because they refuse false closure.

### Overrewarding vocabulary adoption

A learner can say "anchor," "residue," and "propagation" while still reasoning the old way.

Score the structure, not the jargon.

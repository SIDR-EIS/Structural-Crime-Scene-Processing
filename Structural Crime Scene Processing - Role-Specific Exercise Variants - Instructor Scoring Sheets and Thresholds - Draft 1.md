## Academy Evaluation Principles for Role-Specific Work

### Principle 1: Score role execution, not generic case intelligence

A learner may understand the overall case and still mishandle the role.

That should not pass.

### Principle 2: Do not punish lawful uncertainty inside the role

A patrol officer, technician, detective, analyst, supervisor, or prosecutor should not lose points merely because they refuse to counterfeit certainty their role does not yet own.

### Principle 3: Penalize hidden residue and unsafe handoff heavily

The most dangerous role-specific failure is not confusion.

It is hidden instability passed downstream as if it were settled work.

### Principle 4: Distinguish doctrine weakness from role-pressure collapse

Some learners understand the model in discussion but abandon it when speed, noise, hierarchy, status, or public pressure enters the room.

That requires a different remediation path than simple conceptual confusion.

### Principle 5: Require transferability at the role boundary

If the output cannot survive handoff without oral rescue, the learner is not ready for independent use in that role.

### Automatic flags

Check any that apply:

- Output grammar did not match the role's actual task
- Structural weakness was answered with more story rather than more discipline

### Severity note

If any automatic flag is present at a severe level, the learner should not receive an unqualified pass even with a numerically acceptable total.

## Role-Specific Automatic Flag Addendum

### Patrol red-flag overrides

Any of the following should override a simple pass:

- patrol classifies the scene early without protecting arrival-state structure
- responder-caused disturbance is not logged
- witness contamination is allowed to harden into scene understanding
- handoff hides access uncertainty or inherited scene traffic

### Scene technician red-flag overrides

Any of the following should override a simple pass:

- collection begins before usable anchor ordering exists
- generic lab request language substitutes for a real distinction request
- graph logic is replaced by item volume or photo volume
- inherited contamination is acknowledged but treated as analytically irrelevant

### Detective red-flag overrides

Any of the following should override a simple pass:

- multiple witnesses are fused into one implied voice
- favored hypothesis lacks explicit demotion criteria
- residue is buried inside summary prose
- package transfer depends on charisma or explanation instead of structure

### Laboratory red-flag overrides

Any of the following should override a simple pass:

- vague intake is accepted without rewrite or pushback
- null result is allowed to do explanatory work it cannot support
- method limit and evidentiary absence are blurred together
- the lab contribution leaves downstream non-claim boundaries invisible

### Supervisor red-flag overrides

Any of the following should override a simple pass:

- closure is approved because work looks busy
- false stability is rewarded as clean performance
- redesign language collapses into blame or reminder language
- deferrals are approved without real ownership or escalation logic

### Prosecutor red-flag overrides

Any of the following should override a simple pass:

- legal language outruns the anchor hierarchy
- residue is treated as a drafting inconvenience rather than a claim boundary
- demonstrative or memo is cleaner than the package allows
- reentry need is hidden because it weakens immediacy or persuasion

## Fast Threshold Table

### Score bands

`20-24`

Pass, assuming no severe automatic flag and no severe weakness in the role's highest-risk dimensions.

`16-19`

Pass with targeted follow-up if no severe automatic flag is present.

`12-15`

Remediate and re-run.

`0-11`

Not ready. Return to foundational role retraining before re-assessment.

### Severe automatic flags that override score

Any of the following should override a simple pass:

- learner produces an output that the next role cannot safely use without oral rescue
- learner counterfeits closure, certainty, or bounded sequence the role does not lawfully own
- learner allows role pressure to erase visible residue or ownership
- learner substitutes generic case intelligence for role-specific execution
- learner responds to structural weakness by making the story cleaner

These are not minor polish issues.

They indicate the learner is still structurally unsafe in role.

## Pass / Remediate Decision Matrix

### Pass

Use when:

- total score is 20 or higher
- no severe override flag is present
- output is transferable
- role-boundary discipline is intact
- the role's highest-risk dimensions are not materially weak

### Pass with targeted follow-up

Use when:

- total score is 16-19
- the learner is structurally sound overall
- weaknesses are bounded to one or two dimensions
- no severe override flag is present
- the role can perform safely with near-term follow-up

Follow-up should be assigned within the next training cycle.

### Remediate and re-run

Use when:

- total score is 12-15
- or one severe automatic flag appears
- or the learner understands the doctrine but collapses under role pressure

Re-run should occur after targeted remediation rather than simple repetition.

### Not yet ready for independent role use

Use when:

- total score is 11 or below
- or multiple severe automatic flags appear
- or the learner repeatedly converts role-specific uncertainty into polished legacy output

These learners need a reset, not a confidence boost.

## Remediation Routing Guide

### Weak in role-boundary discipline

Route to:

- re-read the relevant role guide sections on role reframing
- repeat the simplest role-specific drill without pressure modifiers
- force the learner to state what the role is not allowed to claim or solve

Do not remediate this by giving more case detail.

The problem is boundary collapse, not lack of information.

### Weak in structural visibility and state control

Route to:

- first-contact or first-read perception drills
- state-and-transition extraction work
- disturbance logging or scene-state mapping drills

The learner needs help making the system visible before acting on it.

### Weak in anchor and uncertainty discipline

Route to:

- anchor ranking drills
- witness-versus-anchor separation drills
- null-boundary or bounded-sequence rewrites

The learner needs help distinguishing what is strongest from what is merely persuasive.

### Weak in action / narrowing-value discipline

Route to:

- ranked collection drills
- smallest-next-step drills
- request rewrite drills
- test-sequencing drills

The learner needs help choosing moves by uncertainty reduction rather than by habit.

### Weak in residue, deferral, and ownership honesty

Route to:

- closure-contract review drills
- excuse-ledger exercises
- deferral ownership rewrites
- role handoff review drills

The learner needs help making incompleteness visible without laundering it.

### Weak in transferability and next-role safety

Route to:

- blind handoff drills
- package transfer drills
- next-role read-back drills
- hostile-reader review exercises

The learner needs help producing outputs that survive separation from the original author.

### Weak in pressure resistance

Route to:

- repeat the same variant with graduated pressure
- add noise, time compression, hierarchy pressure, or public-pressure modifiers one at a time
- compare calm-run and pressure-run outputs side by side

The learner needs pressure inoculation, not a different doctrine lecture.

### Weak in role-appropriate output grammar

Route to:

- role-specific form completion drills
- rewrite exercises into packet, memo, or review language
- compare generic prose to role-appropriate structural output

The learner needs to write like the role actually functions, not like a smart observer.

## Role-Specific Rapid Routing Guide

### Patrol

Common failure patterns:

- early classification
- invisible responder disturbance
- weak witness separation
- soft handoff language

Best remediation sequence:

1. dispatch-to-arrival discipline drill
2. transient-state preservation drill
3. witness separation and narrative containment drill
4. blind handoff re-run

### Scene technicians

Common failure patterns:

- collection-first reflex
- weak anchor ordering
- graph smoothing
- vague lab requests

Best remediation sequence:

1. first-ten-minute anchor capture
2. scene graph honesty drill
3. ranked collection drill
4. packaging-distinction and handoff re-run

### Detectives

Common failure patterns:

- witness fusion
- theory attachment
- lab as confirmation ritual
- transfer overconfidence

Best remediation sequence:

1. witness disaggregation drill
2. ranked-hypothesis maintenance drill
3. residue-writing drill
4. blind package transfer re-run

### Laboratory personnel

Common failure patterns:

- passive intake
- routine sequencing
- overclean null reporting
- weak downstream warning language

Best remediation sequence:

1. structured-question intake drill
2. test-order redesign drill
3. null-result interpretation drill
4. submission rewrite re-run

### Supervisors and review boards

Common failure patterns:

- activity mistaken for progress
- vague closure approval
- false stability misread as competence
- redesign collapse into blame

Best remediation sequence:

1. structural-question drill
2. closure-contract review drill
3. false-stability detection drill
4. after-action redesign re-run

### Prosecutors and downstream legal teams

Common failure patterns:

- overclaim under time pressure
- clean legal language outrunning anchors
- residue suppression
- presentation inflation

Best remediation sequence:

1. integrated-package reading drill
2. bounded-sequence rewrite drill
3. warrant-language discipline drill
4. demonstrative-audit re-run

## Role-Specific Academy Pass / Remediate Thresholds

### Ready for independent role use

Use when:

- learner has at least two successful scored runs in role
- no severe override flag appears in the most recent run
- transferability is consistently acceptable or strong
- pressure drift is acceptable rather than dangerous

### Ready with supervision

Use when:

- learner is structurally usable
- follow-up is still needed in one or two higher-risk dimensions
- output can be fielded if reviewed by a stronger peer or supervisor

### Needs targeted remediation

Use when:

- one or two failure dimensions recur
- the role is not yet stable under pressure
- the learner improves between runs but remains unsafe in specific respects

### Needs foundational retraining

Use when:

- multiple dimensions fail repeatedly
- role boundary and transferability remain weak
- the learner continues to produce polished legacy outputs after feedback

### Promotion rule

No learner should move to independent role use merely because of one strong classroom run.

Promotion should require:

- repeated acceptable scores
- at least one pressure run
- no unresolved severe override flag
- evidence that the role can hand work safely to the next role in the chain

## Recommended Instructor Workflow

1. assign the learner the base role-specific variant
2. score the output with the master sheet
3. mark any automatic flags before interpreting the total
4. record the main failed dimension and the likely pressure trigger
5. assign the next drill from the rapid routing guide
6. repeat with either the same variant under lower noise or the same variant under structured pressure
7. only certify when transferability and pressure discipline are both acceptable

This workflow is slower than impression-based grading.

It is also more honest.

## Common Instructor Failure Modes

### Passing generally smart learners too early

Learners who understand the whole case often sound stronger than they are in role.

Do not confuse broad intelligence with role discipline.

### Using the total score without role emphasis

A role can appear acceptable numerically while remaining weak in the dimensions that matter most for that role.

### Treating calm-run strength as certification

If pressure has never been added, readiness is unproven.

### Remediating by repetition alone

Running the same drill again without routing the failure dimension is not remediation.

### Accepting oral rescue as normal

If the instructor had to ask the learner what they “really meant,” the transfer test likely failed.
